## Decision question

Does the proposed 48-Fault track earn its production cost and player attention in a roughly 20–30 hour authored adventure, and what exact rules keep Faults distinct from generic collectibles, Micro-vaults, and Repair Contracts?

## Fresh research conclusion

**48 is too high for Stillring's stated quality bar. The shipping target is reduced to 36 authored Resonance Faults.**

The issue is not mathematical impossibility. Forty-eight could physically fit in the runtime. The issue is repetition pressure: Stillring already has Contracts, Vaults, Records, Pulse Shards, Trials, Drift Knots, elites, refuges, upgrades, setup/payoff chains, community projects, and a prestige quest. A 48-entry exploration track risks becoming the dominant texture of optional play even if every individual Fault is competent.

Comparative research reinforces two useful lessons:
- Nintendo's current Zelda Notes makes per-element completion legible and can directly guide players toward hard-to-find Koroks/shrines. The useful lesson is that a large repeated track eventually needs strong audit assistance; Stillring should solve that inside the game without requiring a companion app.
- GameDeveloper's analysis of Ghost of Tsushima identifies a specific failure mode: activities that are pleasant when discovered organically become repetitive when large counts are converted into visible cleanup markers. The critique specifically calls out repetition around the high forties. Stillring should not knowingly recreate that pressure.

Stillring therefore favors fewer, more authored Faults with stronger verb/state variety.

## Locked philosophy

> **A Fault is a local world problem worth diagnosing, not a token worth sweeping.**

A Resonance Fault exists because a place/system is behaving incorrectly in an authored, legible way. The player observes evidence, forms a diagnosis, and reconciles the defect using learned mechanics/state knowledge.

Rationale:

- Later regions use fewer, denser Faults because the player's optional ledger is already broad and each late Fault can demand richer cross-system understanding.
- Still-Cairn / Null Meridian gets three because final spaces need completion-bearing content, but the finale must not become a cleanup warehouse.

## Pacing budget

For a completionist playthrough around the upper end of the 20–30 hour target, 36 Faults average roughly one meaningful Fault per 40–50 minutes of total play, but they are intentionally clustered around exploration/revisit waves rather than metronomically spaced.

Production target per Fault:
- **quick read:** ~30–90 seconds when the challenge is recognition/diagnosis and the required action is already understood;
- **standard:** ~2–5 minutes for a compact authored mechanic/state interaction;
- **deep:** ~5–8 minutes only when the Fault justifies a multi-step or cross-state problem.

A region may contain only a small number of deep Faults. If most Faults become 5–8 minute mini-dungeons, the category has drifted into Micro-vault territory.

## Fault versus other completion categories

### Resonance Fault
- lives in ordinary world space;
- begins with an observable synchronization defect;
- core verb is **diagnose → reconcile**;
- completion changes/settles a local semantic world fact;
- may reveal access/reward, but the defect itself is the authored problem.

### Micro-vault
- compact authored pocket/maintenance space;
- core value is a contained 1–5 minute spatial/mechanical idea;
- entering the space and solving its contained challenge is the activity grammar.

### Repair Contract
- person/community problem with social context, authored resolution, and usually dialogue/relationship consequences;
- mechanics serve the human/community problem.

### Pulse Shard
- unique acquisition object/reward track;
- not a diagnosis activity.

A content pitch that cannot clearly state which of these grammars it belongs to fails taxonomy review.

## Required variation matrix

No region may ship a row of Faults that differ only by geometry/location.

Across the 36, production planning must cover materially different problem families, including multiple examples of:
- structural/load resonance;
- pressure/fluid-state interaction;
- thermal/material-state interaction;
- timing/routing/synchronization evidence;
- acoustic/diagnostic interpretation;
- Waking/Hush historical contradiction;
- traversal-positioning diagnosis;
- multi-tool relationship problems;
- post-Stillness changed-world behavior;
- world-state consequences from earlier authored choices.

Hard rule: **no single mechanical template may account for more than 20% of shipping Faults** without explicit review. Cosmetic reskins count as the same template.

## Access and revisit-wave rules

The 36 Faults are braided through progression.

Each Fault declares:
- earliest observable phase;
- earliest resolvable phase;
- required verb/state evidence;
- whether it is intentionally visible before solvable;
- region and semantic world owner;
- completion ID;
- hint tier availability.

At least half of all gated Faults should be **seen or meaningfully foreshadowed before they become solvable**, supporting Stillring's remembered-space principle.

No major verb may unlock a global icon dump. New tools reopen a curated revisit wave, not a map choresplosion.

## Reward cadence

Fault rewards must reinforce exploration without turning 100% into compulsory power grind.

Locked reward structure:
1. **Per-Fault reward:** modest deterministic local value appropriate to the fiction (service/material/catalog/progression support), never random.
2. **Early family milestone:** unlocks better Fault-family journal legibility/diagnostic feedback.
3. **Mid family milestone:** improves search/diagnostic quality of life, not combat damage or mandatory survivability.
4. **Late family milestone:** strengthens regional unresolved-Fault assistance through Bellwright/Tessa network records.
5. **36/36:** narrative/service acknowledgement plus completion credit; no unique must-have combat power is held hostage behind total cleanup.

Exact numeric milestone thresholds and item names remain tuning/content-budget work; the semantic reward classes above are locked.

## Completion and persistence ownership

Fault semantic world resolution is owned by **World State** (or the explicitly authoritative gameplay owner for the local defect) under the locked save taxonomy.

Completion Ledger derives/counts the corresponding `fault.<region>.<slug>` obligation after the world transaction settles.

Rules:
- every Fault has one stable semantic completion ID;
- no Actor/package/map path is canonical identity;
- a solved Fault cannot duplicate its one-shot resolution/reward after unload/reload;
- Waking/Hush representation may change without changing the shared semantic Fault identity when it is one cross-layer world fact;
- layer-local Fault facts are explicit only when the authored design truly makes them separate durable facts;
- percentage and regional counts are recomputed summaries, not saved duplicate truth.

## Player-facing assistance

### Early game
- discovered Faults can appear in the journal;
- no exact regional total is required immediately;
- no automatic map marker explosion when a verb is acquired.

### Mid game
- Bellwright/Refuge records may expose regional **known unresolved** information;
- Cantor improvements may make nearby diagnostic evidence clearer without acting as omniscient radar.

### Unringing / late completion sweep
- exact regional completed/total Fault counts become available;
- the courier/refuge network can identify a physical subregion or contextual clue for the remaining Faults;
- stronger accessibility assistance may escalate to clearer map/search guidance.

### Post-ending knowledge / maximum assist
A player pursuing cleanup must be able to get sufficiently strong in-game help that an external wiki is not required. Assistance may become very explicit after authored escalation rather than protecting secrecy forever.

## Accessibility

Fault resolution may not rely solely on:
- pitch/frequency discrimination;
- color discrimination;
- tiny transient visual effects;
- controller vibration;
- spatial audio localization;
- rapid repeated input/mashing.

Every critical diagnostic channel requires an equivalent readable path through visual/text/icon/timing presentation as appropriate. Stronger completion hints are an accessibility option as well as a late-game service.

## Completion / System IDE requirements

The Completion workbench's Fault view must expose:
- Fault semantic ID and region;
- owning world/gameplay fact;
- completion satisfied state;
- observed/resolvable/resolved phase;
- required verb/state gates;
- mechanical-template family tag;
- evidence channels used (audio/visual/text/haptic etc.);
- current hint tier;
- world fact versus Completion Ledger reconciliation;
- one-shot reward transaction state;
- duplicate-ID/template-density validation;
- regional completed/total derived summaries.

Required fixtures:
1. visible-but-not-yet-resolvable Fault;
2. quick diagnosis Fault;
3. deep multi-tool Fault;
4. Waking/Hush shared semantic Fault;
5. post-Stillness changed Fault;
6. resolve → save → exit → load with no reward duplication;
7. region with one missing Fault and late hint escalation;
8. accessibility alternate evidence path;
9. template-density validator intentionally exceeding 20%;
10. stale representation/path refactor with semantic ID unchanged.

## Red-team / rejection conditions

Reject a Fault if:
- it is fundamentally "walk to marker and press interact";
- it is a Vault wearing outdoor scenery;
- it is a Repair Contract stripped of the person/community context that made it meaningful;
- its only distinction is a different model/material/biome;
- resolution depends on random timing/drop/spawn;
- the player could not reasonably infer the relationship from available evidence;
- exact-coordinate map reveal is the intended primary discovery method from first availability;
- it duplicates a previously used mechanical template without a meaningful new relationship/state;
- it exists only to restore the old 48 count.
