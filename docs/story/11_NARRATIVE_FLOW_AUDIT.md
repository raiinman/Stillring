# Project Stillring — Narrative Flow Red-Team Audit

**Status:** active canonical audit / correction contract  
**Related:** Issue #19, `docs/reference/OOT_100_PERCENT_ANATOMY.md`

This document is intentionally adversarial.

It does not ask whether Stillring has good ideas. It asks where a player could become confused, emotionally detached, over-instructed, bored by repetition, or aware of the design machinery underneath the story.

The reference standard is the set of **functional principles** extracted from Ocarina of Time, not its protected expression:

- clear human motivation from one beat to the next;
- world ownership before escalation;
- familiar places changed rather than endless new locations;
- new verbs reopening remembered spaces;
- optional content braided into the route;
- recurring people and places;
- concise exposition with environmental/action delivery;
- major traversal transformation seeded before payoff;
- dungeon/region variety;
- final synthesis of learned vocabulary.

Stillring must exceed that baseline by adding more living-world continuity, character accountability, community recurrence, and offscreen change without becoming heavier to play.

---

# Audit scale

- **BLOCKER** — breaks story logic, emotional premise, or player motivation.
- **HIGH** — likely to make the adventure feel designed rather than lived.
- **MEDIUM** — good material undermined by repetition, over-explanation, or weak recurrence.
- **LOW** — polish issue worth fixing before screenplay lock.

---

# BLOCKER 1 — Three major regions are damaged before the player knows them

## Current problem

Grand Ring occurs after Rootmere, Saltreach, and Emberstep.

High Aerie, Mireglass, and Cairnfall currently receive their full critical-path visits only in the changed world.

The regional bibles now correctly require each major normal-world location to be worth visiting before catastrophe, but the route does not currently deliver that experience.

A cinematic showing High Aerie's wind stopping cannot create recognition if the player never stood on a normal suspended bridge.

A Mireglass reflection becoming wrong means less if the player never saw reflection weirdness used as a joke or navigation habit.

Cairnfall's instruments all turning to one coordinate means less if the player never shared an ordinary public observation night.

## Ocarina-derived principle being violated

**Recontextualization requires prior ownership.**

The major world-state pivot works because familiar geography, people, routes, and routines carry emotional memory into the changed state.

## Fix — expand First Circuit into six living postcards

The First Circuit remains compact, but its **mandatory social route** now touches all six regional cultures before the first full dungeon.

The player does not clear six regions.

They spend roughly 8–15 minutes in one accessible ordinary-life pocket of each later region:

### Rootmere — Keeper Steps / Widow's Ferry
- cross Lio Fen's ferry under normal conditions;
- see Olo running marker notes;
- hear Amsa and Tavin disagree about a harmless inspection matter;
- see the bell-oak moving freely.

### Saltreach — Reedbreak Harbor / Fog Tower foot
- ride a normal harbor launch;
- meet Vessa Mor;
- see captains arguing about a mundane berth schedule;
- hear fog signals working properly enough to be trusted.

### Emberstep — Freight Yard / Cooling Court
- eat near shift-change workers;
- see maker stamps on older tools;
- meet Sena or a foreman before the political accusation dominates the visit;
- watch ordinary molten work complete successfully.

### High Aerie — lower cargo lift / Wind Kitchen
- ride one normal suspended lift;
- meet Sal Em, Pera, or Mevi;
- cross a beautiful wind span with no crisis;
- see apprentices practicing rope signals badly.

### Mireglass — Reedwater Exchange / Lantern Ferry
- ride Neve Or's evening ferry;
- see children playing with reflected marker poles;
- witness a ridiculous small dispute at the Three Tables;
- experience reflection strangeness as normal and funny.

### Cairnfall — South Steps / public observation night
- use a deliberately flawed training scope;
- meet Hett/Pell/Riva/Aro in ordinary context;
- eat or linger at the Long Table;
- see the sky as shared pleasure before it becomes a correction battlefield.

## Flow protection

This does **not** become six quest hubs with six mandatory chore chains.

The circuit has one practical justification: the Cantor Key cannot be safely trusted against a failing central reference until Neris has sampled several independent regional baselines.

Tessa's route knowledge turns that calibration need into a continental courier loop.

The commissions remain Rootmere / Saltreach / Emberstep. The other three stops are calibration/social visits, not dungeon promises.

## Result

Grand Ring can now cut to specific places the player personally remembers.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# BLOCKER 2 — Why Maelor needs Neris is under-specified

## Current problem

Maelor wants three widely separated Waybells stabilized.

But the story does not yet clearly answer:

> Why can Maelor not send normal Bellwarden crews, Quiet Court loyalists, or simply take the Cantor Key and do it himself?

If the answer is merely “because Neris is the protagonist,” the manipulation collapses.

## Fix

The outage creates **local Hush contamination** that makes normal Meridian-referenced diagnostic equipment self-correct toward the same bad central assumptions.

Ilyra's prototype Cantor Key is uniquely useful because it can compare **local relationships without requiring Meridian as the reference**.

However:
- Maelor cannot operate the Key safely at field scale because he spent eleven years optimizing Meridian-centered models rather than Ilyra's local method;
- the Key's damaged field memory has partially imprinted on Neris during the Brindle seam event, making Neris the only current operator who can carry its unstable local calibration without repeatedly resetting it;
- this is a practical accident, not prophecy or bloodline magic;
- Maelor therefore needs Neris to generate three **clean local reference states** he can later abuse as a continent-scale entrainment baseline.

Rusk's desire to confiscate the Key remains sensible because it is dangerous experimental equipment.

Maelor's intervention now has a precise ulterior motive.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# BLOCKER 3 — The opening outage lacks a causal owner

## Current problem

First Ring fails. Hush tears through Brindle. Orin is taken.

The current story does not cleanly state whether:
- Maelor caused it;
- Meridian failed naturally;
- the annual ritual caused it;
- Ilyra's hidden Key caused it;
- or several conditions interacted.

Mystery is good. Authorial ambiguity is not.

## Fix — genuine system failure, deliberately exploited

The annual First Ring is the one day the regional network performs a ceremonial **full-reference check** inherited from older maintenance practice.

The failing Meridian system is already near instability.

Maelor knows the check will expose severe phase drift.

He does **not** create the underlying failure, but he deliberately disables one emergency damping layer so the drift will become undeniable and produce the regional outages he needs for field re-stabilization.

He tells himself a controlled crisis is necessary because the state will otherwise conceal another year of failures.

He underestimates how violently Hush pressure will escape around locally non-Meridian artifacts.

Ilyra's hidden Cantor Key acts as one such local reference inside the Vale workshop, which is why the seam there becomes unusually strong and takes Orin.

Consequences:
- Maelor bears real responsibility for escalating the opening disaster;
- he did not invent the failing network or select Orin as a victim;
- Neris's family loss is connected to his choice without reducing everything to a personal revenge plot;
- later evidence can reveal he disabled damping without needing a villain monologue.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE / reveal timing must remain late Act One or early changed world.

# HIGH 1 — First Circuit currently fights the urgency instead of serving it

## Current problem

Neris believes Orin is trapped across the Hush boundary.

Maelor has just issued urgent commissions.

Tessa then suggests a sightseeing-style circuit because a bellwright should know roads.

That can feel like the game asking the player to ignore the protagonist's emergency so the designer can unlock side systems.

## Fix

The circuit becomes **necessary field calibration** for the damaged Cantor Key.

Maelor's central calibration is provisional because Meridian itself is suspect.

Eda explains that the Key needs multiple local baselines or every later diagnosis risks inheriting the same central error.

Neris wants the circuit because a false reading could send them away from Orin.

Tessa wants the circuit because she knows the physical route network.

The player is not taking a break from the objective.

They are making the only instrument that can find Orin trustworthy.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# HIGH 2 — Line Skiff arrives after the pivot without enough childhood/history seed

## Current problem

Tessa arrives in changed Brindle with an obsolete Line Skiff.

Mechanically it is strong.

Narratively it currently risks feeling like the game spawned a traversal upgrade because Act Two needed faster travel.

## Ocarina-derived principle

A major traversal transformation is stronger when its relationship/history is planted before acquisition and later becomes both freedom and side-system support.

## Fix

During the First Circuit, Graymile contains a locked route shed with a retired **maintenance skiff** hanging from wall hooks.

Tessa has history with it:
- she learned illegal route-running on a similar sled;
- she calls them unsafe, loud, and better than walking;
- central route modernization mothballed them because newer lines depended on active Waybell authorization.

Kest Lorn says the old strips still physically exist even if the network no longer recognizes them.

The player cannot use the machine yet.

After Grand Ring, Tessa retrieves/restores this exact machine or its sister unit because old non-authorized infrastructure still works.

The payoff becomes recognition, not surprise loot.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# HIGH 3 — “The three Countertones” are introduced too much like a checklist

## Current problem

Shortly after Ilyra's reunion, the story explains:
- there are three Countertones;
- they are Motion, Memory, Ending;
- each has a corresponding place/problem;
- Neris needs them to release Stillring safely.

Even though they are relations rather than magic stones, the player-facing structure still risks feeling like:

> collect three named metaphysical things, then proceed.

It also front-loads a large amount of exposition immediately after the most emotionally important reunion in the game.

## Fix — discovery before taxonomy

Ilyra does **not** begin with a complete three-item plan.

### At first reunion
She knows only:
- destroying Meridian immediately is unsafe;
- some naturally unstable places remain capable of local change;
- High Aerie is currently producing a strong non-locked relation that might teach them how to survive outside the Hush.

Immediate objective:

> get back to Brindle, find Tessa, reach High Aerie before its moving windows collapse.

### High Aerie
Neris learns the first relation through action.

Only afterward does Ilyra recognize it as analogous to two incomplete patterns she encountered during eleven years in the Hush.

She gives the category a technical name **after** the player understands one example.

### Bone Archive / Mireglass
Evidence reveals the second relation rather than Ilyra simply pointing at a map.

### Still-Cairn
The third is not known as a destination until Cairnfall data and Orin's repeating workshop reveal what is missing.

## Result

The underlying design may still contain three relations, but the player experiences a chain of discoveries rather than a metaphysical shopping list.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# HIGH 4 — Maelor explains too much before Grand Ring

## Current problem

The current confrontation allows Maelor to explain the three-point engineering truth immediately before activation.

That weakens the investigation Neris, Sena, Tessa, and Caldrin just completed.

## Fix

The heroes arrive already understanding enough:
- three stable references;
- old emergency entrainment;
- unrepaired Waybells can be dragged into alignment.

Maelor does not give a lecture confirming their homework.

He only supplies what they cannot know:
- he intentionally selected those three references;
- he believes the outage proves there will not be another safe repair cycle;
- he has already committed the system to the first step.

Neris should be trying to physically stop him while the conversation happens.

The sequence becomes action with argument inside it.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# HIGH 5 — Ilyra reunion currently contains too much plot delivery

## Current problem

The reunion quickly covers:
- eleven years in Hush;
- failed return attempts;
- Maelor learning seam information;
- Stillring mechanics;
- moving pockets;
- Countertones;
- Deep Wounds;
- access requirements.

That is excellent design information and poor reunion rhythm.

## Fix

Split into three experiences:

1. **Recognition / anger** — minimal plot. Neris wants Orin. Ilyra wants proof Neris is real.
2. **Workbench survival** — playable repair demonstrates that Hush states can repeat and that Ilyra survives through routine.
3. **Escape problem** — one immediate technical fact: Meridian phase-lock is pulling everything toward one state; a locally changing relation near High Aerie might offer a way to move.

The larger history is earned over later travel, Bone Archive, and Mireglass.

Ilyra must also receive mundane scenes where she is hungry, irritable, funny, wrong about Neris's preferences, and interested in a repair unrelated to the conspiracy.

**Status:** REQUIRED CHANGE.

# HIGH 6 — Tessa's Saltreach confession was too cleanly innocent in one version

## Problem

Earlier wording said Tessa believed the Quiet Court freight was tax evasion.

That lets the character escape the exact responsibility theme the story otherwise gives her.

## Correction

Tessa knew:
- the manifests were forged;
- the pay was abnormally high;
- the containers were restricted research hardware;
- asking questions would probably make the job disappear.

She did not know Stillring or the Court's ultimate plan.

Her failure is **deliberate incuriosity**, not secret complicity.

The side story then rhymes with Orin/Ilyra/Rusk without making all four the same person.

**Status:** ALREADY partially corrected in character/100% docs; core Act One wording must match.

# HIGH 7 — Too many bosses reveal “the monster/system was not really the problem”

## Current pattern

- Mawhart: creature trapped by bad braces; survives.
- Nine-Lung Leviathan: creature trapped by coercive pressure system; survives.
- Cinder Regent: bad command logic.
- Saint Varo: historical construct trapped by official story.
- Mirror Widow: fragmented person trapped by competing histories.
- Gravemoon: correction engine trapped in loop.

Each idea is good.

Together they create predictability:

> the boss is always an innocent thing being misused by a system.

## Fix

Vary moral and combat conclusions.

- **Mawhart** remains a liberation boss because it establishes the pattern once.
- **Nine-Lung Leviathan** becomes bittersweet: the machinery can be removed, but generations of forced integration have left the animal too damaged to survive long-term. It chooses open water and later dies offshore; Saltreach holds a practical harbor remembrance rather than a trophy celebration. The player succeeds and still cannot repair all accumulated harm.
- **Cinder Regent** remains an unambiguous machine hazard and can be destroyed/disabled without moral reversal.
- **Choir of Talons** remains ordinary predators. No secret tragedy.
- some optional elites are simply dangerous people/creatures/machines whose actions are their own.

Stillring is not a story where violence is always caused by misunderstood infrastructure.

**Status:** RECOMMENDED CORE CHANGE.

# HIGH 8 — Side quests repeat the central thesis too often

## Current problem

A high percentage of C01–C24 involve:
- state standard versus local variation;
- official record versus suppressed record;
- one correct value versus multiple healthy values;
- preservation versus release.

The side content risks making Orra feel like every citizen independently enrolled in the same philosophy seminar.

## Fix — side-story palette

Every region needs at least three kinds of optional interaction:

### A. Theme-bearing
Directly complicates infrastructure/memory/control.

### B. Character-bearing
Primarily about relationships, embarrassment, affection, rivalry, grief, ambition, or responsibility.

### C. Life-bearing
Funny, practical, celebratory, competitive, aesthetic, or pleasantly pointless.

No more than roughly half of a region's optional scenes should directly restate the central political/philosophical conflict.

Examples added to quiet-moment ledger:
- ferry cook-off judged badly by children;
- High Aerie apprentices losing a laundry sheet into a wind route;
- Cairnfall students betting on two deliberately flawed scopes;
- Emberstep shift crews competing to cast the ugliest functional bracket;
- Mireglass reflected-marker ring game;
- Tessa dragging Neris into an inn argument about whose road shortcut is actually shorter.

These are not necessarily completion-bearing quests.

Some living-world interactions should award nothing.

**Status:** REQUIRED INTERACTION CHANGE.

# MEDIUM 1 — C01 risks a hidden “correct” choice

The story summary often emphasizes the crooked local bell creating the useful moving pocket.

The canonical setup ledger correctly gives the standardized repair a different safety advantage.

## Fix

Every core-story callback must preserve both branches:

- local asymmetric repair -> irregular moving pocket around several homes;
- standardized bracket -> no pocket, but the bell remains structurally stable during a dangerous later motion pulse and protects a different evacuation route.

Neither is the foresight/goodness answer.

# MEDIUM 2 — Neris needs a personal future, not only family rescue

## Problem

Neris's wants are strong but mostly reactive:
- find Orin;
- learn Ilyra truth;
- stop Maelor;
- repair damage.

The ending makes Neris a traveling bellwright, but that identity is not yet seeded strongly enough as something Neris might want.

## Fix

Throughout Act One:
- Neris is fascinated by regional repair practices;
- keeps sketches of weird local solutions;
- envies Tessa's road knowledge while complaining about travel;
- expresses discomfort with inheriting Orin's workshop as a predetermined life.

After Grand Ring, travel stops being escape from home and becomes chosen responsibility.

The final road then pays off a desire that grew during play rather than inventing a career in the epilogue.

# MEDIUM 3 — Rusk needs more recurrence before his crisis choice

Rusk currently enters as confiscation authority and later opens locked doors during Grand Ring.

Give him at least two middle appearances:
- after Rootmere, he argues Neris's findings cannot justify removing every central brace and is partly right;
- after Saltreach, he orders an investigation of the forged manifests but refuses public release without verification;
- before Emberstep, his attempt to protect institutional credibility directly worsens the foundry conflict.

Then his Grand Ring act—opening the very locks he defended—has history behind it.

---

# MEDIUM 4 — Final philosophical language is too polished in places

Lines like the central Maelor/Neris exchange can remain.

But several supporting lines currently sound as though characters have read the thematic checklist.

## Fix

Screenplay pass should prefer:
- trade metaphors;
- personal examples;
- unfinished sentences;
- practical disagreement;
- people talking around what hurts.

The Story Bible may say “coordination without enforced sameness.”

A ferry captain should say when the fog bell is wrong and who misses the rocks because of it.

---

# MEDIUM 5 — Listener alternatives should use the player's actual memories

The current final sequence lists generic alternate good worlds.

## Fix

Mandatory alternatives use critical-path memories.

Optional completion substitutes or adds personalized preserved moments:
- Mara's fire-bell neighborhood untouched;
- a favorite ferry/social space before catastrophe;
- an optional NPC whose life changed;
- the exact workshop arrangement the player lingered around;
- a completed relationship scene that the Listener offers to freeze permanently.

The finale then weaponizes **the player's attachment**, not only Neris's biography.

---

# LOW — Credits risk resolving politics too cleanly

The credits should show rebuilding in progress:
- arguments continue;
- some old symbols remain while public interpretation changes;
- decentralized systems fail occasionally and are repaired;
- Maelor's trial is ongoing rather than a neat historical verdict;
- Mireglass seam policy remains contested;
- Rusk can participate in disclosure without becoming the face of reform.

The world has moved, not arrived.

1. Pre-Grand Ring six-region familiarity.

4. First Circuit urgency rewrite.
