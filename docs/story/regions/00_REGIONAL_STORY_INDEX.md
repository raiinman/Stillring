# Project Stillring — Regional Story Index

**Status:** canonical regional narrative index  
**Related:** Issue #18, `docs/02_STORY_BIBLE.md`, `docs/11_QUEST_AND_COMPLETION_LEDGER.md`

Stillring's regions are not biome wrappers around dungeons.

They are communities shaped by work, infrastructure, geography, inherited arguments, and dependence on systems larger than themselves. The player should be able to remember **people and social spaces** from a region even if every dungeon room were removed from memory.

# Regional narrative contract

Every major region must answer these questions before production content is considered story-ready:

## Ordinary life
- What wakes people in the morning?
- What work happens before noon?
- What fails often enough that locals have slang for it?
- Where do people eat, argue, trade, mourn, wait, and gossip?
- What does a child learn about the region before learning its politics?

## Infrastructure
- What does the Waybell/network actually do here?
- Which local practices existed before central standardization?
- Which state standards genuinely improved life?
- Which local knowledge was erased or made unofficial?
- Who benefits from the current arrangement and who carries its hidden cost?

## Internal disagreement
No region gets one unified political opinion.

At minimum include:
- someone who values Cairnspire standardization for good reasons;
- someone who resents it for good reasons;
- someone whose position is primarily practical rather than ideological;
- someone whose opinion changes after the Grand Ring.

## Recurring people
Named residents are not quest terminals.

Each recurring resident should have:
- work;
- relationships with at least two other people;
- an opinion about a local problem unrelated to Neris;
- pre-dungeon state;
- post-dungeon state;
- post-Stillness state if alive/mobile;
- Unringing or credits role where appropriate.

## Catastrophe
Grand Ring changes:
- access to food/water/work;
- who can move and when;
- local leadership legitimacy;
- family separation;
- maintenance priorities;
- trust in old systems.

It cannot be merely an art-lighting swap.

## Unringing
Each community project must grow out of people and practices introduced before the late game.

No region waits for Neris to invent self-government in the final hour.

# Regional overview

| Region | What ordinary life is built around | Network dependence | Core local argument | Changed-world question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brindle / First Circuit | hill farming, small repair work, courier roads, mills | warning relays, route clocks, flood signals | how much practical independence is worth inefficiency | can small local systems survive without becoming isolated? |
| Cairnspire | administration, maintenance, trade routing, archives, dense civic services | nearly total | does scale require centralized authority and secrecy? | what happens when the center cannot command itself? |
| Rootmere | orchard/ferry work, living-material maintenance, irrigation | flood/load timing and Waybell reference | should living infrastructure conform to fixed standards? | can flexible local systems coordinate without being forced rigid? |
| Saltreach | fishing, ship repair, navigation, pressure works | navigation/fog/tide coordination | safety through common timing vs knowledge tied to a changing coast | how do many crews coordinate when the clock itself is wrong? |
| Emberstep | foundries, repair alloys, kilns, clinics, heavy fabrication | casting schedules, heat distribution, state inspection | anonymous standard purity vs traceable maker responsibility | can industrial coordination survive without one schedule? |
| High Aerie | rigging, archives, weather observation, suspended habitation | load timing, chain inspection, archival authority | preservation as stewardship vs preservation as hierarchy | who decides what knowledge survives when weight/time are limited? |
| Mireglass | reed farming, interpretation, mediation, water travel, memory customs | diplomatic/legal records and route reference | whether one official version is needed for peace | how do people live when incompatible memories become physically present? |
| Cairnfall | surveying, astronomy, instrument calibration, teaching | correction/reference signals | agreement as accuracy vs disagreement as information | can a network report differences without correcting them away? |
| Hush-Cairnspire / Null | accumulated abandoned civic states and central machinery | the network is the prison itself | preservation vs continued possibility | what must be allowed to end for anything to continue? |

# Shared cultural baseline

Orra is currently planned as predominantly human communities with regional cultural variation. Do **not** create one-to-one fantasy-species stand-ins for familiar Zelda peoples merely to recreate a classic adventure silhouette.

Regional distinction should come from:
- labor;
- material culture;
- climate and routes;
- historical relationship to Meridian;
- food and maintenance practices;
- architecture;
- civic organization;
- speech rhythm and technical vocabulary;
- family/community traditions.

A later deliberate species/fantasy-people decision would require its own worldbuilding pass rather than being smuggled in through regional stereotypes.

---

# Recurrence rule

At least one resident first encountered before each major regional dungeon must reappear after Stillness and again during the Unringing or credits.

Preferred pattern:

`ordinary person -> local problem -> dungeon consequence -> catastrophe consequence -> local action -> credits payoff`

This recurrence is one of the main tools for making a 20–30 hour adventure feel like one world rather than a sequence of levels.

---

# Voice rule

Regional speech can differ through:
- preferred metaphors;
- sentence length;
- trade terminology;
- local humor;
- what people assume needs no explanation.

Do not write phonetic caricature accents.

A Saltreach captain and an Emberstep foreman should sound different because they think about different physical problems, not because one drops letters from words.

---

# Credits rule

Credits shots must be earned by earlier people.

Bad:
> Generic Rootmere villagers maintain trees.

Better:
> Olo Wren reads the recovered worker name from a new maintenance board while Amsa corrects his rope placement and Lio Fen argues from the ferry below.

The player should recognize faces and relationships in the ending without needing captions to explain why they matter.
