# Regional Story Bible — Rootmere

**Status:** canonical regional narrative  
**Related:** C06–C09, Root Cathedral, Anchor Line, Setups `green_span` and `wrens_margin`

Rootmere is a floodplain community whose infrastructure is partly **grown, trained, repaired, and negotiated with** rather than simply constructed.

Its central argument is not nature versus technology.

Rootmere is extremely technical.

The disagreement is over whether living systems should be forced into fixed engineering assumptions or whether engineering should account for systems that flex, season, heal, and change.

---

# 1. Ordinary life

Rootmere's settlements sit on raised root mounds, timber platforms, and stone flood islands connected by ferries and trained living spans.

People make a living through:
- orchard cultivation;
- reed/resin harvesting;
- ferry work;
- floodgate maintenance;
- living-material repair;
- fish traps and wetland grazing;
- regional production of flexible seals, cordage, and organic composite parts.

The bell-oak is not worshiped as a god.

It is old civic infrastructure and a living organism. Locals can respect it deeply while still complaining about sap in their hair and roots breaking floors.

---

# 2. Daily rhythm

## Dawn
Keepers walk water marks before bells are consulted.

This matters culturally: **look first, signal second**.

## Midday
Ferries carry workers between orchard islands; rootweavers adjust young spans while material is warm/flexible.

## Evening
Water gates are set for predicted overnight flow and families gather on raised drying platforms.

During heavy rain, meals move to shared flood halls because nobody assumes a house will remain the best place to sleep.

---

# 3. Why the Waybell matters

Rootmere's Waybell is integrated with the enormous bell-oak network and helps coordinate:
- floodgate timing;
- irrigation windows;
- ferry warnings;
- regional upstream/downstream load expectations.

Central synchronization reduced deadly communication delays generations ago.

It also encouraged Bellwarden engineers to redesign local systems around one expected timing model.

The old foreign braces at the heart of the Root Cathedral are the physical result of that mindset: they “corrected” a living load path until the system could no longer move safely.

---

# 4. Social spaces

## Keeper Steps
Raised public platforms surrounding the main bell-oak trunk.

Used for:
- maintenance briefings;
- markets during dry weather;
- public arguments when state inspectors arrive.

## Widow's Ferry
Lio Fen's crossing, named before his marriage and therefore the source of endless jokes after he actually became widowed.

The ferry is a social bottleneck: everybody eventually hears everybody else's business there.

## Split-Note Orchard
Nemi Tal's orchard and irrigation demonstration site.

Seasonal bell tones differ with water pressure, making the orchard a local example of healthy states that do not sound identical.

## Reed-Screw House
Regional Bellwright Refuge built around manually fabricated pump screws, resin seals, and living-material repair benches.

## Root Cathedral approach
Not a holy entrance.

It is an old civic irrigation sanctuary with generations of maintenance marks layered over each other.

Locals call it “the Cathedral” because Cairnspire surveyors did, and some keepers roll their eyes at the name.

# 5. Recurring cast

## Keeper Amsa Wren — senior bell-oak keeper

**Contract:** C06 The Root That Isn't Sick.  
**Work:** regional load/flood keeper; coordinates bell-oak maintenance.  
**Relationships:** grandmother/aunt-like guardian to Olo Wren; long professional rivalry with state inspector Tavin Ro; relies on Lio's ferry reports; trades seasonal data with Nemi Tal.

### Before Neris
Amsa has years of evidence that old metal bracing is damaging the tree but no authority to cut state-installed supports.

She distrusts Cairnspire engineers because previous “repairs” arrived with conclusions already written.

### Important nuance
Amsa can be wrong too.

She initially underestimates how much modern regional warning coordination saved lives upstream.

Her late-game position becomes local control **plus** shared data, not isolation.

### Post-Root Cathedral
Her warning is validated, but she refuses to use the victory to purge every state component from Rootmere.

Useful parts stay.

### Post-Stillness
Amsa coordinates people around oscillating living routes that still move because they were never fully rigid-phase systems.

### Unringing
Leads the living-resonator community project while insisting other regions publish their load assumptions openly.

### Credits
Corrects Olo's rope placement while he reads a recovered worker name from the maintenance board.

---

## Olo Wren — child keeper apprentice

**Contract:** C09 A Name Under Bark.  
**Work:** too young for full keeper status; performs marker checks, carries water notes, knows exactly which adults ignore which rules.

**Relationships:** Amsa's family; idolizes Lio's ferry work; bothers Caldrin with questions after the Bone Archive; friends with orchard worker children.

### Before Grand Ring
Olo notices the bark scar/name because children use that root as a shortcut adults stopped looking at closely.

### Post-Stillness
Becomes valuable because small bodies can traverse narrow moving root paths adults cannot.

This should not turn Olo into a child soldier. His contributions are messaging/observation.

### C09 payoff
The recovered worker name becomes Olo's first public maintenance annotation.

### Credits
Olo is training properly, still asking questions before Amsa finishes answering the last one.

---

## Lio Fen — ferry operator

**Contract:** C07 Widow's Ferrygate.  
**Work:** ferry crossing, river-depth observation, informal news exchange.  
**Relationships:** late husband **Karo Fen** built the asymmetric gate; longtime friend of Nemi; argues with Amsa about closures because keepers can close a river route without having to refund passengers.

### Before Neris
Lio is tired of state inspectors treating asymmetry as evidence of incompetence.

He also knows Karo's gate is increasingly difficult to maintain and cannot let grief become a technical argument.

### Post-C07
Whichever linkage the player chooses, Lio learns/document the reason rather than treating preservation/replacement as morality.

### Post-Stillness
The ferry becomes one of the few ways to cross between moving root islands.

### Unringing
Lio organizes manual crossing windows tied to actual water/load observations.

### Credits
Runs a crowded ferry beneath the new maintenance board, complaining that decentralization somehow created more paperwork.

---

## Nemi Tal — orchard keeper

**Contract:** C08 Orchard of Split Notes.  
**Work:** irrigation and orchard health; experiments with pressure-dependent signaling.  
**Relationships:** trades fruit with Lio; supplies resin-bearing cuttings to Bram Ko; thinks Amsa can be too protective of keeper tradition.

### Opinion
Nemi wants better instrumentation, including state-made equipment.

Her conflict is with diagnostics that label seasonal variation as fault.

### Post-C08
Her two-tone irrigation record becomes one of Neris's strongest practical examples of healthy multiple states.

### Post-Stillness
Nemi adapts irrigation around moving intervals rather than waiting for the central clock to return.

### Unringing
Helps design Rootmere's local condition-report format.

### Credits
Her orchard posts two expected healthy tone ranges instead of one “correct” note.

---

## Bram Ko — rootweaver / living-material craftsperson

**Work:** trains young spans, prepares living fiber, repairs flexible joints.  
**Relationships:** former apprentice of Amsa; supplier for the Reed-Screw House; dislikes Tavin Ro personally but admits Tavin once prevented a dangerous amateur span from opening.

### Story role
Bram owns the **green-span setup**.

He asks Neris to help choose between rigid state plating and flexible keeper weave because neither option is obviously foolish before Grand Ring.

### Post-Stillness
The branch determines which route survives/moves first.

### Unringing
Bram contributes living fibers to The Unstandard Tool and teaches visiting engineers how to specify **range of motion** rather than fixed shape.

---

## Tavin Ro — regional Bellwarden inspector

**Work:** state safety inspection; outsider who has lived in Rootmere long enough to be neither fully local nor simply “the capital guy.”

**Relationships:** professional enemy/friend of Amsa; trusts Nemi's measurements; fined Lio twice and quietly voided a third fine; thinks Bram is brilliant and reckless.

### Before Neris
Tavin believes old braces should remain until an engineered replacement exists because removing load-bearing infrastructure without full modeling is dangerous.

That is a legitimate concern.

### Root Cathedral aftermath
When evidence proves the braces are the fault, Tavin signs their removal record rather than trying to hide the mistake.

### Post-Stillness
His state authority is useless, but his inspection experience is not.

He becomes the person who asks local projects uncomfortable failure-mode questions.

### Unringing
Helps Rootmere document local standards in a form other regions can actually read.

### Credits
Still carries an official inspection stamp nobody legally needs anymore because, as he says, “it makes a satisfying noise.”

---

## Sera Pell — flood-hall cook and record keeper

**Work:** runs shared flood-hall kitchen and keeps family-location boards during storms.  
**Relationships:** knows every ferry family; sister to a worker whose name vanished from an old maintenance record; friendly with Caldrin after he helps trace it.

### Narrative role
Sera turns flood response from abstract infrastructure into household reality.

### Post-Stillness
Her manual family board becomes essential because official resident lists cannot update.

### C09 connection
The erased worker may be a relative from Sera's family line, giving the recovered name living consequence without making it a secret royal ancestor or destiny reveal.

### Credits
Sera writes the recovered name onto the flood-hall board beside current workers.

# 6. Internal opinion spectrum

## Amsa
Living local systems were damaged by fixed central assumptions.

## Tavin
Shared safety rules exist because local brilliance can still kill people when failures cross boundaries.

## Nemi
The problem is not measurement; it is demanding one healthy state.

## Lio
If a regulation closes a ferry, someone must understand what that does to people who use it.

## Bram
Maintenance should specify what a thing must **do**, not exactly what shape it must have.

## Sera
During floods, ideological purity is less important than knowing where everyone sleeps tonight.

# 7. Region story progression

## Arrival
Neris's capital authorization actively damages trust.

The player has to diagnose before being granted Root Cathedral access.

## Dungeon consequence
Removing the braces proves Amsa's warning but also reveals how dependent modern Rootmere remains on region-wide timing.

Amsa does not declare “we never needed them.”

## Between regions
On later revisits:
- Lio's ferry changes;
- Nemi's orchard develops new diagnostic language;
- Bram's young span visibly grows/changes;
- Tavin files a public correction to the state report;
- Olo keeps noticing things adults stopped seeing.

## Grand Ring
Rootmere's response varies by infrastructure type:
- rigid state components phase-lock hard;
- living spans may continue limited oscillation;
- water itself creates dangerous pressure mismatches between frozen/moving systems.

The bell-oak becomes a patchwork of still and moving tissue rather than a simple frozen tree visual.

## Changed-world return
Amsa is not waiting at the same quest marker.

She may be coordinating a different flood island while Olo carries messages and Lio runs emergency crossings.

## Unringing
Community project: **Living Accord Network**.

Rootmere does not replace the Waybell with one smaller local bell.

Instead it coordinates:
- root tension markers;
- water-height observations;
- ferry signals;
- orchard pressure tones;
- shared reports to neighboring regions.

The local system changes with conditions and publishes its assumptions.

# 8. Setup/payoff continuity

## Green Span
Rigid plate vs flexible weave changes the first post-Stillness route.

Neither branch kills an NPC or blocks completion.

Later repair work can recover the route not initially available.

## Wren's Margin
Copying Amsa's handwritten warning before it enters official channels lets Caldrin trace the erased worker sooner after archive access becomes unstable.

If not copied, the same truth remains recoverable through harder field evidence.

This is optional depth, not the only route to necessary history.

---

# 9. Local food and material culture

Common foods:
- smoked river fish with sour orchard fruit;
- reed-flour flatcakes;
- fermented plum/root relish stored above flood line;
- thick bean stews cooked in shared flood halls during storms.

Material culture:
- flexible resin joints left visible for inspection;
- woven load markers that change orientation under strain;
- maintenance notes carved into replaceable bark-safe plaques rather than the tree itself.

People often touch/press structures while talking about them. A Rootmere engineer trusts what a joint **feels like under load**, not only what a gauge says.

---

# 10. Credits payoff

High-completion credits should show recognizable continuity:

- Amsa and Tavin arguing over a new report format while clearly using it together;
- Olo reading the recovered worker name;
- Lio's ferry moving under manually coordinated signals;
- Nemi showing two healthy orchard tones to a visiting Cairnspire apprentice;
- Bram training a young root span with an intentionally flexible joint;
- Sera updating the flood-hall family board;
- Mawhart visible at distance in healthy woodland rather than displayed as a trophy.

Rootmere's ending is not a victory of “nature over machines.”

It is a community learning to engineer **with change instead of against it**.
