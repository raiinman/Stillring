# Regional Story Bible — Cairnfall

**Status:** canonical regional narrative  
**Related:** C23–C24, Fallen Orrery, Gravemoon Engine, seam mode, Hett Vara

Cairnfall is not “the astronomy region.” It is a high, dry survey district whose identity is built around **measurement, calibration, teaching, disagreement between instruments, and the limits of correction**.

Its core argument is not science versus superstition. Cairnfall is intensely empirical.

The disagreement is whether good measurement means making instruments agree—or preserving disagreement long enough to understand what it tells you.

---

# 1. Ordinary life

People make a living through:
- astronomy and weather observation;
- map and route calibration;
- instrument repair;
- survey teaching;
- clock and lens manufacture;
- signal alignment;
- maintaining water cisterns and long-distance mountain supply routes;
- hosting visiting apprentices from across Orra.

Cairnfall’s observatory is part school, part workshop, part public utility, part research institution.

The region is culturally comfortable with argument. A meal can turn into three adults comparing measurements nobody asked them to take.

---

# 2. Daily rhythm

## Dawn
Day crews compare overnight instrument logs before touching calibration screws.

## Morning
Students run ground-survey exercises and service public route markers.

## Midday
Lensmakers work while light is strongest.

## Evening
The whole region slows before night observation. Workshops darken unnecessary lamps to reduce glare.

## Night
Public observation decks open. Families, students, visiting couriers, and technicians share scopes and cheap tea.

A local saying is:

> “If two instruments agree too quickly, check whether they share the same mistake.”

---

# 3. Why the Waybell matters

Cairnfall’s Waybell is primarily a reference/listening node. It helps compare:
- regional clocks;
- weather and pressure readings;
- survey baselines;
- long-distance route signals;
- celestial observations used for seasonal calibration.

Central correction brought enormous benefits. It allowed maps from distant regions to share coordinates and made shipping, surveying, and disaster response more reliable.

But Meridian increasingly stopped being a comparison reference and became an **automatic correction authority**. Instruments that disagreed were silently normalized in software and maintenance procedure.

That destroys exactly the information Cairnfall was built to preserve.

# 4. Social spaces

## South Steps Observation Deck
Public viewing platform where children and visitors use inexpensive scopes.

## Night Survey Hut
Refuge/workshop for field teams, full of mismatched lenses and hand-written drift notes.

## The Long Table
Communal dining and calibration table where students lay instruments between bowls and cups.

## Lensmarket
Small commercial strip selling glass, housings, maps, repairs, and dubious “perfectly corrected” tourist scopes.

## Fallen Orrery yard
Training and maintenance court around the old rotating observational machinery.

# 5. Recurring cast

## Hett Vara — senior surveyor

**Contracts:** C23 The Telescope That Disagrees; C24 No Correction Required.  
**Work:** field surveying, teaching, observatory diagnostics.  
**Relationships:** mentor to junior surveyors Pell Dain and Riva Sol; professional friend of Jaro Pell in High Aerie; frequent adversary of administrator Marek Tann.

### Before Neris
Hett has noticed that central correction logs are too clean. She suspects disagreement is being erased before human review.

### Important nuance
Hett is not anti-standardization. She loves shared coordinate systems and thinks people who reject calibration entirely are dangerous.

### Post-Gravemoon
She becomes a leading advocate for publishing raw and corrected measurements together.

### Credits
Runs a school where students are graded partly on explaining disagreement instead of merely matching a reference answer.

---

## Pell Dain — junior surveyor

**Work:** route markers, student field measurements, instrument transport.  
**Relationships:** student of Hett; best friend/rival of Riva Sol; sibling of supply cook Mar Dain.

### Before crisis
Pell is obsessed with getting the “right” answer and quietly redoes measurements until they match expected values.

### Post-Stillness
That habit becomes dangerous because Meridian’s expected values are actively wrong.

### Change
Learns to report raw disagreement without treating it as personal failure.

### Credits
Corrects a younger student who tries to erase an outlier from a chart.

---

## Riva Sol — junior instrument maker

**Work:** clock housings, lens mounts, survey tools.  
**Relationships:** friend/rival of Pell; apprentice to lensmaker Aro Venn; respects Hett but finds her lectures endless.

### Before crisis
Riva is mechanically confident and statistically impatient.

### Post-Stillness
Becomes valuable because she can identify when “impossible” readings come from physical distortion rather than bad data.

### Unringing
Helps build local instruments that can report confidence ranges instead of one corrected output.

---

## Marek Tann — observatory administrator

**Work:** scheduling, certification, data dispatch, funding.  
**Relationships:** clashes with Hett; relies on Aro Venn’s workshop; responsible for keeping visiting apprentices safe.

### Before Grand Ring
Marek supports aggressive central correction because most regional users do not have time to interpret raw disagreement. He can point to real disasters caused by incompatible maps.

### Problem
He has allowed convenience to become opacity. Operators no longer know when a number was corrected or by how much.

### Post-Stillness
His confidence in automatic correction collapses, but he does not become anti-system.

### Credits
Runs dispatch rules requiring raw-value provenance and correction annotations.

---

## Aro Venn — lensmaker

**Work:** optical glass, mirror alignment, repair.  
**Relationships:** mentor to Riva; trades materials with Emberstep; friendly argument partner to Hett.

### Story role
Owns the old uncorrected survey lens seeded during the First Circuit.

### Opinion
Aro believes imperfect instruments are fine if their imperfections are measured and documented.

### Memorable ordinary beat
He sells intentionally flawed training lenses to students so they learn calibration instead of assuming factory perfection.

### Post-Stillness
Uses known lens drift to distinguish real Hush coordinates from Meridian-generated corrected ones.

---

## Mar Dain — supply cook / storekeeper

**Work:** meals, fuel, water stores, visitor lodging.  
**Relationships:** Pell’s older sibling; distrusts Marek’s budget priorities; trades dried food with Tessa’s couriers.

### Story role
Makes the observatory human.

Mar measures almost nothing precisely on principle and constantly irritates surveyors by cooking “until it looks done.”

### Post-Stillness
Manual stores become critical when automated inventory locks to a preserved state.

### Credits
Runs a larger public night-observation supper with intentionally unlabeled serving spoons.

---

## Leto Marr — public astronomy teacher

**Work:** children’s lessons, public observation nights, basic navigation.  
**Relationships:** colleague of Hett; parent of two young children; often mediates Pell/Riva arguments.

### Before crisis
Leto sees astronomy as civic education, not elite research.

### Grand Ring effect
Public lesson notes freeze at one “perfect” predicted sky while the actual sky/Hush alignment diverges.

### Unringing
Turns discrepancy into a public lesson: models are tools, not reality.

### Credits
Children compare two valid observations from different nights and are asked why they differ.

---

# 6. Internal opinion spectrum

- **Hett:** disagreement contains information and must be preserved.
- **Marek:** shared corrected outputs are necessary for large systems to function.
- **Aro:** imperfection is manageable if measured honestly.
- **Pell:** initially equates agreement with competence.
- **Riva:** trusts physical inspection more than abstract correction.
- **Leto:** any system ordinary people cannot understand at all is civically fragile.

Cairnfall does not conclude “correction is bad.” It concludes that hidden correction is dangerous.

# 7. Before anything bad happens — worth visiting test

Required pre-catastrophe texture:
- attend a public night-observation session;
- borrow a deliberately flawed training scope from Aro;
- hear Pell and Riva bet lunch over whose reading is wrong;
- eat at the Long Table while surveyors argue across plates;
- help a child find one bright object in the sky with no story consequence;
- watch a technician chalk drift values directly onto an instrument housing;
- see the Fallen Orrery turning slowly under ordinary maintenance conditions.

The player must remember **the sky as a shared public pleasure** before it becomes a correction battlefield.

---

# 8. Grand Ring impact

Stillring causes all major listening arrays to turn toward one impossible coordinate.

Consequences:
- corrected maps become dangerously self-consistent;
- local clocks agree while physical events do not;
- some instruments repeat old readings exactly;
- observation crews disagree over whether to trust hardware, sky, or network;
- automated route beacons begin correcting travelers toward wrong paths.

The horror here is epistemic but must remain physical: people get lost, water deliveries miss windows, and structures align to bad references.

---

# 9. Fallen Orrery consequence

The Gravemoon Engine is not evil machinery. It is a correction system trapped in an instruction loop.

Neris does not smash the observatory.

Neris disconnects its central authority while preserving local measurement.

This is important because Cairnfall provides one of the story’s strongest examples that dismantling central control does not require rejecting complex infrastructure.

---

# 10. Unringing project — Open Baseline Network

Cairnfall establishes:
- published raw measurements;
- optional correction layers;
- local reference notes;
- confidence/uncertainty ranges;
- shared coordinate formats;
- independent instrument audits.

Other regions can still coordinate with Cairnfall because formats remain compatible.

The difference is that disagreement is visible instead of silently erased.

# 12. Credits payoff

High-completion credits may show:
- Hett teaching disagreement analysis;
- Pell leaving an outlier visibly uncrossed-out;
- Riva fitting a new local instrument with a transparent correction dial;
- Marek dispatching both raw and corrected route data;
- Aro selling flawed training lenses to a new class;
- Mar serving dinner during a public astronomy night;
- Leto’s children pointing at two instruments that disagree and laughing instead of panicking.

Cairnfall’s ending is not “nothing is objectively true.”

It is a community learning that **accuracy improves when disagreement is investigated rather than hidden**.
