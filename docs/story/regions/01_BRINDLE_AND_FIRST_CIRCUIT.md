# Regional Story Bible — Brindle and the First Circuit

**Status:** canonical regional narrative  
**Related contracts:** C01, C03, C04; Setups S01–S03; Old Viaduct Refuge

Brindle is where Stillring proves the world is worth caring about before asking the player to save it.

It is not “the peaceful tutorial village.” It is a working hill settlement close enough to Cairnspire to depend on the network and far enough away to resent paying for standards designed elsewhere.

The First Circuit is the ring of old roads, relay hamlets, repair sheds, toll remnants, farms, and service stations between Brindle and Cairnspire. It is where the player first learns that Orra is made of **maintenance relationships**, not just destinations.

---

# 1. Brindle identity

Brindle sits on a sloped agricultural shelf above two flood channels and an old bell road.

It grew because three kinds of work meet there:

- hill orchards and grain terraces;
- small repair/fabrication shops serving farms and road crews;
- courier traffic avoiding the more expensive state stations closer to Cairnspire.

Most homes have at least one repair bench.

Not because everyone is a bellwright, but because waiting three days for a certified technician is a good way to lose a harvest.

## Local saying

> “If it breaks on Firstday, fix it before Secondday learns about it.”

This is practical humor, not an anti-government slogan.

---

# 2. Daily rhythm

## Dawn
- mill sluices open;
- orchard workers check frost/flood chimes;
- courier inn shutters are raised before kitchens start;
- Orin's workshop usually has customers before Neris is awake.

## Midday
- the central lane becomes repair/trade space;
- children carry messages between workshops because the local bell sequence is intentionally sparse;
- courier traffic peaks.

## Evening
- farm signals report which channels should remain open overnight;
- small local bells mark practical events rather than synchronized ceremony;
- people gather at Dori Senn's road inn or outside Mara Pell's carpenter shed.

## First Ring festival
Once a year Brindle participates in the ceremonial relay toward Cairnspire.

Locals disagree about what it means:
- civic belonging;
- expensive nonsense;
- useful annual systems test;
- memory of people who built the road.

The festival should feel normal enough that its failure is disturbing.

# 3. Important social spaces

## The Vale Workshop
Home, shop, and emotional center.

It contains:
- Orin's ordinary repair business;
- Ilyra's sealed history;
- the Cantor Key compartment;
- an old traveling case Orin almost opens before First Ring;
- enough clutter that later Hush versions can be recognizably wrong.

## Mara Pell's Carpenter Shed
Half workshop, half neighborhood bench.

People borrow tools, argue about repairs, and leave messages there.

Mara's late wife **Tovi Pell** cast Brindle's crooked fire bell from scrap metal donated after an older flood.

This is why C01 is emotionally specific rather than “old lady likes old item.”

## Dori's Road Inn
Small, cheap, useful.

Couriers, road technicians, orchard hands, and off-duty Bellwarden staff all use it.

The inn is politically interesting because people who would not attend the same meeting still eat at the same long table.

## The Mill Walk
A wooden/stone service route beside the tailrace.

Children use it as a shortcut despite posted warnings.

The old mill tailbox produces the strange resonance promise that becomes an Anchor revisit.

## Relay Square
Used for markets most days and First Ring once a year.

The Waybell relay is practical infrastructure before it is symbolism.

# 4. Recurring Brindle cast

## Orin Vale — repairman

**Work:** general mechanical repair, flood signals, farm implements, route fittings.  
**Relationships:** father of Neris; old friend of Mara; frequent customer/critic of Dori's improvised equipment; knows Eda Vell from older field work.  
**Before Neris leaves:** wants to keep family life ordinary and Ilyra's history contained.  
**Post-Grand-Ring:** trapped in Hush/Still-Cairn.  
**Ending:** returns and stops choosing what truth Neris is allowed to receive.

### Local reputation
Orin is trusted because he says when he does not know how to fix something.

That makes his silence about Ilyra more painful: his professional ethic is better than his family ethic.

---

## Mara Pell — retired carpenter

**Contract:** C01 The Crooked Fire Bell.  
**Work/history:** retired structural carpenter; still repairs doors, braces, carts, and anything she considers “too simple to pay a fool from Cairnspire.”  
**Relationships:** widow of Tovi Pell; longtime friend of Orin; argues constantly with Jo Ren about whether old materials are worth saving; lets children use her scrap pile if they sort it afterward.

### Before dungeon arc
Mara does not oppose state standards in principle. She opposes replacing understandable local things with sealed components nobody nearby can service.

### Post-C01
She respects Neris more if Neris can explain the chosen repair, regardless of branch.

### Post-Stillness
The fire bell's repair history changes the local moving/safety pocket.

Mara becomes one of the adults organizing people around what actually works rather than what was certified.

### Unringing
She helps convert donated regional metal into several smaller practical bells.

### Credits
Mara supervises badly aligned bell brackets while loudly refusing to call herself retired.

---

## Dori Senn — innkeeper / mechanical tinkerer

**Contract:** C04 A Lantern Without a Bell.  
**Work:** runs the road inn and repairs her own shutters, kitchen pumps, and arrival lanterns because she refuses subscription-style state service contracts.

**Relationships:** Tessa has slept in Dori's stable more often than she has paid for a room; Kest Lorn uses the inn as an unofficial road-worker meeting point; Mara thinks Dori overcomplicates every mechanism.

### Before Grand Ring
Dori is not ideological. Her rule is: if she cannot afford the official solution, she finds another one.

### Post-Stillness
The wind-driven local arrival lantern still works.

That makes the inn a natural courier rendezvous.

### Unringing
Dori's long table becomes one of Tessa's route-planning hubs.

### Credits
The inn has three different locally made arrival indicators, none matching and all functional.

---

## Jo Ren — mill keeper

**Work:** grain mill, tailrace gates, flood-channel maintenance.  
**Relationships:** childhood friend of Orin; practical rival of Mara; parent of **Eli Ren**, one of the kite children.

### Personality
Jo likes standards because predictable replacement parts let one person keep the mill running.

Jo is the local voice reminding the player that standardization solved real problems.

### Story role
- teaches the jammed mill tutorial context;
- points out the old tailbox anomaly;
- after Stillness, discovers the mill can run in short local cycles even when state timing is frozen;
- helps design Brindle's local water schedule during Unringing.

### Credits
Jo and Mara argue over a new sluice bracket while Eli ignores them and adjusts it correctly.

---

## Eli Ren — mill keeper's child

**Opening role:** kite/traversal help.  
**Relationships:** child of Jo; treats Mara's scrap pile as a treasure field; admires Neris before understanding what a bellwright actually does.

### Purpose
Eli makes Brindle's later damage personal without needing melodrama.

### Post-Stillness
Depending on local setup states, Eli may be in one of two survivor groups but is not killed by an optional branch. Branches change rescue route/experience, not “good choice saves child / bad choice kills child.”

### Unringing
Carries handwritten local timing cards between Brindle stations.

### Credits
Learns to test a cracked bell by listening twice—the opening lesson passed forward without prophecy.

---

## Hara Quill — orchard coordinator

**Work:** coordinates terrace irrigation and harvest crews.  
**Relationships:** buys mill service from Jo; distrusts Dori's improvised bookkeeping; worked with Orin during an old flood.

### Opinion of Cairnspire
Hara favors central flood warnings because one missed valley signal can kill dozens downstream.

She is therefore suspicious of romantic localism.

### Post-Stillness
Central flood timing is useless, forcing Hara to build manual observation chains with orchard workers.

### Unringing
She becomes one of the strongest proponents of **federated** local signals: not isolation, not one central master.

### Credits
Her orchard posts both local water marks and courier-shared upstream reports.

---

## Neris Vale

Brindle's residents do not initially treat Neris as a “hero.”

Neris is:
- Orin's kid;
- the apprentice who leaves tools in bad places;
- surprisingly good at hearing stress faults;
- someone people have known since infancy.

After the Grand Ring, some residents are grateful, some frightened by the Key, and a few angry that Neris's repairs fed Maelor's reference field.

The game should allow that discomfort without turning villagers into an accusatory mob.

# 5. Brindle opinion spectrum before Grand Ring

## Mara Pell
Local repair knowledge is undervalued; state replacement culture wastes understandable systems.

## Jo Ren
Common parts and timing standards are what let small settlements operate with too few specialists.

## Hara Quill
Shared warnings matter more than local pride when floods cross borders.

## Dori Senn
She uses whichever system she can afford and repair.

## Orin
Distrusts Cairnspire politics but still uses state technical references when they are useful.

## Younger residents
Generally care less about ideology and more about whether the road works.

This spectrum should survive the catastrophe. Grand Ring changes evidence, not everyone's personality overnight.

# 6. First Circuit communities

The First Circuit is not one settlement. It is a lived road network.

## Graymile
An old toll/service hamlet built around a viaduct approach.

Most residents work in:
- route maintenance;
- cart repair;
- freight staging;
- small food stalls serving crews.

The certified tunnel is famously unsafe while the unofficial bypass remains in daily use.

### Kest Lorn — route technician
**Contract:** C03 Graymile's Open Bypass.  
**Relationships:** drinks at Dori's inn; former trainee of Eda Vell; distrusts Pellon Vey's city office while privately knowing Pellon is understaffed rather than lazy.

**Post-Stillness:** whichever bypass choice occurred determines what rescue problem Kest must solve, not whether he is a good/bad person.

**Unringing:** becomes one of Tessa's infrastructure scouts.

---

## Old Viaduct Refuge
An obsolete independent bellwright station under a bridge span bypassed by modern state routing.

### Eda Vell — independent bellwright
**Work:** semi-retired field bellwright, tool rebuilder, teacher when she feels like it.  
**Relationships:** taught Kest; knew Orin as a younger field technician; corresponded with Ilyra but was never Quiet Court.

### Crucial rule
Eda must **not** know the whole conspiracy.

She suspected Ilyra was working on local-reference alternatives and disliked the secrecy around it.

### Before Grand Ring
Teaches Common Measure and seeds The Unstandard Tool.

### Post-Stillness
Her refuge becomes one of the first stable local machine pockets because its systems were never fully converted to Meridian timing.

### Unringing
Eda is living proof that alternatives existed, but she refuses to become the new central authority.

### Credits
Apprentices build visibly different Freehand Frame variants from shared principles.

---

## Willowcut Station
Small relay stop with a communal pump and covered freight yard.

### Rinn Valeo — pump steward
No relation to the Vale family.

Rinn is a useful minor recurring face who cares about whether road users maintain shared equipment.

During Stillness, the pump's manual flywheel makes the station a shelter.

Rinn helps explain why “manual backup” is not primitive nostalgia—it is resilience.

# 7. Brindle and Circuit story arc

## Phase A — ordinary world
The player sees:
- local expertise;
- state dependence;
- cheap improvisation;
- old grudges;
- people who know Neris personally.

## Phase B — after Neris leaves
Brindle does not freeze in time waiting for the player.

While Neris is in Cairnspire/regions:
- Orin remains absent;
- Mara helps cover workshop jobs;
- Jo and Hara absorb flood/irrigation responsibilities;
- Dori carries route rumors;
- some villagers resent the rising number of state inspections after the Key incident.

A return before Grand Ring should contain changed dialogue and small visual evidence of this workload.

## Phase C — Grand Ring
Brindle's local systems split into different behavior based on how tightly they were tied to central phase.

The catastrophe creates:
- frozen farm operations;
- short irregular moving pockets;
- separated families;
- demand for handwritten/manual signaling.

## Phase D — changed Brindle
Neris returns expecting home to be emotional safety.

Instead, home becomes evidence that every prior repair mattered.

Tessa arrives with the Line Skiff.

The Vale workshop becomes a seam contact point with Ilyra.

## Phase E — Unringing
Brindle's contribution is not a grand military force.

It becomes a **local coordination prototype**:
- mill water signals;
- orchard manual reports;
- courier timing cards;
- Dori's mechanical arrival indicator;
- multiple small bells instead of one master relay.

This is the smallest-scale proof that Orra can coordinate without identical timing.

## Refuges

# 9. Speech and cultural behavior

Brindle speech tends to use:
- repair metaphors;
- weather/flood timing;
- teasing familiarity rather than titles.

People often refer to objects by who last repaired them:
- “Mara's hinge”;
- “Jo's second gate”;
- “Orin's stupid bracket.”

This contrasts with Cairnspire's numbered/state-certified terminology.

The joke becomes politically meaningful later: maker attribution preserves knowledge.

---

# 10. Credits payoff

A high-completion Brindle credits sequence can show:

- Mara supervising several small local bells made from donated regional metal;
- Jo opening a sluice after checking Hara's handwritten upstream report;
- Dori's arrival shutters clacking before a courier enters frame;
- Kest arriving from Graymile with a route board;
- Eli listening twice to a cracked chime instead of immediately replacing it;
- Eda passing through, refusing an offered ceremonial title;
- the Vale workshop active again without pretending the Vale family has returned to its old shape.

The emotional point is not “Brindle restored.”

It is **Brindle continued**.
