# Regional Story Bible — Mireglass

**Status:** canonical regional narrative  
**Related:** C21–C22, Palace Under Reeds, Mirror Nail, Memory, Palinode

Mireglass is not “the spooky mirror swamp.” It is a wetland region built around **mediation, interpretation, reed agriculture, water travel, legal memory, and the ordinary human problem of people remembering the same event differently**.

Before Stillring, residents already lived with reflections that occasionally behaved strangely. Most treat this as an environmental nuisance and cultural fact, not supernatural destiny.

The region’s core argument is whether social peace requires one official version of the past—or whether people can live responsibly while admitting that records, witnesses, and memories disagree.

---

# 1. Ordinary life

People make a living through:
- reed farming and paper making;
- shallow-draft ferry transport;
- wetland food cultivation;
- translation and legal interpretation;
- document copying and treaty work;
- glass polishing and mirror repair;
- fish traps and eel harvesting;
- preservation of family and civic testimony.

Mireglass has a reputation elsewhere in Orra for producing lawyers, interpreters, and people who can argue for twelve minutes about the meaning of one sentence.

Locals find this stereotype annoying and sometimes deserved.

---

# 2. Daily rhythm

## Dawn
Boat crews mark which channels actually exist after overnight water shifts.

## Morning
Reed cutters work while fog remains low and temperatures are cooler.

## Midday
Interpretation houses, ferry exchanges, and document markets are busiest.

## Evening
Families clean water mirrors and cover some reflective surfaces—not from fear, but because reflected lamplight makes navigation dangerous.

## Night
Story circles and witness-copy sessions often happen after work because many residents treat memory as something maintained collectively rather than privately.

A common local phrase is:

> “Say what you saw. Then say where you were standing.”

---

# 3. Why the Waybell matters

Mireglass depends on shared reference for:
- ferry-route timing;
- flood and fog warnings;
- legal date/time certification;
- document dispatch;
- synchronization of diplomatic archives;
- confirming which physical route corresponds to which mapped channel.

Central standards solved real problems. A treaty dated differently in three districts can become a war.

But the state increasingly treated *agreement* as equivalent to *truth*, normalizing conflicting local accounts into one certified version.

That habit becomes catastrophic when Stillring makes discarded versions physically present.

---

# 4. Social spaces

## Reedwater Exchange
Boat landing, market, courier stop, public notice wall, and argument center.

## The Three Tables
A public interpretation house with three long worktables: claimant, respondent, witness. Locals use them for everything from contract disputes to arguments about lost goats.

## Reed-Chapel Workshop
A deconsecrated diplomatic chapel converted into a repair/refuge space.

## The Lantern Ferry
Evening ferry route known for reflected lantern paths that appear slightly offset from the real boats.

## Palace Under Reeds approach
An old diplomatic estate partly abandoned because maintaining one official version of every renovation became absurdly expensive.

# 5. Recurring cast

## Senae Pell — former court interpreter

**Contract:** C22 Witness at Reedwater.  
**Work:** independent interpretation, document annotation, witness preparation.  
**Relationships:** longtime colleague/rival of magistrate Oren Tal; friend of Sol Marr; hires ferry pilot Neve Or during flood season.

### Before Neris
Senae left formal court work after watching judges treat “certified translation” as proof that alternate readings were dishonest.

She still believes shared legal procedure matters.

### Post-Stillness
Becomes one of the few people capable of helping residents distinguish a present person from a Hush impression of that person without simply declaring one false.

### Unringing
Designs public testimony records that preserve source, uncertainty, and disagreement.

### Credits
Runs the Three Tables with students who annotate rather than overwrite disputed testimony.

---

## Iven Marr — reed farmer / householder

**Contract:** C21 The House That Remembers a Door.  
**Work:** reed farming, seasonal boat repair.  
**Relationships:** spouse of Sol Marr; cousin of ferry pilot Neve Or; uncomfortable around Senae because she helped during the family’s bereavement proceedings.

### Before the region crisis
Iven is practical and wants their home to stop behaving strangely.

### C21
The echo-door into a demolished child’s room tempts Iven toward permanent preservation.

The important rule: Iven is not “the grieving irrational one.” The room contains useful and painful memory, and wanting access to it is understandable.

### Post-resolution
Whatever memorial form the couple chooses, Iven’s later dialogue reflects the decision without turning it into a morality score.

### Unringing
Helps mark safe civic memory spaces because personal experience taught him that neither permanent restoration nor total erasure is sufficient.

---

## Sol Marr — paper maker / civic copyist

**Contract:** C21.  
**Work:** produces reed paper, copies household and civic records.  
**Relationships:** spouse of Iven; friend of Senae; supplier to Oren Tal’s court office; teaches apprentice Jessa Moor.

### Before Neris
Sol is more frightened by the echo-room than Iven and wants the house to be physically present-focused.

### Nuance
Sol is not “the person who understands the theme.” Sol sometimes destroys old drafts too quickly because unfinished records feel dangerous.

### Post-Stillness
Uses paper markers to distinguish physical routes from reflection-only routes.

### Credits
Produces a family book with blank pages intentionally left for future entries.

---

## Oren Tal — district magistrate

**Work:** contract and property adjudication.  
**Relationships:** professional rival of Senae; depends on Sol’s copy work; uncle to young clerk Jessa Moor.

### Before Grand Ring
Oren strongly supports a single certified record because he has seen families lose homes when competing deeds remain unresolved.

This is a legitimate argument.

### Post-Stillness
His court becomes unusable because physically present alternate records all claim authority.

### Change
Oren initially tries to rank echoes into one legal hierarchy and makes the local instability worse.

After C22 and the Memory campaign, he adopts a system where rulings can be final for practical purposes while the historical record remains explicitly disputed.

### Credits
Signs decisions that include dissent and provenance instead of silently replacing earlier files.

---

## Neve Or — ferry pilot

**Work:** shallow-channel ferry service.  
**Relationships:** cousin of Iven; works for Senae during investigations; friendly rivalry with mapmaker Daro Keel.

### Before crisis
Neve treats strange reflections as practical route hazards. She has names for common ones and thinks outsiders are too dramatic about them.

### Memorable ordinary beat
She lets children toss reed rings at reflected marker poles and laughs when they miss the real pole entirely.

### Post-Stillness
Becomes essential because only experienced pilots can tell whether a channel is reflected, remembered, or actually navigable.

### Unringing
Leads safe seam-ferry protocols.

---

## Daro Keel — mapmaker / survey copyist

**Work:** wetland route maps and seasonal corrections.  
**Relationships:** rivalry with Neve; provides maps to Oren; buys paper from Sol.

### Opinion
Daro likes central map standards because shared symbols save lives.

He hates central maps that pretend channels do not migrate.

### Post-Stillness
Starts publishing layered maps showing “physical now,” “recorded before,” and “Hush-present” without pretending they are one thing.

### Credits
Sells maps with removable transparent overlays rather than one corrected master sheet.

---

## Jessa Moor — apprentice clerk

**Work:** copies hearing notes, carries files, studies interpretation.  
**Relationships:** niece/student of Oren; student of Sol; fascinated by Senae’s annotated methods.

### Story role
Jessa is young enough to be caught between institutional habits rather than already committed to a side.

### Post-Stillness
Keeps a handwritten list of which people have physically moved because official attendance records cannot update reliably.

### Credits
Teaches children the “where were you standing?” witness exercise as a game.

---

## Tovin Marr — innkeeper / Iven’s older sibling

**Work:** runs Reedwater lodging house and eel stew kitchen.  
**Relationships:** older sibling of Iven; thinks Oren is useful but exhausting; gives Neve free meals in bad weather.

### Story role
Provides humor and normalcy.

Tovin refuses to discuss philosophy while serving dinner and routinely ends arguments by placing bowls in front of people.

### Post-Stillness
The inn becomes a meeting point because its kitchen has few reflective surfaces and a manually vented stove.

---

# 6. Internal opinion spectrum

- **Oren:** practical society sometimes needs one enforceable decision.
- **Senae:** one enforceable decision does not require pretending one historical account is absolute truth.
- **Sol:** memory can become an unhealthy physical demand.
- **Iven:** loss can make preservation feel humane rather than oppressive.
- **Daro:** shared notation is good; frozen maps are dangerous.
- **Neve:** lived local knowledge can outperform official diagrams without making diagrams useless.

Mireglass is not “contradiction is always good.” Some conflicts must still be resolved enough for people to live.

---

# 7. Before anything bad happens — worth visiting test

Required pre-catastrophe texture:
- ride the Lantern Ferry at dusk;
- visit the Three Tables during a ridiculous minor property dispute;
- watch reed paper being made and pressed;
- eat Tovin’s aggressively salty eel stew;
- see children play the reflection-ring game with Neve;
- help Daro update a channel marker after the water moves overnight;
- witness a couple disagree politely about an event neither considers politically important.

The player must experience Mireglass reflections as **beautiful, funny, and ordinary** before they become horrifying.

---

# 8. Grand Ring impact

Stillring turns familiar reflection weirdness into physical contradiction.

Consequences:
- some residents appear in multiple local states;
- ferry maps disagree with water itself;
- property boundaries become physically ambiguous;
- old rooms return;
- people encounter impressions of relatives at ages they remember rather than ages they are;
- court procedure collapses because every certified copy can become physically present.

The horror is intimate, not merely surreal geometry.

A woman sees her late father sweeping a shop that was demolished twenty years ago.

A child sees two versions of the same front door and does not know which one leads home.

---

# 9. Palace Under Reeds consequence

The Mirror Widow campaign should not teach that one shared memory solves every disagreement.

It demonstrates one specific case: incompatible selves can stop fighting for singular authority when they recognize a shared event that none fully owns.

The region afterward still has legal disputes, grief, and conflicting accounts.

Memory is a tool for living, not a universal answer.

---

# 10. Unringing project — Marked Seams Compact

Mireglass creates a civic system for safe Hush contact:
- publicly marked seam zones;
- witness/provenance records;
- time-limited Mirror Nail-style service practices;
- ferry routes that distinguish physical and echo channels;
- legal decisions that can be operationally final without rewriting the historical record.

This is controversial.

Some residents want every seam closed permanently.

Others want free access to memories.

The adopted compromise remains politically unstable in the credits.

---

# 11. Recurrence / recognition beats

Required familiarity:
- Neve appears during an ordinary ferry ride, later emergency navigation, and Unringing route work;
- Iven and Sol are seen at home before C21 matters emotionally;
- Oren is first encountered handling something mundane before his philosophy becomes important;
- Tovin’s inn shifts from comic social hub to refuge and back toward ordinary life;
- Jessa’s notes visibly change over multiple visits.

---

# 12. Credits payoff

High-completion credits may show:
- Neve teaching seam-ferry markings to visiting pilots;
- Iven and Sol using their chosen memorial arrangement without the game declaring it ideal;
- Senae and Oren arguing over wording in a public ruling, then both signing it;
- Daro producing layered route maps;
- Jessa running a witness game for children;
- Tovin serving food while people continue debating around him;
- one safe civic seam being used quietly for mourning rather than spectacle.

Mireglass’s ending is not “all truths are equal.”

It is a community learning that **a workable present does not require lying about an uncertain past**.
