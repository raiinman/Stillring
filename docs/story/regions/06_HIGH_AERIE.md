# Regional Story Bible — High Aerie

**Status:** canonical regional narrative  
**Related:** C18–C20, Monastery of Updraft, Tempest Belfry, Vane Cloak, Motion, Caldrin

High Aerie is not “the sky region.” It is a chain of suspended mountain settlements whose ordinary life depends on **rigging, wind, load, weather judgment, and the preservation of records**.

Its deepest local argument is not knowledge versus ignorance. It is about what preservation means when weight, space, time, and attention are finite.

Some people believe a central archive must decide what survives. Others believe every copy should remain visible even when they disagree. Most residents simply want the bridges not to fall while scholars argue.

---

# 1. Ordinary life

People make a living through:
- suspension-rig maintenance;
- weather observation;
- archive copying and binding;
- rope, sailcloth, and vane fabrication;
- cliff agriculture in sheltered terraces;
- messenger work between hanging settlements;
- salvage from abandoned upper platforms;
- teaching and certification of load crews.

The monasteries are not secluded temples full of mystics. They are civic institutions: archive, school, weather station, rigging authority, dormitory, workshop, kitchen, and emergency shelter tangled together.

---

# 2. Daily rhythm

## Before dawn
Weather crews release weighted ribbons and listening vanes before most people wake.

## Morning
Load crews inspect chains and anchor plates while archivists move valuable records away from damp windward walls.

## Midday
Cargo and students cross the major bridges while wind is most predictable.

## Evening
Meals happen in long common rooms because heat is expensive and isolated kitchens are dangerous in high wind.

## Night
Copyists work during calmer air. Children are taught to identify dangerous chain tones before they are taught formal archive history.

A local saying is:

> “If the bridge sings once, listen. If it sings twice, get off it.”

---

# 3. Why the Waybell matters

High Aerie uses regional timing to coordinate:
- suspension-load changes;
- scheduled bridge closures;
- weather observations;
- warning bells during sudden pressure drops;
- record transfers between towers;
- cargo movement through exposed crossings.

Central standardization genuinely reduced accidents by preventing one monastery from opening a span while another was shifting its counterweights.

It also allowed one central archive to become the official source for historical truth.

The physical and archival systems share the same institutional habit: **one master reference**.

---

# 4. Social spaces

## Chainhouse Seven
A working rigging shop, refuge, classroom, and argument pit.

People eat standing up because every flat surface becomes a workbench.

## Lower Archive Hall
Public-facing archive and reading room. Citizens bring family records, work claims, accident reports, and disputed copies here.

## The Wind Kitchen
Communal kitchen sheltered behind old stone baffles. It is where apprentices, riggers, monks, and visitors mix without formal rank.

## The Hanging Court
A broad suspended square used for announcements, rope-market stalls, and public disciplinary hearings.

## Storm Blind
Tiny upper weather shelter reached only by experienced crews. It becomes a refuge after Stillness and later a Vane service station.

# 5. Recurring cast

## Brother Caldrin — archivist / monk-engineer

**Critical role:** main cast.  
**Work:** archive comparison, historical machinery study, load-record interpretation.  
**Relationships:** teacher to Pera Dinn; professional tension with Prior Selen Voss; relies on Sal Em for practical rigging judgments; becomes close with Tessa through courier-copy work.

### Before Neris
Caldrin suspects master records have been quietly normalized for generations but lacks enough provenance to accuse the institution that trained him.

### Pre-Grand Ring
He appears in Cairnspire, notices Ilyra’s shorthand, and may send a disputed record copy through Tessa.

### Post-Stillness
Returns to find the monastery physically and institutionally frozen around a hierarchy of what “must be saved first.”

### Unringing
Creates a public provenance system: contradictory copies remain together with their sources attached.

### Credits
Runs an open reading room where corrections are written in margins rather than silently replacing earlier text.

---

## Sal Em — rigging master

**Contract:** C19 Chain Nine Sings.  
**Work:** suspension inspection and emergency load planning.  
**Relationships:** older friend of Caldrin; trainer of Pera; constantly argues with Prior Selen over budget; respects Bellwarden load tables more than she admits.

### Before Neris
Sal believes archive people routinely forget buildings are held up by things that rust.

### Opinion
Central standards are useful when they specify safe load ranges. They become dangerous when administrators treat those ranges as proof local observation is unnecessary.

### Post-Stillness
Sal becomes de facto physical operations lead because formal superiors are trapped in inaccessible frozen sections.

### Unringing
Designs the distributed bridge-opening schedule using local wind observations rather than Meridian time.

### Credits
Still refuses the title “master coordinator” because she says titles add weight without increasing chain strength.

---

## Pera Dinn — novice rigger / archive student

**Contract:** C20 The Novice Who Cut the Bellrope.  
**Work:** mixed novice duties; load checks, copy runs, kitchen work.  
**Relationships:** student of Caldrin; terrified of disappointing Sal; younger sibling to dormitory caretaker Ila Dinn.

### Before Grand Ring
Pera is bright, nervous, and overly obedient to written procedure.

### Grand Ring
Cuts a ceremonial bellrope when its forced oscillation threatens a dormitory route.

Pera later believes this act caused part of the catastrophe because official emergency doctrine says never sever a synchronization line.

### Post-Motion
Evidence shows the cut saved lives.

The important change is not “Pera was secretly right.” It is that Pera learns procedure can be overridden responsibly when reality no longer matches the procedure’s assumptions.

### Credits
Teaches apprentices how to document *why* they deviated from procedure.

---

## Prior Selen Voss — senior archive administrator

**Work:** institutional leadership, master-copy authority, funding allocation.  
**Relationships:** mentor-turned-opponent to Caldrin; depends on Sal’s crews; protective of the monastery’s reputation; privately fond of Pera.

### Before Grand Ring
Selen genuinely believes one certified historical record prevents political conflict and protects vulnerable claims from endless dispute.

He is not a censorship cartoon.

### Post-Stillness
Orders canonical archive rooms prioritized during rescue because property and legal claims across Orra depend on them.

That choice is defensible and still harms people whose histories exist only in marginal copies.

### After Motion
Selen refuses Caldrin’s first demand to open everything.

Later, after seeing two contradictory copies jointly prevent a dangerous structural mistake, he accepts a provenance model he does not fully like.

### Credits
Works in the same open archive system and writes the most irritatingly detailed source notes in Orra.

---

## Ila Dinn — dormitory caretaker / seamstress

**Work:** housing, sailcloth repair, clothing, dormitory stores.  
**Relationships:** Pera’s older sibling; supplies Vane cloth to Sal; feeds Caldrin when he forgets meals.

### Story role
Makes High Aerie about residents, not only archives and bridges.

### Post-Stillness
Organizes people by which dormitory sections enter motion together and marks safe windows with dyed cloth strips.

### Unringing
Her color-coded local signals become part of the regional manual coordination system.

### Credits
Sells practical wind-cloaks based on maintenance sailcloth, annoyed that visitors call them fashionable.

---

## Jaro Pell — weather observer

**Work:** pressure and wind logging at Storm Blind.  
**Relationships:** married to cook Mevi Pell; argues with Sal about trusting forecasts; sends raw weather copies to Cairnfall surveyors.

### Opinion
Jaro values regional data-sharing and thinks anti-central rhetoric can become an excuse for refusing useful outside information.

### Post-Stillness
Discovers the storm is carrying pressure released from phase-locked infrastructure, not ordinary weather.

### Unringing
Builds a shared weather report that publishes uncertainty instead of one “correct” forecast.

---

## Mevi Pell — Wind Kitchen cook

**Work:** communal kitchen, supply accounting, apprentice gossip.  
**Relationships:** spouse of Jaro; aunt-like presence to Pera; longstanding grudge against Selen for cutting food-store space to expand archives.

### Story role
Provides ordinary warmth and humor before the region becomes dangerous.

### Memorable beat
On Neris’s first safe visit, Mevi insists outsiders eat before crossing the upper spans because “heroics weigh less when you’re not fainting.”

### Post-Stillness
The kitchen becomes a mobile food-distribution hub whenever access bridges move.

### Credits
A bigger kitchen occupies space formerly assigned to redundant ceremonial storage.

---

# 6. Internal opinion spectrum

- **Selen:** one master record protects continuity and legal certainty.
- **Caldrin:** provenance and contradiction are safer than silent normalization.
- **Sal:** physical reality outranks paperwork, but shared load standards save lives.
- **Jaro:** distributed systems still need shared data.
- **Pera:** begins by trusting procedure more than judgment; learns responsible deviation.
- **Mevi:** institutions should be judged partly by whether ordinary people can eat and sleep safely inside them.

No one gets to reduce the region to “archives bad.”

---

# 7. Before anything bad happens — worth visiting test

High Aerie must be enjoyable before catastrophe.

Required pre-crisis texture:
- ride a cargo lift while workers argue about someone overloading it with turnips;
- eat in the Wind Kitchen;
- watch apprentices practice emergency rope signals badly;
- hear Storm Blind weather reports shouted down the chain line;
- see children daring one another to touch a humming anchor plate;
- browse contradictory old travel sketches that are funny rather than ominous;
- cross one beautiful suspended span under normal wind with no enemies or crisis.

The player must have a memory of **wind as freedom** before Stillness removes it.

---

# 8. Grand Ring impact

The first horror is silence: wind stops.

Then gravity asserts itself.

Some spans begin falling before phase-lock catches them. Others freeze under impossible loads.

Social consequences:
- senior administrators become physically inaccessible;
- practical riggers gain authority;
- food distribution becomes harder than archive protection;
- apprentices must make decisions without permission;
- people learn which ceremonial systems were actually load-bearing and which were symbolic.

The changed region should feel like familiar architecture caught between frames of motion, not a new sky dungeon.

---

# 9. Regional campaign consequence

## Monastery of Updraft
Restoring local airflow lets people move safely but also begins releasing stored strain.

## Tempest Belfry
The storm is the cost of restarting change, not a villain-created punishment.

## Saint Varo
The region’s heroic myth breaks because both cowardice and courage were real.

This must not conclude “heroes are fake.”

The conclusion is that people remain responsible for what they did even when one moment does not define the whole person.

---

# 10. Unringing project — Open Load Accord

High Aerie establishes a regional system combining:
- local wind stations;
- chain-load reports;
- manual bridge windows;
- independent archive copies;
- courier distribution of both data and provenance.

The system deliberately has no single master bell.

It also deliberately has **shared protocols**.

High Aerie’s lesson complicates Stillring’s final politics: decentralization without compatible reporting would be lethal here.

---

# 11. Recurrence / recognition beats

The player should encounter these faces more than once:
- Pera before the crisis, after cutting the rope, and in the Unringing;
- Sal before Vane acquisition, during emergency load work, and later on a repaired span;
- Mevi at ordinary meal, crisis rationing, and credits;
- Selen before his authority is challenged and after he reluctantly adopts provenance.

A credits cameo is not enough.

---

# 12. Credits payoff

High-completion credits can show:
- Pera teaching a deviation-report exercise;
- Sal and Selen arguing over a bridge-opening protocol while both using the same published load sheet;
- Caldrin shelving two contradictory accounts side by side;
- Mevi reclaiming archive floor space for kitchen storage;
- Jaro sending raw weather data to Cairnfall without a correction stamp;
- wind moving through the region continuously rather than in synchronized ceremonial pulses.

High Aerie’s ending is not “truth defeats authority.”

It is a community learning that **preservation only matters if life can keep moving around what is preserved**.
