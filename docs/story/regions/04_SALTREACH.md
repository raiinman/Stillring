# Regional Story Bible — Saltreach

**Status:** canonical regional narrative  
**Related:** C10–C13, Tide Foundry, Glasslung Reed, Tessa's Quiet Court freight history

Saltreach is built around the fact that **the coast does not stay where maps put it**.

Channels migrate. Sandbars move. Fog erases landmarks. Low tide reveals streets of old industrial foundations and high tide covers things that looked permanent six hours earlier.

Its people are not mystical sea folk. They are navigators, repair crews, fishers, shipwrights, pressure engineers, dock workers, traders, and families whose practical knowledge has always included change.

The regional argument is whether safety comes from one shared navigation reference or from keeping many local ways of reading a coast that never stops changing.

---

# 1. Ordinary life

Saltreach is a chain of harbor settlements rather than one city.

The main harbor surrounds the old Tide Foundry, now partly submerged and repurposed across generations.

People make a living through:
- fishing and shell harvest;
- cargo lightering between deep ships and shallow docks;
- ship repair;
- fog signaling;
- pressure-pump maintenance;
- salt processing;
- salvage from old industrial foundations;
- coastal pilot work.

Children learn tide marks and fog calls before they learn formal route notation.

---

# 2. Daily rhythm

Saltreach days are organized by **water condition**, not only clock time.

## Falling tide
- dry-dock work accelerates;
- salvage crews enter exposed foundations;
- market carts move onto temporary lower streets.

## Low water
- pressure housings and navigation bells are serviced;
- children collect shellfish around pilings;
- ferries use different approach lanes.

## Rising tide
- lower shops pack away movable goods;
- pilots reposition marker floats;
- fog towers change signal sets.

## High water
- larger boats move close to central docks;
- taverns and repair sheds fill with crews waiting for weather windows.

Central Meridian timing helped coordinate this complexity across many harbors, but locals still talk in physical states:

> “two boards under the north mark”

often matters more than:

> “14:30.”

---

# 3. Why the Waybell matters

Saltreach's underwater bells propagate through fog and water to coordinate:
- channel warnings;
- harbor entries;
- low-visibility fleet movement;
- pressure-gate timing;
- storm evacuation.

The shared system saved many lives.

Its weakness is that calibration assumes charted coastline/reference geometry that can become obsolete while still reporting perfect timing.

C10's lying fog tower is the cleanest early example: **correct signal, wrong world model**.

# 4. Social spaces

## Fog Tower Steps
A meeting point for pilots waiting on visibility.

Vessa Mor treats the steps like her porch and has opinions about everybody who passes.

## Lowwater Market
Exists only during sufficiently low tide on broad stone foundations.

Vendors use wheeled stalls because staying too long is an understood stupidity.

This makes the market a natural visual symbol of a community comfortable with temporary space.

## Dry Dock Three
Pell Aro's workplace and neighborhood social center.

Workers eat on hull braces, argue over repairs, and paint personal marks where state inspectors cannot see them from the main walkway.

## The Split Mast
Harbor kitchen/tavern used by captains, fishers, couriers, and pressure crews.

Tessa once arranged questionable freight there.

Her Act One discomfort should be visible before she confesses why.

## Memorial Jetty
Families hang small non-resonant tags for people lost at sea.

The tags are intentionally silent because not every memory needs to call back.

C12's Kelp Bell Funeral should connect to this custom.

# 5. Recurring cast

## Vessa Mor — retired signal keeper

**Contract:** C10 The Fog Tower That Lies.  
**Work:** nominally retired; still maintains local signal paint and teaches pilots.  
**Relationships:** aunt-like mentor to Captain Imi Sorn; old professional antagonist of harbor administrator Dalen Or; knows Tessa from courier years; trades insult-heavy weather reports with Pell Aro.

### Before Neris
Vessa has argued for years that the state calibration no longer matches the coastline.

Her frustration is not anti-instrument.

She owns more measuring equipment than most Bellwarden offices.

### C10
Neris proves the tower is mechanically healthy and geographically wrong.

### Setup payoff
If local fog code is repainted before Grand Ring, crews can read it during Stillness because it is physical/spatial rather than centrally timed.

### Post-Stillness
Vessa becomes a manual harbor-information hub.

### Unringing
Trains younger pilots to publish **what they observed** separately from **what route they recommend**.

### Credits
Corrects a trainee's signal, then quietly accepts the trainee's better local observation.

---

## Pell Aro — shipwright

**Contract:** C11 Dry Dock at Low Water.  
**Work:** hull repair and pressure-dock maintenance.  
**Relationships:** Vessa's friend/rival; employs Marao relatives; owes Captain Rell Dava for a salvaged crane; dislikes Imi's willingness to run old hulls hard.

### Core position
Pell likes state standards for fasteners, pressure fittings, and emergency hardware because compatible parts matter at sea.

He hates route authorities pretending every harbor geometry is interchangeable.

### Post-C11
Dry Dock Three becomes a service node that can operate under local pressure state.

### Post-Stillness
Pell keeps the dock working during brief water-motion windows by using physical gauges rather than central schedule.

### Unringing
Builds modular manual signal/pressure kits for smaller harbors.

### Credits
A dock wall displays components from multiple regions with their makers/sources labeled.

---

## Tali Marao — net maker / memorial organizer

**Contract:** C12 Kelp Bell Funeral.  
**Work:** makes weighted fishing nets and maintains family memorial tags.  
**Relationships:** sibling of the late **Eris Marao**, whose navigation bell is the subject of C12; cousin works for Pell; trusts Vessa more than the harbor authority.

### Before C12
Tali wants the physical bell recovered because officials have treated Eris's final rescue as an evidence number rather than family history.

The authority wants it left underwater because its cylinder records accident timing.

### Resolution rule
The quest must allow memory/evidence to be preserved without making possession of the physical bell the only respectful outcome.

### Post-Stillness
Tali organizes family-location boards at Memorial Jetty and helps identify crews caught in separated moving pockets.

### Unringing
Turns the silent memorial-tag system into a source of crew/family provenance for Tessa's route network.

### Credits
A recovered/recorded version of Eris's signal is archived beside conflicting official accident data while the memorial tag remains simple and silent.

---

## Captain Rell Dava — cargo captain

**Contract:** C13 Two Captains, One Tide.  
**Work:** commands a heavy cargo vessel that keeps several inland communities supplied.  
**Relationships:** owes Pell Aro for emergency repairs; publicly clashes with Captain Imi Sorn; respects Vessa's pilot knowledge while disliking her contempt for schedules.

### Before Grand Ring
Rell favors central timing strongly.

A cargo ship cannot improvise its way through every harbor while carrying perishables and passengers.

He has lost crew to local captains ignoring common rules.

### Post-Stillness
His ship becomes trapped near one moving pocket with supplies another pocket desperately needs.

### C13
Rell has to coordinate with Imi without either captain's clock becoming master.

### Unringing
Becomes a proponent of shared **window publishing** rather than universal timing.

### Credits
His cargo board lists several acceptable route windows instead of one scheduled departure.

---

## Captain Imi Sorn — fishing/rescue captain

**Contract:** C13 co-owner.  
**Work:** small fast boat used for fishing, rescue, and shallow pilot work.  
**Relationships:** trained under Vessa; dislikes Rell's big-ship privilege; has Tessa's respect because Imi once carried stranded couriers for free.

### Before Grand Ring
Imi thinks central schedules privilege deep-draft commercial traffic and erase local knowledge.

She is also willing to take risks that other people then have to rescue her from.

### Post-Stillness
Her shallow boat can move through routes unavailable to Rell, but lacks supply capacity.

### C13
The two captains must create a cooperative schedule from unequal capabilities.

### Credits
Imi and Rell still argue, which is the point. Cooperation does not require personality convergence.

---

## Dalen Or — harbor administrator

**Work:** state harbor office, inspection coordination, accident evidence retention.  
**Relationships:** long feud with Vessa; relies on Rell for bulk shipping; thinks Imi is dangerously informal; knows Tessa's old forged-manifest reputation.

### Before Neris
Dalen has genuine reasons to preserve common navigation standards and accident evidence.

He is also politically invested in treating local changes as deviations rather than evidence the chart is obsolete.

### Tessa history
Dalen was a junior customs officer when Tessa moved Quiet Court freight.

He suspected manifest fraud but never discovered the contents.

His presence makes Tessa visibly uncomfortable in the Split Mast before the manifest reveal.

### Post-Stillness
State authority cannot make the tide move.

Dalen becomes useful by opening sealed emergency stores and releasing evidence copies rather than guarding procedure.

### Unringing
Serves as a recorder/mediator for published harbor windows, not their commander.

---

## Sori Keff — pressurewright

**Work:** maintains cisterns, pressure gates, navigation-bell housings.  
**Relationships:** works with Pell; thinks Vessa's paint-code nostalgia can become dangerous if crews stop maintaining pressure systems; one of Tessa's old freight contacts.

### Story purpose
Sori is the practical expert who helps the player understand Tide Foundry without becoming a dungeon-exposition NPC.

He recognizes some Quiet Court hardware as modified civilian pressure equipment but cannot explain the project.

### Post-Leviathan
Helps decide whether the cistern bypass remains independent or returns to central regulation (Setup S07).

### Post-Stillness
The bypass outcome determines which refuge/dock Sori can keep alive first.

### Unringing
Designs local pressure bells that advertise actual chamber condition rather than waiting for one global cycle.

# 6. Tessa and Saltreach

Saltreach is where Tessa's past stops being flavor.

## Before the reveal
The player should notice:
- Tessa knows old customs routes too well;
- Vessa calls her by an older courier nickname Tessa dislikes;
- Dalen recognizes her and becomes formal;
- Tessa avoids the Split Mast's rear freight room.

No one says “Quiet Court” yet.

## Manifest scene
The surviving freight record links:
- forged papers;
- Tessa's route signature;
- Maelor's seal;
- Ilyra's field code.

Tessa admits deliberate ignorance.

## After the confession
Local NPCs should not all condemn or forgive her.

- Vessa: knew Tessa ran questionable freight and thinks young couriers were exploited, but does not absolve her.
- Dalen: points out forged manifests can kill people regardless of cargo.
- Sori: remembers being paid not to inspect one pressure crate and has his own culpability.
- Pell: cares more about what Tessa does now than public shame.

This makes the issue social rather than a single companion confession bubble.

# 7. Region story progression

## Arrival
Saltreach appears chaotic to Neris because local timing is tide-based and route markers seem inconsistent.

The player gradually discovers the inconsistency contains information.

## Pre-dungeon
C10 proves the tower is wrong because the **model** is stale, not because the hardware or local keeper failed.

## Tide Foundry
The player sees old civic industry layered beneath current life.

Glasslung Reed emerges from pressure-work practice rather than being an arbitrary water key.

The Nine-Lung Leviathan encounter reinforces the distinction between **keeping something alive** and **giving it agency/health**.

## Post-dungeon
Tessa's manifest changes the social tone.

The region is no longer only about coast/navigation; it is connected to Quiet Court logistics.

## Grand Ring
Saltreach becomes terrifying because water does not obey a simple frozen-world rule.

Use authored states:
- some surface waves freeze while deep pressure continues;
- boats can be trapped against immobile water shapes;
- central signal bells repeat one phase;
- manually painted markers remain legible;
- pressure systems become isolated pockets of movement.

## Changed-world return
Vessa, Pell, Sori, Rell, Imi, Tali, and Dalen are each solving different immediate problems.

No single harbor chief gives Neris one “save Saltreach” quest.

## Unringing
Community project: **Many-Tide Harbor Accord**.

Components:
- physical tide/water observations;
- Vessa's local fog language;
- Sori's chamber-state signals;
- Rell/Imi route-window negotiation;
- Dalen's evidence/notice infrastructure;
- Tessa's cross-region courier exchange.

The result is a harbor that coordinates by publishing conditions/windows, not pretending one clock can make the coast stay still.

# 8. Setup/payoff continuity

## Manual fog code
If repainted, the physical code helps crews reach a particular shelter during the first Stillness crisis.

If not, a different rescue route is required later.

## Leviathan pressure release
Independent bypass creates a moving deep-inlet refuge.

Reconnected bypass protects another dock from destructive pressure.

Both branches create meaningful later work.

# 9. Local rituals, food, material culture

## Food
- smoked eel/fish over sharp salt greens;
- fried shell cakes sold only at lowwater market;
- thick sea-onion soup on fog days;
- fermented kelp paste used as both condiment and frequent subject of inland jokes.

## Rituals
### Last Clear Call
Before a crew leaves in heavy fog, another person repeats the vessel name and intended route from shore.

It is not superstition. It creates a human witness when instruments fail.

The practice becomes emotionally important after Grand Ring.

### Memorial tags
Silent tags for the dead/lost hang at the jetty rather than bells that continually call the past back.

This quietly foreshadows Memory/Ending.

## Speech
Saltreach people distinguish:
- what a chart says;
- what water is doing;
- what a captain plans.

They often phrase uncertainty directly:

> “Chart says clear. Water says maybe. I say wait ten.”

# 10. Credits payoff

High-completion images:
- Vessa training pilots using both physical marks and shared regional data;
- Pell's dry dock using locally published pressure windows;
- Tali adding Eris's evidence record to a public archive while leaving a silent memorial tag at the jetty;
- Rell and Imi's boards showing different ship windows under one cooperative notice;
- Dalen posting uncertainty/source instead of one official route time;
- Sori testing an independent cistern signal;
- Tessa's courier skiff crossing the background using a route Saltreach helped establish.

Saltreach's ending is not freedom from coordination.

It is coordination that admits **the coast is allowed to move**.
