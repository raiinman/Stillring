# Project Stillring — Quiet Moments and Travel Beats

**Status:** canonical narrative texture contract  
**Related:** Issue #19, `13_EMOTIONAL_PACING_MAP.md`

Stillring's story cannot become more alive merely by adding more plot.

This document protects **ordinary time**: meals, travel, jokes, bad repairs, awkward silence, work rhythms, small celebrations, and scenes that do not exist to reveal a conspiracy.

Some of these are mandatory. Some are ambient. Some are optional and reward nothing.

The absence of a reward is sometimes the point.

---

# Rules

1. Every major region gets at least two memorable non-crisis interactions.
2. Every major cast relationship gets scenes where neither person is delivering a plot fact.
3. Travel after a dungeon should carry consequences, rumor, relationship texture, or simple beauty.
4. Do not turn every quiet interaction into a collectible quest.
5. Humor comes from people and work, not Marvel-style tension-deflating quips during grief.
6. Silence is allowed.
7. The player may choose to linger.

---

# Opening / Brindle

## Orin's bad chair
Orin has repaired one workshop chair three times. One leg remains shorter.

Neris knows exactly which folded scrap of leather goes underneath it.

No quest.

Later Still-Cairn can preserve a perfect version of the chair. Neris notices it feels wrong.

## First Ring food table
Festival food is set out before the ceremony.

Players can sample small local foods and hear villagers argue over who made the worst batch.

After Grand Ring, the same table exists under frozen rain.

## Tessa's practice-blade complaint
Tessa insists Orin weights the practice blades unfairly.

Orin says she loses because she talks while swinging.

She immediately talks while swinging.

# Road to Cairnspire

## Mile-marker guessing game
Tessa asks Neris to guess how far a route marker is by wear, moss, and bell timing.

Neris keeps trying to calculate it precisely.

Tessa cheats because she knows the route.

## First camp
If the trip reaches evening, the player can sit while Tessa repairs a boot sole and complains about courier food.

No lore dump.

---

# Cairnspire

## Queue commerce
People in the registration queue trade food, spare parts, rumors, and places in line.

A child offers to sell Neris a clearly fake “official Meridian tuning fork.”

## Fourth Landing glimpse
Before Glasslung access, the player hears voices/laughter through an inaccessible lift stop.

Jeren Coil shouts through the shaft that yes, there are people there and no, the official map is lying.

## Maelor's harmless competence
During one return visit after Rootmere, Maelor notices Neris's shoulder strap is damaging the Cantor Key case.

He fixes the strap while talking about nothing important for a moment.

He tells one embarrassing Ilyra workshop story that does not contain a clue.

The player needs a reason to miss the version of Maelor who could have been only a good engineer.

# Post-Rootmere travel

## Ferry return
If the player uses Lio's crossing after the dungeon, Mawhart can be seen far away in healthy woods.

Lio does not comment unless asked.

## Tessa's rope imitation
Tessa tries the Anchor Line once under supervision and immediately hates the recoil.

Neris enjoys this too much.

## Maelor relay anecdote
A personal Ilyra anecdote, not a conspiracy clue.

# Saltreach

## Before Tide Foundry
Tessa knows a cheap quay meal order without looking at the board.

One old acquaintance remembers her by a nickname she hates.

This makes her history local before the manifest becomes serious.

## After Leviathan
The harbor is quieter.

A fisherman asks whether Neris “fixed it.”

There is no clean answer.

Later, after the Leviathan's death offshore is confirmed, boats hang pressure-chamber tags on an old rail for one evening. No cutscene speech.

# Emberstep

## Shift-change meal
Sena eats while standing and keeps stealing pieces from somebody else's plate.

## C16 cup scene
Keep it small.

Sena's mentor's cracked cup matters because Sena wants to keep using it, not because a character says the crack symbolizes history.

## After Kiln
No celebration. The three-point record creates urgency.

The previous ordinary scenes are what the player loses when the route becomes a rush.

# Grand Ring aftermath / Ilyra

## Wrong breakfast
At the workbench, Ilyra attempts to recreate a food Neris loved at six.

Neris either hated it later or never liked it as much as Ilyra remembers.

The Hush version tastes slightly wrong because memory supplies texture imperfectly.

No lore revelation.

## Tool drawer
Neris puts a tool in a different drawer.

Ilyra unconsciously moves it back.

Later she notices herself doing it and stops.

## Old village question
Ilyra asks about a Brindle resident who died or moved years ago.

Neris has to tell her.

The scene is short and not scored as a dramatic reveal.

# Changed Brindle / Line Skiff

## Recognition walk
Before Tessa arrives, let the player revisit:
- the food table;
- the practice yard;
- Orin's bad chair;
- Mara's fire-bell area;
- children's route.

No objective markers on all of them.

## First Skiff fun
After the dangerous tutorial wave, give the player one stretch of open surviving road where Tessa tells Neris to stop overthinking and just lean into the turn.

Music can become energetic.

The second half needs a genuine “holy shit this is fun” beat.

# After full Mercy Window reveal

## Ilyra repairs the wrong thing
Ilyra becomes absorbed in fixing an irrelevant broken hinge at the workbench because she cannot fix the conversation.

Neris can help silently or leave.

Both lead to the same story state but different animation/dialogue acknowledgment later.

# Cairnfall

## Public sky
This region is intentionally calmer.

Allow a full observation segment where the player can point a scope at several targets and hear short reactions.

One target means nothing.

Not everything in the sky foreshadows the sequel.

## Neris/Ilyra seam-build scene
They work well together before they speak well together.

Corrections happen through gestures and tool handoffs.

At the end, one of them makes a tiny joke about the other's technique.

This is more valuable than an apology monologue.

# Still-Cairn

## Orin memory fragments
Before the confrontation, include ordinary family echoes:
- Orin repairing the bad chair;
- Ilyra and Orin arguing over money;
- young Neris asleep under the workbench;
- Orin rehearsing a confession and failing to start it.

These make the perfect preserved workshop tempting.

# Unringing

This is not a war camp.

Required ordinary-life texture:
- regional meals shared by people who previously did not work together;
- Tessa drowning in route forms;
- Rusk being told nobody needs his stamp and using it to hold paper down;
- Sena teaching someone to mark their own work;
- Caldrin getting corrected by a teenager on a source note;
- Ilyra listening more than talking;
- Orin taking a repair instruction from Neris without overriding it.

Small celebrations occur before the final confrontation.

People have earned them.

# Null Meridian

## Worker spaces
Before the final bell chamber, let Neris pass through one abandoned meal room with mugs, shift notes, and an unfinished card game.

No ghost exposition required.

The system was operated by ordinary people.

## Maelor's room
Keep it quiet and small.

The personal objects do more work than a diary explaining his grief.

# Listener sequence

Preserved alternatives should include mundane happiness:
- dinner table continuing;
- Tessa laughing at a road mistake;
- Orin's chair still wobbling;
- a regional social space crowded exactly as before.

The trap is stronger if the preserved world contains **imperfection the player loved**, not only fantasy perfection.

---

# Epilogue

## No objective urgency
The player can:
- sit at a meal;
- hear bells used for mundane reasons;
- visit Mara;
- inspect Orin's chair;
- hear Ilyra get someone's name wrong and correct herself;
- see Tessa waiting but not impatient.

The final walk begins when the player chooses it.

---

# Ambient interaction budget

Each major settlement should target:
- 6–10 short ambient resident interactions per major state;
- 2–4 recurring micro-stories across visits;
- 1–2 no-reward interactables/activities that players may remember;
- at least one local joke that changes meaning after Grand Ring;
- at least one small visual routine that returns in the credits.

These are production targets, not demands for fully voiced cinematics.
