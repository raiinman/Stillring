# XXV. CREDITS MONTAGE

During credits, every playthrough shows the core consequences of the old centralized order ending:

- children in Rootmere learn to maintain living bell-oaks without metal braces;
- Saltreach sailors repaint and staff old manual signal towers;
- Emberstep workers stamp their own names into castings;
- High Aerie copies once-forbidden records for traveling libraries;
- Mireglass families visit safe Hush seams to remember the dead;
- Cairnfall converts the observatory into a school and independent listening station;
- Cairnspire removes statues of the “founding saviors” and replaces them with a public diagram explaining how the old system actually worked;
- Maelor sits in a courtroom listening as witnesses speak.

No triumphant restoration of the old order occurs.

The world is messier and freer.

## Completion-sensitive montage rule

Optional side content can change **specific shots inside the credits**, but it cannot buy a morally superior ending or reverse the central consequences of the story.

Examples:

- a completed Rootmere community-disconnection project shows several independently maintained resonators instead of one emergency workaround;
- repaired Saltreach route infrastructure changes which harbor signals are operating;
- completed Emberstep craft quests can place the player's reconstructed non-standard implement in a public training workshop rather than a private collection;
- High Aerie testimony recovery changes which conflicting records Caldrin displays together;
- Mireglass repair/relationship quests determine which families or civic spaces appear around safe seams;
- Cairnfall optional work changes whether the school is already teaching apprentices or still being converted;
- Tessa's courier-network side work determines how many independent route lines are visible on the closing map table;
- Brindle optional First Circuit choices can visibly survive into the rebuilt village.

These are acknowledgements of what the player invested in, not a hidden requirement for the story to mean what it means.

A player who follows only the critical path still receives the complete canonical ending.

A 100% player sees a **denser accounting of relationships, repairs, and local consequences**.

# XXVII. THEMATIC CHECKLIST FOR WRITERS

Every major story addition should respect these truths:

- Grief is not a technical error to be patched out of life.
- Remembering something does not require recreating it forever.
- Institutions can preserve knowledge and still distort truth.
- Decentralization creates mess and responsibility, not utopia.
- Coordination is not the same thing as enforced sameness.
- The Hush is not “the evil dimension.”
- Maelor must remain understandable without being excused.
- Neris is not secretly royal, divine, chosen by prophecy, or a reincarnated ancient hero.
- Optional completion may deepen consequences but must not invalidate a normal player's ending.
- Side quests should reveal how people live with systems, not exist only to distribute rewards.
- Cross-state payoffs should produce authored consequences, not good/evil score increments.
- The ending should resolve the central argument through player action, not a last-minute speech alone.
