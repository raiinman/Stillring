# XXIX. CREDITS MONTAGE

Credits are not a slideshow proving every political problem is solved.

They are an accounting of **people the player knows continuing to live after the central system ends**.

Every playthrough shows the core consequences of Meridian's forced synchronization ending, but the images should use recognizable faces, workplaces, arguments, and routines rather than generic regional crowds.

## Core montage

### Brindle
Orin is repairing the same bad workshop chair again.

Neris is not there to fix it for him.

Ilyra notices the leather shim under the short leg and laughs because her Hush reconstruction always made the chair level.

Outside, different practical bells ring at slightly different times.

### Rootmere
Olo Wren reads a recovered worker name from a new maintenance board while Amsa corrects his rope placement.

Lio Fen shouts something from the ferry below.

Tavin Ro carries an official inspection stamp nobody legally requires anymore because, as he later says, it still makes a satisfying noise.

### Saltreach
Vessa Mor repaints a manual signal marker while Captains Rell Dava and Imi Sorn argue over whose crew missed a timing window.

Near the water, old pressure-chamber tags from the Nine-Lung Leviathan remain on a simple rail.

The remembrance is part of the harbor now, not a monument.

### Emberstep
Sena watches apprentices stamp their own names into new castings.

One apprentice stamps a maker mark crooked.

Sena makes them leave it visible and correct the documentation rather than grind the mistake away.

### High Aerie
Pera teaches a procedure-deviation drill.

Caldrin shelves two contradictory accounts beside each other.

Mevi Pell has reclaimed some former archive storage for the Wind Kitchen and is winning an argument with nobody important.

### Mireglass
Neve Or runs the Lantern Ferry past a marked safe seam.

Children have cautiously resumed the reflected-marker ring game in a shallow supervised pool.

Senae Pell and Oren Tal are still arguing over the wording of one public ruling.

Both eventually sign it.

### Cairnfall
Pell Dain leaves an outlier visible on a chart instead of erasing it.

Riva Sol adjusts a transparent correction dial.

Aro Venn hands a deliberately flawed training scope to another student.

Public observation night continues around them.

### Cairnspire
Fourth Landing Commons is now on an official public lift map, though Jeren Coil has crossed out the official name and written something rude beside it.

Workers open Meridian service diagrams for public inspection.

Rusk participates in the disclosure process while several citizens openly argue that he should have done it years earlier.

The montage does not tell the player which opinion is correct.

### Maelor
His public trial is underway.

He sits and listens while testimony continues.

There is no verdict shot.

History is not finished deciding what to call him.

# Completion-sensitive montage rule

Optional side content changes **specific shots and density**, not whether the ending counts as valid.

Examples:
- completed Rootmere work changes how mature the Living Accord Network appears and which named residents participate;
- Saltreach route work changes which manual signals and ferry routes are functioning;
- The Unstandard Tool can appear in a public training workshop as one of several visibly different compatible frames;
- High Aerie testimony recovery changes which disputed records are displayed together;
- Mireglass relationship quests determine which families use, avoid, or contest civic seam spaces;
- Cairnfall optional work changes whether apprentices are already teaching newer students;
- Drift Knot completion adds Tessa's safe-route maps and a recognizable courier crew;
- Brindle First Circuit choices remain visible in the rebuilt village;
- optional NPCs used by the Listener can appear again here in changed present-day states.

A 100% player sees a **denser accounting of relationships and consequences**.

A critical-path player still sees a complete ending.

No side quest purchases the only moral future.

# XXXI. THEMATIC CHECKLIST FOR WRITERS

Every major story addition should respect these truths:

- Grief is not a technical error to be patched out of life.
- Remembering something does not require recreating it forever.
- Institutions can preserve knowledge and still distort truth.
- Decentralization creates mess and responsibility, not utopia.
- Coordination is not the same thing as enforced sameness.
- Shared standards can save lives; hidden assumptions and unaccountable authority are the danger.
- Local autonomy can also fail and should be allowed to fail visibly.
- The Hush is not “the evil dimension.”
- The Listener should remain partly unknowable.
- Maelor must remain understandable without being excused.
- Ilyra must remain responsible for Mercy Window without being made responsible for Maelor's later choice.
- Neris is not secretly royal, divine, chosen by prophecy, a reincarnated ancient hero, or biologically required by the universe.
- Neris's temporary uniqueness as Cantor Key field operator is an accident of Brindle calibration and crisis timing.
- Optional completion may deepen consequences but must not invalidate a normal player's ending.
- Side content must include character-bearing and life-bearing material, not only miniature versions of the central theme.
- Cross-state payoffs should produce authored consequences, not good/evil score increments.
- Major locations need before-memories worth missing.
- New verbs should make players remember old places they can now understand differently.
- Characters should sound like workers, relatives, couriers, archivists, captains, and craftspeople—not like the Story Bible.
- The ending should resolve the central argument through player action, not a last-minute speech alone.
- Credits show a world in motion, not a perfect political settlement.
