# Project Stillring — Side Interaction Rebalance

**Status:** canonical side-story texture contract  
**Related:** Issue #19, `docs/11_QUEST_AND_COMPLETION_LEDGER.md`, regional story bibles

Stillring already has strong authored repair contracts.

The red-team audit found a different problem:

> too much optional material can still feel thin if every resident exists to restate the same central argument.

The fix is not fewer people.

It is a broader **interaction palette**.

---

# 1. Three side-content jobs

Every region should contain all three.

## A — Theme-bearing
Directly complicates questions of infrastructure, memory, standards, control, responsibility, or release.

Examples:
- C06 The Root That Isn't Sick;
- C20 The Novice Who Cut the Bellrope;
- C22 Witness at Reedwater;
- C24 No Correction Required.

## B — Character-bearing
Primarily exists to reveal a relationship, vulnerability, rivalry, grief, affection, pride, embarrassment, or personal history.

Examples / reclassification:
- C07 Widow's Ferrygate is primarily about Lio deciding whether grief is a technical argument;
- C12 Kelp Bell Funeral is primarily about a family's relationship to an object used in a final rescue;
- C16 Crack in the Foreman's Cup is primarily about Sena and her mentor;
- C21 The House That Remembers a Door is primarily about Iven/Sol grief and disagreement.

These may support the main theme without existing to explain it.

## C — Life-bearing
Exists because people play, eat, compete, gossip, flirt, waste time, show off, make ugly things, keep pets, lose tools, tell bad stories, and have preferences unrelated to saving Orra.

Many life-bearing interactions should:
- have no completion percentage;
- give no upgrade;
- change no ending;
- be memorable anyway.

---

# 2. Density rule

For the player's total **experienced optional interactions** in a major region, target roughly:

- no more than ~50% overtly theme-bearing;
- at least ~25% character-bearing;
- at least ~25% life-bearing.

This is not a spreadsheet quota for every build.

It is an anti-monotony check.

If a player spends an hour doing optional content and every conversation turns into “the central authority misunderstood local conditions,” rewrite the hour.

# 3. Brindle / First Circuit additions

## LI-B01 — Kite Argument Rematch
The child whose kite Neris retrieved insists the original rescue did not count because the wind was unfair.

Later returns with a worse kite design.

No reward.

Post-Stillness, the kite remains caught in a place the player remembers.

## LI-B02 — Orin's Chair Betting Pool
Several villagers know exactly how long Orin's repaired chair will last before wobbling again.

Small ambient dialogue changes each visit.

No quest marker.

## LI-B03 — Dori's Dinner Board
Dori Senn changes the roadside inn special after regional deliveries arrive.

Tessa has strong, often unjustified opinions about each dish.

This becomes a low-cost way to show route connectivity changing.

## LI-B04 — Shortcut War
Tessa and Kest Lorn disagree over which of two Graymile walking routes is actually shorter.

The player can physically test them.

Both have cheated their measurements in different ways.

Reward: one funny line and route familiarity.

Not completion-bearing.

# 4. Cairnspire additions

## LI-C01 — Fake Meridian Fork
A child in the registration queue sells a clearly fake “official tuning fork.”

If purchased, it rings badly and remains a useless inventory trinket / workshop decoration.

No secret upgrade later.

## LI-C02 — Queue Economy
Citizens trade food, places in line, repair tips, and rumors.

Return visits show who moved, gave up, got repaired, or started helping somebody else.

## LI-C03 — Fourth Landing Tea Tin
Fourth Landing Commons has one communal tea tin everyone claims to refill and nobody admits emptying.

Jeren Coil keeps a tally of accusations.

## LI-C04 — Rusk's Stamp
Before Grand Ring, Rusk treats his official stamp with absurd care.

During Unringing, someone tells him nobody needs it anymore.

He uses it as a paperweight.

No speech.

# 5. Rootmere additions

## LI-R01 — Sour Fruit Test
Nemi gives outsiders different orchard fruit without warning how sour each one is.

Olo keeps an informal score of reactions.

Neris can eventually learn which one Tessa refuses to eat.

## LI-R02 — Ferry News
Lio repeats local gossip with visible uncertainty.

Some stories later prove wrong.

The ferry becomes a social information node, not only a route unlock.

## LI-R03 — Rope Placement
Olo is repeatedly corrected on one simple rope placement before eventually correcting a younger child in the credits.

Tiny recurrence, no quest.

## LI-R04 — Amsa / Tavin Inspection Bet
Amsa and Tavin make a low-stakes bet about whether a harmless old platform passes inspection.

Tavin loses and argues the measuring tool was damp.

This gives their relationship affection beneath policy conflict.

# 6. Saltreach additions

## LI-S01 — Tessa's Old Nickname
An old quay acquaintance calls Tessa a nickname she hates.

Neris can ask what it means.

Every answer from different locals is different.

None is confirmed.

## LI-S02 — Knot Rail
Children and off-duty sailors tie decorative knots onto a signal railing.

Vessa complains because some interfere with actual line work.

After Grand Ring, the same rail becomes serious manual signaling infrastructure and the decorative knots are carefully moved rather than discarded.

## LI-S03 — Bad Fried Food
One quay stall is famous because everyone insists it used to be better.

No one agrees when.

## LI-S04 — Berth Argument
Rell and Imi's first on-screen disagreement should be over something petty before C13 makes them coordinate survival windows.

The later cooperation lands harder if the player has watched them bicker about mundane harbor nonsense.

---

# 7. Emberstep additions

## LI-E01 — Ugliest Functional Bracket
Shift crews compete to cast the ugliest bracket that still passes a load test.

Sena judges with total seriousness.

Possible recurring winners change after materials/routes change.

No completion reward.

## LI-E02 — Plate Theft
Sena steals food from somebody else's plate during shift meal and insists this is a scheduling issue.

The victim has been planning revenge for years.

## LI-E03 — Scrap Percussion
A child uses scrap as percussion until a worker complains, then joins the rhythm accidentally while hammering.

Post-Stillness, the silence of the same cooling court hurts.

## LI-E04 — Maker-Mark Vanity
Workers have opinions about who has the ugliest maker stamp design.

Restoring traceability therefore carries vanity and humor as well as politics.

---

# 8. High Aerie additions

## LI-H01 — Turnip Load
Someone repeatedly overloads a cargo lift with turnips.

Sal Em takes this personally.

The identity of the offender is never a major mystery.

## LI-H02 — Laundry Flight
A sheet breaks loose in high wind and apprentices try to recover it without telling Ila Dinn.

The player may help or watch the disaster unfold.

No reward besides dialogue.

## LI-H03 — Rope Signal Practice
Pera's early bad rope signals can be observed more than once.

After Motion, the same system becomes life-saving and Pera becomes competent without becoming humorless.

## LI-H04 — Wind Kitchen Seating
People have long-running informal ownership of specific bench spots despite the kitchen being communal.

Mevi refuses to recognize any of these claims.

---

# 9. Mireglass additions

## LI-M01 — Reflection Ring Game
Children throw reed rings toward reflected poles and try to predict where the real pole is.

This exists before crisis, disappears when unsafe, and cautiously returns after Memory.

## LI-M02 — Goat / Boat / Boundary Case
The Three Tables spends absurd professional effort deciding whether a wandering goat crossed a property boundary by boat, bank, or reflected marker.

The case is not secretly important.

## LI-M03 — Tovin's Eel Stew
Everyone says Tovin oversalts it.

Tovin says people who dislike salt can eat somewhere else.

There is nowhere nearby better.

## LI-M04 — Bad Reflection Hair
A polished shop mirror shows one person's hair slightly differently in the reflection.

The owner prefers the reflection.

Do not turn this into a Hush omen.

# 10. Cairnfall additions

## LI-F01 — Flawed Scope Wager
Pell and Riva bet lunch over readings from deliberately flawed training scopes.

Aro knows both instruments are wrong in different documented ways and lets them argue.

## LI-F02 — Unmeasured Soup
Mar Dain refuses to use a measuring ladle in the most measurement-obsessed settlement in Orra.

Hett pretends this bothers her more than it does.

## LI-F03 — Meaningless Star
A child asks Neris to find one bright object with a scope.

It has no prophecy, no post-credit link, and no secret item.

It is beautiful.

## LI-F04 — Chalk Drift Graffiti
Students write drift values on instrument housings; some add jokes and doodles around the numbers.

After Grand Ring, old chalk becomes useful evidence.

# 11. Hush / Ilyra additions

## LI-I01 — Wrong Breakfast
Ilyra recreates a childhood food memory incorrectly.

No lore unlock.

## LI-I02 — Tool Drawer
Neris repeatedly puts a tool somewhere Ilyra would not.

Ilyra moves it back until one later visit when she deliberately leaves it.

## LI-I03 — Old Neighbor
Ilyra asks about someone from Brindle who died or moved years ago.

The scene ends without turning the loss into a thesis.

## LI-I04 — Irrelevant Hinge
After the full Mercy Window truth, Ilyra fixes an unimportant broken hinge because she cannot fix the conversation.

Neris may help silently or leave.

Both are valid.

# 12. Line Skiff / travel additions

## LI-T01 — First Fun Run
After the dangerous first tutorial segment, Tessa gives Neris one stretch of road to simply enjoy the Skiff.

This is mandatory emotional pacing even though it advances no mystery.

## LI-T02 — Route Distance Game
Tessa asks Neris to estimate distance from route wear and timing.

She cheats because she already knows the answer.

## LI-T03 — Courier Graffiti
Old route shelters contain notes from couriers rating food, beds, weather, and supervisors.

Tessa has authored some embarrassing old entries.

---

# 13. Unringing additions

The Unringing must contain life, not only infrastructure projects.

Examples:
- cross-region meals;
- workers comparing tools badly;
- Tessa buried in route forms;
- people misunderstanding one another's trade slang;
- small celebration after the first successful independent cross-region signal;
- somebody immediately complaining the successful signal was late;
- Orin taking an instruction from Neris without “fixing” it;
- Ilyra mostly listening during one group conversation.

These moments protect the late game from becoming a revolutionary planning meeting simulator.

# 15. Reward rule

Not every interaction gets:
- currency;
- Pulse Shard;
- journal percentage;
- achievement;
- permanent stat.

Some interactions exist because recognition itself is a reward.

This is intentional and should survive production trimming unless playtest shows the interaction is actually dull.
