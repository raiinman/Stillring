# Project Stillring — Final Reveal and Foreshadow Ledger

**Status:** final canonical mystery/reveal timing contract  
**Related:** `docs/story/07_SCENE_BEAT_LEDGER.md`, `docs/story/09_MAIN_QUEST_OBJECTIVE_FLOW.md`, `docs/story/10_DIALOGUE_ANCHORS.md`, `docs/12_100_PERCENT_ROUTE.md`

This ledger controls what the player and Neris may know, suspect, prove, and act on at each point in the story.

A major reveal should normally have:
1. **seed** — something odd enough to remember;
2. **evidence** — information that narrows plausible explanations;
3. **pressure** — a reason the question matters personally now;
4. **reveal** — the essential truth becomes knowable;
5. **payoff** — later action changes because the truth was learned.

Optional content may deepen a chain. It may not contain the only indispensable link.

---

# R01 — Orin controlled the truth about Ilyra

## Hidden truth
Orin knew Ilyra worked with Maelor's research circle, knew the official field-accident story was false, and knew her disappearance was connected to dangerous work. He did **not** know she survived in the Hush or know Maelor's future Stillring plan.

## Seeds
- `M00-S02`: Orin hides a Cairnspire-marked letter when Ilyra is mentioned.
- Brindle residents remember Orin shutting down questions differently.
- `M00-S04`: Orin almost opens Ilyra's traveling case, then delays until after First Ring.

## First hard evidence
- `M00-S06`: “Your mother didn't die where they said she did.”
- `M02-S05`: Lower Archive proves Ilyra's official record was altered or hidden.

## Pressure
Neris repeatedly encounters adults deciding when inconvenient truth may be known. Tessa's deliberate incuriosity and Ilyra's staged confession make Orin's choice part of a broader relationship problem rather than one isolated mystery.

## Essential reveal
`M14-S05` — Orin admits exactly what he knew and why he withheld it.

## Payoff
- Neris releases the perfect family workshop rather than treating hidden history as something that can be restored cleanly.
- `M18-S03`: Orin answers plainly and gives Neris the traveling case rather than choosing the next route.

## Never imply
- Orin knew Ilyra was alive;
- Orin knew Stillring's final design;
- an early optional note contains the complete confession.

# R02 — Why the Cantor Key needs Neris

## Hidden truth
Normal Bellwarden diagnostics assume Meridian is the correct master reference. Once Meridian itself is wrong, those tools can become precisely calibrated to the wrong assumption.

Ilyra's prototype Key can compare local relations without first accepting Meridian. During `M00-S07`, the Brindle seam overload damages its field memory and partially writes Neris's emergency local calibration into it.

Others can operate the Key at a bench, but sustained field use drifts unless Neris carries and adjusts it. Safely retraining it would take longer than the crisis allows.

This is accident and circumstance, not prophecy, bloodline, or sentient selection.

## Seed
`M00-S07`: the Key overloads in Neris's hands.

## First evidence
`M02-S02`: Maelor carries the Key away from Neris; its field reading drifts and stabilizes when returned.

> “For now, it remembers you better than it remembers its own instructions.”

## Demonstration
`M03-S11`: six independent baselines let the Key distinguish healthy regional variance from real collapse while remaining stable with Neris.

## Payoff
Maelor has a precise reason to preserve Neris's access and manipulate Neris into producing the three clean crisis references.

## Never imply
- the Listener chose Neris;
- Ilyra designed the Key for Neris specifically;
- family lineage grants unique magical access.

# R03 — Maelor knew Ilyra personally

## Hidden truth
Maelor and Ilyra were close professional collaborators and friends, not distant colleagues and not secret lovers.

## Seeds
- `M02-S02`: Maelor reacts strongly to the Key and Neris.
- `M02-S06`: he knows Ilyra's cracked-cup habit.

## Evidence
- `M04-S06`: “Well enough to miss arguing with her.”
- `M05-S06`: Maelor admits both knew Meridian was killing people.
- `M06-S05`: technical records pair their names repeatedly.

## Reveal
By Emberstep the player can conclude they were core collaborators. Ilyra's own account in `M08-S02` confirms the relationship without melodramatic twist framing.

## Payoff
Mercy Window is a fracture between people who respected each other's work, making Maelor's later extrapolation more personal and more tragic without excusing it.

# R04 — First Ring was a real failure deliberately made worse

## Hidden truth
The Meridian network was genuinely nearing dangerous instability.

First Ring includes an inherited annual full-reference maintenance check. Maelor deliberately disables one emergency damping layer so severe drift cannot be hidden by Bellwarden institutions for another year.

He expects a containable crisis.

The released Hush pressure is much worse than predicted. Ilyra's buried Key acts as a stubborn non-Meridian local reference, concentrating the Vale workshop seam. Orin is taken by consequence, not targeting.

## Seeds
- `M00-S05`: Cairnspire fails during an otherwise ordinary annual Ring.
- route bells were already worsening before the event.
- Maelor repeatedly speaks truthfully about serious central instability.

## Evidence 1
`M04-S06`: Rootmere maintenance comparison shows First Ring damping behavior was abnormal.

## Evidence 2
`M05-S06` and post-Saltreach procurement traces establish privileged maintenance paths existed beyond ordinary field crews.

## Evidence 3
`M06-S05`: restricted Emberstep records show the damping behavior required high-level access and coincides with the three-point reference work.

## Essential reveal
`M07-S02`: Neris accuses Maelor. He admits disabling the damping layer to force the hidden failure into the open and admits he expected to control the result.

## Payoff
The conflict is not “Maelor murdered Orin on purpose.” It is about a person who repeatedly decides other people's risk is acceptable because his diagnosis is correct and his solution feels urgent.

## Never imply
- the underlying Meridian failure was fabricated;
- Maelor selected Orin personally;
- First Ring was harmless until the Key existed.

# R05 — Quiet Court began as legitimate reform

## Hidden truth
Quiet Court began as a reform/research coalition responding to actual Meridian deaths, falsified records, and unsafe centralized maintenance. It fractured over what safety, authority, and phase-control justified.

## Seeds
- `M02-S05`: contradictory casualty records.
- Maelor's advice genuinely saves people.
- Rusk's concerns about incompatible repairs are not nonsense.
- regional residents describe real harm caused by bad state interventions.

## Evidence
- Saltreach freight includes ordinary research tools alongside later restricted equipment.
- Emberstep records contain multiple contributors and dissenting annotations.
- High Aerie copies preserve competing reform proposals.

## Partial reveal
`M08-S02`: Ilyra says she joined willingly because the system was killing people and the official channels would not correct course.

## Full institutional reveal
`M16-S04`: the Quiet Court archive shows how reform hardened into Maelor's coercive permanent-hold project.

## Payoff
The ending cannot simply replace “evil state” with “good rebels.” Orra inherits difficult coordination, standards, accountability, and disagreement.

# R06 — Tessa knowingly chose not to know

## Hidden truth
Tessa knowingly transported restricted research hardware using forged manifests for unusually high pay. She knew the job was dirty and deliberately avoided learning its purpose. She did not know Stillring.

## Seeds
- `M03-S03`: she recognizes a Saltreach pressurewright mark and changes subject.
- she knows unofficial customs routes unusually well.
- people in Saltreach know an old nickname she dislikes.

## Hard evidence
`M05-S06`: the recovered manifest matches her old route.

## Reveal
Tessa admits:

> “I knew enough to know I didn't want to know more.”

## Payoff
- first serious Neris/Tessa fracture;
- she stops treating unofficial status as moral cleanliness;
- after Grand Ring she accepts formal responsibility for route relief and eventually the courier network.

## Never imply
- Tessa secretly understood Stillring;
- her freight stream uniquely caused the project;
- ignorance makes her innocent or equivalent to Maelor.

# R07 — The three crisis regions were selected deliberately

## Hidden truth
Rootmere, Saltreach, and Emberstep were chosen because three stable, geographically separated local references are sufficient for Meridian emergency entrainment to calculate a continent-scale relation and seize the remaining Waybells remotely.

## Seeds
- Maelor calls them “reference” regions, initially normal technical language.
- Cairnspire never convincingly proves the other three Waybells are less important or less unstable.
- First Circuit gives the player healthy baseline data from all six regions, making the selective urgency retrospectively suspicious.

## Evidence
- `M04-S06`: Ilyra's warning against forced full-network synchronization.
- `M05-S06`: shared Maelor/Ilyra field codes and restricted research logistics.
- `M06-S05`: the three regions appear together in unauthorized phase-reference geometry and entrainment records.

## Essential reveal
By `M07-S01`, Neris, Tessa, Sena, Caldrin, and Rusk can derive the mechanism themselves.

`M07-S02` confirms only intent: Maelor selected those three specifically and has already committed Meridian to the first entrainment step.

## Payoff
Grand Ring. Neris understands that excellent local repairs can strengthen a coercive global architecture.

## Never present
The three as sacred trials, magical keys, elemental temples, or destiny markers.

# R08 — Mercy Window

## Hidden truth
Ilyra designed a temporary local phase hold during a route-collapse rescue. It saved workers. A mortally injured worker remained held beyond the intended interval while the team argued about release. Physical state persisted; conscious response repeated; new memory stopped advancing normally.

Ilyra ordered release. The worker later died from the original injuries.

Ilyra concluded release must remain part of the design. Maelor concluded loss had been delayed by engineering.

## Thematic seeds
- living Rootmere systems are harmed by forced geometry;
- Saltreach pressure machinery keeps a creature functioning while coercing it;
- Temper work uses transitions rather than permanent uniformity;
- several ordinary side stories distinguish memory from possession.

## Direct seed
`M06-S05`:

> **MERCY WINDOW — LOCAL HOLD TEST / I. VALE LEAD**

## Pressure
`M07-S02`: Maelor says “Ask her” and calls the death account convenient.

## Partial reveal 1
`M08-S02`: Ilyra admits Quiet Court membership, Key authorship, and dangerous phase work but withholds Mercy Window detail.

## Partial reveal 2
`M11-S04`: Bone Archive reveals the older rescue relation; Ilyra admits she designed Mercy Window.

## Full reveal
`M12-S05`: Mireglass reconstruction shows rescue, extended hold, repetition, release, and the Ilyra/Maelor interpretive split.

## Payoffs
- seam mode explicitly includes closure;
- Still-Cairn requires release of useful states;
- final decommission rejects infinite hold as successful repair.

## Never imply
- Ilyra opposed the project from the first sketch;
- Maelor's interpretation was technically stupid;
- the full human consequence is available in an Act One optional record.

# R09 — Ilyra is alive

## Hidden truth
Ilyra survived eleven years in the Hush.

## Seeds
- `M00-S06`: Orin carefully says she did not die **where they said**.
- Maelor reacts to Ilyra as an unresolved subject rather than ordinary bereavement.
- occasional structured workbench-like Hush patterns are too deliberate to feel random.

## Pressure
`M07-S02`:

> “That has always been the most convenient version.”

## Reveal
`M08-S01`: Ilyra is physically present beside the repeatedly rebuilt wrong-dimension workbench.

## Payoff
Her survival complicates rather than solves Neris's family goal. The lost mother is now an unfamiliar adult with guilt, habits, damage, and agency.

## Never allow
Optional content to plainly state “Ilyra is alive” before Grand Ring.

# R10 — Meridian is a containment machine, not a victory monument

## Hidden truth
The Hush is a naturally coupled echo-layer of abandoned possibilities, memory, and unchosen states. Ancient bellwrights did not defeat an invading evil. They compressed unstable Waking/Hush relations into forced synchronization and imprisoned the emergent Listener with them.

## Seeds
- natural local practices work without Meridian reference;
- some “wounds” predate the network;
- Common Measure coordinates without a master;
- early Hush spaces feel unfinished and mournful, not uniformly hostile.

## Evidence
- `M10-S05`/`M10-S06`: High Aerie records describe containment and pressure language rather than conquest.
- `M11-S03`/`M11-S04`: Bone Archive predates Meridian and proves local Waking/Hush contact existed without apocalypse.

## Partial explanation
`M08-S03`: Ilyra explains only enough to establish that destroying Meridian immediately would release compressed relation uncontrollably.

## Essential conceptual reveal
By Bone Archive, the player can understand that official “the bells saved us from the Hush” history is propaganda or severe simplification.

## Payoff
The midpoint answer cannot be “smash the evil machine now.” The player needs relations that permit controlled release.

# R11 — The Listener is not the hidden villain

## Hidden truth
The Listener is emergent consciousness compounded from compressed unfinished possibility. It is imprisoned, overloaded, and unable to maintain ordinary individual identity.

## Seeds
- not every Hush entity is hostile;
- Hush conflict often occurs where forced states overlap;
- Mirror Widow is fragmented identity, not possession;
- Ilyra has never received a coherent revenge demand from the Hush.

## Evidence
- High Aerie/Bone records describe containment;
- Palinode demonstrates incompatible histories compressed together;
- Still-Cairn shows preservation can itself create cages.

## Reveal
`M17-S01` through `M17-S04`: the Listener communicates through preserved possibilities and asks:

> **“MUST ALL ENDINGS BE CAGES?”**

## Payoff
Neris releases the Listener from forced singularity rather than killing a cosmic enemy.

## Never reveal
That the Listener possessed or secretly guided Maelor. Maelor owns his choices.

# R12 — Motion

## Hidden relation
**Motion** is a stable relation in which systems can remain coordinated while continuing to change instead of resetting to one phase.

## Seeds
- living Rootmere flexes safely;
- Saltreach works through changing pressure and tide;
- Temper uses controlled transitions;
- Line Skiff travels by differential movement.

## Discovery through action
`M10-S05`: Saint Varo's arena stabilizes only when the player allows failure and return to coexist instead of forcing one perfect story to reset.

## Naming
Only after the player performs it does Ilyra call the relation **Motion**.

## Payoff
Supports moving pockets, Unringing coordination, and the final release relation.

## Never do
Do not tell the player at `M08-S03` that Motion is “one of three Countertones to collect.”

# R13 — Memory

## Hidden relation
**Memory** preserves information and meaning without requiring the present to physically become and indefinitely inhabit the past.

## Seeds
- Ilyra memories are fragmentary rather than perfect replays;
- Kelp Bell Funeral separates recorded sound from physical possession;
- archives disagree;
- Palinode preserves incompatible histories.

## Discovery through action
`M12-S04`: Mirror Widow resolves when contradictory selves stop fighting to become the official one and a shared mundane memory can anchor relation without recreating a perfect political outcome.

## Naming
Only after resolution is the relation named **Memory**.

## Payoff
Neris can know Mercy Window without living inside it and later recognizes Listener worlds as preserved states rather than futures.

# R14 — Ending

## Hidden relation
Allowing a state to end does not erase that it existed.

## Seeds
- Rootmere improves when a bad repair is removed;
- Saltreach remembrance exists after irreparable loss;
- Mercy Window's ethical boundary is release;
- Mirror Nail commits only temporarily;
- seam mode includes closure.

## Discovery through action
`M14-S02` through `M14-S04`: useful safe states become cages unless released. Neris deliberately releases the perfect Vale workshop without destroying its memory.

## Naming
Only after the workshop is released does Ilyra name **Ending**.

This is the first point where the story may naturally summarize Motion / Memory / Ending as a learned set.

## Payoff
Neris can end Meridian's central hold without erasing the Hush or the past.

## Hidden truth
Rusk participated in concealment, restrictive archive policy, and a culture that treated public confidence as infrastructure. He did not knowingly authorize Stillring.

## Seeds
- reasonable attempt to control the experimental Key;
- process obsession;
- euphemistic incident language;
- resistance to uncontrolled disclosure.

## Evidence
- post-Rootmere: he orders limited inspections but resists broader conclusions;
- post-Saltreach: he investigates procurement while preserving institutional control over release;

## Clarification
`M07-S03`: he is horrified by Grand Ring and opens locks his own rules kept sealed.

## Payoff

# R16 — Maelor's thesis

## Hidden truth
Maelor believes irreversible state transition is the root of grief. If meaningful states can be held physically, he believes future suffering from irreversible loss can be eliminated.

## Personal origin
His wife and young son died in a Waybell synchronization accident hidden by the same institution that later concealed other deaths.

## Seeds
- obsession with stable reference;
- unusual calm around disaster;
- stories about preserving flawed instruments rather than discarding them;
- oblique family references in records and colleagues' speech.

## Evidence
- `M05-S07`: “we disagreed about what safety required.”
- `M07-S02`: “Another tomorrow.”
- `M12-S05`: Mercy Window shows why he believed holding state was technically meaningful.

## Midgame pressure
Maelor occasionally makes correct narrow warnings about what uncontrolled release would destroy. The story allows him technical competence without granting ethical authority.

## Full statement
`M16-S05`/`M16-S06`: his final offer is an end to future loss, not shared power.

## Payoff
Neris rejects the premise that wanting a good moment to last is consent to having no future after it.

# R17 — The final answer is dismantling, not restoration

## Hidden truth
Meridian is not a sacred machine waiting for the right repair. Its architecture depends on forced singular reference and central authority.

## Seeds through play
- `M02-S03`: locally healthy / systemically wrong.
- Common Measure: coordination without master phase.
- Rootmere: removing a bad repair helps the living system.
- Saltreach: decoupling control is necessary even though harm remains.
- Emberstep: a purity/safety command becomes lethal when unable to tolerate variance.

## Evidence
- Grand Ring proves three good repairs can strengthen coercion.
- High Aerie/Bone reveal containment history.
- Cairnfall improves when correction is disconnected.
- Unringing proves regions can coordinate imperfectly without one master.

## Essential realization
By `M16-S01`, the player should already understand that “repair Meridian properly” is the wrong objective.

No character needs to announce “destroy it” as a late twist.

## Payoff
`M17-S05`: Neris's final repair is decommissioning the central heart.

# R18 — Decentralization is not utopia

## Hidden truth
The story's answer is not “local always good, standards always bad.” Shared standards, watershed coordination, navigation timing, industrial safety, and archival discipline remain valuable when they are accountable and revisable.

## Seeds
- Rusk has legitimate safety arguments.
- Saltreach captains remember real lives saved by shared timing.
- Rootmere flood problems cross local boundaries.
- Emberstep needs coordinated industrial limits.

## Evidence
- side quests present genuine failures of local practice as well as central overreach;
- Unringing projects require communities to coordinate, compromise, document, and sometimes accept common standards.

## Payoff
Credits show distributed systems occasionally failing, people arguing about policy, and repair continuing. Orra has replaced coerced singularity with accountable imperfection, not solved politics forever.

# Optional-content reveal rules

## Optional content may reveal early
- names of Quiet Court contributors;
- specific historical casualties;
- Ilyra's technical shorthand;
- Maelor-funded procurement;
- evidence that natural Hush contact predates Meridian;
- local opinions that complicate official history;
- incomplete hints that First Ring damping was unusual.

## Optional content may not reveal before schedule
- Ilyra is alive;
- the full Mercy Window human consequence;
- Maelor's explicit First Ring intervention;
- exactly how present-day three-point entrainment will be activated before Emberstep evidence;
- the Listener's final nature/voice;
- Orin's complete knowledge;
- that Neris will destroy Meridian.

## Completionist acknowledgement
If optional evidence lets a player infer something early, mandatory dialogue acknowledges the inference instead of pretending ignorance.

Example: if multiple Court procurement records were found before Saltreach, Neris may say:

> “This isn't the first time I've seen that seal.”

The mandatory manifest still owns Tessa's personal involvement.

# Reveal quality test

Before adding or moving a reveal, answer:
1. What could the player reasonably suspect beforehand?
2. What evidence makes that suspicion fair?
3. Does the reveal answer an old question and create a better new one?
4. Which relationship changes because of it?
5. What does the player **do differently** afterward?
6. Can a completionist know more without breaking the critical path?
7. Is the reveal delivered through action/evidence where dialogue would otherwise repeat what the player already proved?

A reveal that exists only for surprise and changes no later action should be cut or rebuilt.
