# Project Stillring — Reveal and Foreshadow Ledger

**Status:** canonical mystery/reveal timing contract  
**Related:** Issue #16, `docs/story/07_SCENE_BEAT_LEDGER.md`

This ledger prevents the story from accidentally revealing its own answers too early or withholding them so long that twists feel arbitrary.

A major reveal should normally have:

1. **seed** — an oddity the player can notice before understanding it;
2. **evidence** — a clue that narrows possibilities;
3. **pressure** — a character or event that makes the question matter personally;
4. **reveal** — the point where the essential truth becomes knowable;
5. **payoff** — a later action, not merely another explanation, that uses the truth.

Optional content may enrich a chain. It may not contain the only indispensable link.

---

# R01 — Orin lied about Ilyra

## Hidden truth
Orin knew Ilyra worked with Maelor's research circle and knew the official field-accident account was false. He did not know she survived in the Hush.

## Seeds
- `M00-S02`: Orin hides a Cairnspire-marked letter when Ilyra is mentioned.
- villagers remember Orin shutting down questions about Ilyra differently.
- `M00-S04`: Orin nearly opens Ilyra's traveling case, then delays the conversation until after First Ring.

## First evidence
- `M00-S06`: Orin says, “Your mother didn't die where they said she did.”
- Lower Archive proves Ilyra's state record was hidden/altered.

## Pressure
Neris spends Act One chasing truths Orin deliberately withheld.
Tessa's Saltreach “I didn't ask” defense makes secrecy a recurring relationship problem rather than a single family mystery.

## Full reveal
`M14-S04` — Orin admits what he knew and why he hid it.

## Payoff
- Neris refuses the perfect family reconstruction in Still-Cairn.
- Orin's epilogue repair is to answer questions rather than controlling what Neris may know.

## Do not
- imply Orin knew Ilyra was alive;
- imply Orin understood Stillring's final plan;
- reveal the exact research-circle connection in an early optional note.

# R02 — Maelor knew Ilyra personally

## Hidden truth
Maelor and Ilyra were close professional collaborators and friends, not distant colleagues.

## Seeds
- `M02-S02`: Maelor visibly reacts to Neris and the Cantor Key.
- `M02-S06`: he knows Ilyra's cracked-cup habit.

## Evidence
- `M04-S06`: Maelor admits he misses arguing with her.
- Saltreach manifest contains both field codes.

## Reveal
- `M05-S06`: Maelor admits they funded unsanctioned work together.
- Emberstep logs formally pair their names.

## Payoff
Mercy Window is emotionally stronger because the technical split occurs between people who respected each other.

## Do not
Turn their relationship into a secret romance unless the entire family history is deliberately rewritten. Their intimacy is intellectual/friendship, not a cheap affair twist.

# R03 — Quiet Court was not born evil

## Hidden truth
Quiet Court began as a genuine reform/research coalition responding to real Meridian deaths and Bellwarden cover-ups. It fractured over what replacement/safety justified.

## Seeds
- contradictory accident records;
- local communities recount real state-repair harm;
- Maelor's Act One advice genuinely saves people;
- Rusk's concerns about incompatible repairs are not nonsense.

## Evidence
- Saltreach freight lists mundane research equipment mixed with later restricted components;
- Emberstep records show multiple technical contributors rather than one Maelor order chain;
- High Aerie copies describe competing reform proposals.

## Partial reveal
Ilyra's reunion: she says she joined willingly because the system was killing people.

## Full reveal
Null Meridian Quiet Court archive (`M16-S03`) shows the coalition's evolution and fracture.

## Payoff
The ending does not simply replace one central authority with “the rebels.” Communities inherit difficult responsibility and provenance.

## Optional enrichment
All 32 testimony/record entries can add specific Court members, dissenters, victims, and technical branches.

# R04 — Tessa moved Quiet Court equipment

## Hidden truth
Tessa knowingly moved sealed research freight under forged manifests for high pay but did not know the Stillring purpose. She protected herself by not asking.

## Seeds
- `M03-S03`: she recognizes a Saltreach maker mark and changes subject.
- Tessa is unusually familiar with unofficial Saltreach customs routes.

## Evidence
`M05-S05`: surviving manifest matches her old route.

## Reveal
Tessa admits the forged papers and deliberate ignorance.

## Payoff
- Neris/Tessa first serious conflict.
- Tessa later chooses formal responsibility by organizing the courier network.
- route workers recognize her in optional scenes.

## Do not
Make the freight shipment the unique cause of Stillring. It was one logistics stream among many.

# R05 — Rootmere, Saltreach, Emberstep were chosen deliberately

## Hidden truth
Maelor selected the three “urgent” commissions because they are geographically useful stable reference points for the Meridian emergency entrainment system.

## Seeds
- Maelor repeatedly calls them “reference” regions, a technically normal phrase at first.
- the three regions are physically distinct enough to make good phase baselines.
- Cairnspire diagnostics do not clearly establish that the other three Waybells are less damaged.

## Evidence
- Ilyra warning against forced full-network synchronization in Rootmere;
- old Quiet Court/field codes in Saltreach;
- Emberstep sealed log names all three in an unauthorized phase-reference test.

## Reveal
`M07-S02`: Maelor explains that three stable separated signals are enough for emergency entrainment to seize the rest.

## Payoff
Grand Ring.

## Do not
Present the three regions as sacred chosen trials or three magical keys. Their relationship is engineering geometry and network control.

# R06 — Mercy Window

## Hidden truth
Ilyra designed a brief local phase hold as a rescue procedure. It saved workers. Extending the hold on a mortally injured worker preserved the body while conscious response repeated and new memory stopped progressing. Ilyra concluded release must remain part of the design. Maelor concluded loss could be delayed by engineering.

## Seed 1 — family language
The opening teaches Ilyra's approach: listen to flaws rather than force a perfect tone.

## Seed 2 — technical history
- Rootmere proves local systems can be harmed by forced state geometry.
- Saltreach shows preservation machinery can keep a creature alive while becoming coercive.
- Temper Gauntlet teaches intentionally non-uniform states.

These are thematic preparation, not direct clues.

## Direct seed
`M06-S05`: **MERCY WINDOW — LOCAL HOLD TEST / I. VALE LEAD**.

## Pressure
`M07-S02`: Maelor says “Ask her” and implies the official death story is convenient.

## Partial reveal 1
`M08-S02`: Ilyra admits joining the Court and developing precursor technology but avoids Mercy Window.

## Partial reveal 2
`M11-S04`: Bone Archive shows the older local-hold rescue relation; Ilyra admits she designed Mercy Window.

## Full reveal
`M12-S04`: Mireglass reconstruction shows the worker rescue, extended hold, repetition, release, and split between Ilyra/Maelor interpretations.

## Payoff 1
Cairnfall seam mode is designed with **release/closure** as an explicit operational step.

## Payoff 2
Still-Cairn teaches Ending by releasing safe temporary states.

## Payoff 3
The final act destroys permanent central hold rather than treating infinite duration as a successful repair.

## Do not
- reveal the full human-trial outcome in an optional Act One record;
- make Ilyra secretly opposed from the first sketch;
- make Maelor's interpretation technically nonsensical. It is ethically catastrophic, not stupid.

# R07 — The Meridian Bell is a containment engine

## Hidden truth
The ancient Bellwrights did not defeat an invading Hush evil. They compressed a naturally occurring coupled state and its emergent Listener into forced synchronization.

## Seeds
- old records call natural seams “wounds” without explaining why;
- some pre-network local practices function without Meridian reference;
- Rootmere and Saltreach systems behave healthier when coercive central mechanisms are decoupled;
- Common Measure works without central phase.

## Evidence
- High Aerie forbidden tablets describe construction as containment/pressure language rather than victory language.
- Bone Archive predates Meridian and shows Waking/Hush contact without apocalypse.

## Partial reveal
Ilyra reunion explains that destroying Meridian immediately would release compressed Hush pressure catastrophically.

## Full conceptual reveal
High Aerie/Bone Archive: the Hush was present before Meridian; official “invasion” history is propaganda/oversimplification.

## Payoff
Neris cannot simply smash Meridian at midpoint. Motion, Memory, Ending and seam control are needed for a controlled release.

# R08 — The Listener is not the villain

## Hidden truth
The Listener is emergent consciousness produced/compounded by compressed unfinished possibility. It is imprisoned and overwhelmed, not plotting conquest.

## Seeds
- early Hush impressions are mournful/unfinished rather than predatory by default;
- some Hush entities attack because forced state conflict destabilizes them;
- Mirror Widow conflict is about incompatible identity, not evil possession.

## Evidence
- High Aerie tablets describe imprisonment.
- Ilyra has never received a coherent revenge demand from the Hush.
- Palinode shows multiple histories compressed together.

## Reveal
Final encounter: Listener cannot distinguish current Neris from possible Neris states and asks, “MUST ALL ENDINGS BE CAGES?”

## Payoff
The solution releases forced singularity rather than killing a cosmic enemy.

## Do not
Use a secret reveal that Maelor was possessed/manipulated by the Listener. That would launder his agency.

# R09 — Maelor's actual thesis

## Hidden truth
Maelor believes grief/loss are consequences of state transition. If meaningful states can be preserved physically, he believes suffering from irreversible change can be eliminated.

## Seeds
- his technical obsession with stable reference;
- personal calm around crisis;
- cracked-cup and flawed-instrument anecdotes;
- family death referenced obliquely by colleagues/records.

## Evidence
- Saltreach: “we disagreed about what safety required.”
- Emberstep: three-point phase experiment.
- Grand Ring line: “Another tomorrow.”
- Mercy Window history provides the proof-of-concept he extrapolated from.

## Midgame argument
Cairnfall call explicitly challenges Neris on why death is accepted but repetitive preservation is considered horror.

## Full statement
Null Meridian offer: preserve everyone before the next irreversible loss.

## Payoff
Neris's response distinguishes wanting a moment to last from consenting to no future.

# R10 — Ilyra is alive

## Hidden truth
Ilyra survived eleven years in the Hush.

## Seeds
- Orin says she did not die where reported.
- Maelor reacts as if her status is unresolved rather than simple bereavement.
- Cantor Key occasionally catches workbench-like Hush patterns that are too structured to be random.

## Pressure
Grand Ring confrontation: Maelor says the death story was “convenient.”

## Reveal
`M08-S01`: Ilyra appears beside the repeatedly rebuilt wrong-dimension workbench.

## Payoff
Her survival complicates rather than solves Neris's family goal.

## Do not
Allow optional content to plainly state “Ilyra is alive” before Grand Ring.

# R11 — Motion

## Hidden truth
Motion is not speed or wind power. It is a stable relation in which systems remain coordinated while continuing to change rather than repeating one phase.

## Seeds
- living Rootmere systems flex safely;
- local tide practices change with conditions;
- Temper work uses controlled transitions;
- Line Skiff relies on differential movement rather than fixed position.

## Reveal through action
High Aerie storm stabilizes only when Saint Varo's construct stops resetting itself to one perfect story and suspended systems are allowed to move in bounded relation.

## Payoff
Motion becomes necessary for local moving pockets and final Countertone use.

---

# R12 — Memory

## Hidden truth
Memory can preserve information/meaning without physically recreating and indefinitely inhabiting the past state.

## Seeds
- Ilyra memories are sensory fragments rather than perfect replay;
- Kelp Bell Funeral distinguishes recorded sound from physical ownership;
- archives disagree;
- Bone Archive teaches Palinode.

## Reveal through action
Mirror Widow resolves when contradictory selves stop fighting to be the official one and a shared mundane event can be remembered without recreating one perfect treaty outcome.

## Payoff
Neris can know Mercy Window without living inside it; Listener alternate worlds are recognized as preserved states rather than futures.

---

# R13 — Ending

## Hidden truth
Allowing a state to end does not erase that it existed.

## Seeds
- Mawhart survives after bad braces are removed;
- Kelp Bell Funeral;
- Mercy Window's ethical boundary is release;
- Mirror Nail pins only temporarily;
- seam mode includes closure.

## Reveal through action
Still-Cairn requires the player to repeatedly let useful states disappear. Neris releases the perfect family workshop without destroying its memory.

## Payoff
Meridian central heart can be deliberately ended; the Hush is not erased.

# R14 — Orin is trapped in his protection logic

## Hidden truth
Stillring amplifies Orin's protective secrecy into a physical state: his Hush shell preserves each apparently safe moment and prevents Neris from becoming anything beyond the child he remembers failing to protect.

## Seeds
- opening delays truth “until after First Ring.”
- Neris repeatedly reacts to adults controlling information.
- Ilyra repeats the same mistake with Mercy Window confession timing.

## Reveal
Still-Cairn encounter.

## Payoff
Orin's epilogue action is to give Neris the traveling case and stop choosing the route.

# R15 — Rusk is complicit but not Maelor

## Hidden truth
Rusk participated in institutional secrecy and prioritizes public confidence, but did not knowingly authorize Stillring.

## Seeds
- legitimate concern about experimental Key safety;
- desire to confiscate it;
- insistence on process;
- euphemistic language around incidents.

## Evidence
Archive policy/withholding shows his complicity in institutional culture.

## Clarification
Grand Ring: Rusk is horrified by Maelor's activation and manually opens locks to save civilians.

## Payoff
He can later testify against Maelor and Bellwarden cover-ups without becoming a spotless hero.

# R16 — The final solution is dismantling, not restoration

## Hidden truth
Meridian is not a sacred machine that only needs the right hero to repair it. The centralized forced-synchronization architecture itself is the problem.

## Seeds
- Public Bell Office: local healthy / systemic wrong.
- Common Measure: coordination without master phase.
- Rootmere: removing an old repair improves the living system.
- Saltreach: decoupling control saves Leviathan.
- Emberstep: purity command harms what it protects.

## Evidence
- Grand Ring proves three good repairs can strengthen coercive architecture.
- High Aerie/Bone Archive reveal containment history.
- Cairnfall works better after correction is disconnected.
- Unringing proves local systems can coordinate without one master.

## Reveal
By Null Meridian, the player has already mechanically disproven the assumption that Meridian must be preserved.

## Final payoff
Neris's last “repair” is decommissioning the central heart.

## Do not
Have a last-minute character simply tell Neris “destroy it.” The player should already understand why through play.

# Optional-content reveal rules

## Allowed early optional knowledge
Optional content may reveal:
- names of Quiet Court contributors;
- specific historical casualties;
- local resistance to standardization;
- Ilyra's technical shorthand;
- Maelor-funded procurement;
- hints that natural Hush contact predates Meridian.

## Not allowed before critical reveal
Optional content must not plainly state before its scheduled reveal:
- Ilyra is alive;
- Mercy Window's full human-trial result;
- exactly how three-point entrainment will be used in the present;
- the Listener's final nature/voice;
- Orin's complete knowledge;
- that Neris will destroy Meridian.

## Completionist acknowledgement
When optional evidence precedes a mandatory reveal, dialogue should acknowledge it.

Example:

If the player collected multiple Court procurement records before Saltreach, Neris may say:

> “This isn't the first time I've seen that seal.”

The mandatory manifest scene still establishes Tessa's personal involvement.

# Reveal quality check

Before adding or moving a reveal, ask:

1. What did the player reasonably suspect beforehand?
2. What evidence made that suspicion possible?
3. Does the reveal answer an old question and create a better new one?
4. Which character is hurt or changed by learning it?
5. What does the player **do differently** because of the truth?
6. If an optional player already knows more, does the scene acknowledge that without breaking the critical path?

A reveal that exists only to shock the player and changes no later action should be cut or rebuilt.
