## Locked principle

> **The Line Skiff network is world infrastructure first and optional-content scaffolding second.**

The same authored roads should support multiple legitimate purposes where fiction and level design permit:

- ordinary cross-region travel;
- story travel;
- Tessa/courier infrastructure;
- community/world-state connectivity;
- Trial 05 mastery;
- Drift Knot hunts;
- tool-gated maintenance interactions;
- changed-world route consequences.

Stillring does **not** build twelve isolated racetracks merely because there are twelve Drift Knots.

A second locked principle:

> **The Skiff should still be worth using after every Drift Knot is complete.**

Its core value remains enjoyable, expressive traversal through a known world.

---

# 1. Network coverage philosophy

## Sparse enough to preserve the world, connected enough to matter

The Line Skiff network is not an overlay covering every walkable road.

It should feel like real legacy courier/maintenance infrastructure:

- concentrated along routes where reinforcement strips historically made sense;
- incomplete, damaged, bypassed, or repurposed in places;
- strongest along long connectors where speed meaningfully changes travel;
- weaker or absent in dense local spaces where on-foot exploration is the point.

## Cross-region requirement

By the natural late-game/completion state, the usable/recoverable network must **meaningfully approach every major region** through at least one authored trunk/connector relationship where story/world-state permits.

This does not require Skiff access to every internal hub street, dungeon entrance, or secret.

The expected pattern is often:

```text
regional trunk
   ↓
perimeter / service approach
   ↓
park
   ↓
last meaningful stretch on foot or with another authored traversal verb
```

The Skiff transforms travel between remembered places without deleting the places themselves.

## No first-visit erasure

No route design may cause the player to skip a major region's required before-memory/ordinary-life experience.

The Skiff is acquired after the existing First Circuit and regional groundwork. Future route restorations must preserve that authored chronology.

---

# 2. Route-purpose families

These are authoring/pacing families, not separate persistence identity systems.

## A. Trunk Corridors

Primary sustained travel connections between major regions/route hubs.

Goals:

- clear route readability;
- meaningful travel-time reduction versus on-foot Sprint;
- enough length to enjoy acceleration/coasting;
- stable world composition at Skiff speed;
- practical service/park nodes where long travel needs recovery or nearby content access.

A trunk should not run at maximum speed through dense social spaces merely because the spline can fit.

## B. Hub Approaches

Moderate-speed connectors near settlements, service yards, refuges, or major destination edges.

Goals:

- transition from high-speed travel back into readable place-scale movement;
- provide safe parking/arrival;
- avoid blasting through crowds/dialogue spaces;
- let the player understand where they arrived.

## C. Regional Spurs

Shorter optional branches serving:

- repair/service infrastructure;
- refuges;
- courier connections;
- community-project work;
- optional traversal opportunities;
- selected completion content.

A spur does not need to exist merely because a collectible is nearby.

## D. Legacy / Maintenance Lanes

Old or dormant route infrastructure that may require:

- Roadhand Pulse;
- Switchshoe;
- another authored tool interaction;
- changed-world repair;
- specific route knowledge.

These create deeper network literacy and often support Drift Knot or courier content.

## E. Hazard / Hunt Segments

Sections whose changed-world state creates a meaningful moving-fault or route challenge.

They should normally reuse or extend believable network infrastructure rather than becoming disconnected arcade tracks.

## F. Mastery Course

Trial 05's old courier loop is a deliberately authored mastery environment.

It remains believable infrastructure with route-history function rather than a floating abstract race arena.

---

# 3. Regional network identity

The same control grammar should not make every region feel like the same road with different wallpaper.

Regional route presentation and route problems may differ through authored infrastructure/world conditions, for example:

- Circuit / Cairnspire — old courier loops, civic ring roads, viaduct maintenance strips;
- Rootmere — reinforced low roads, living-root crossings, wet structural relationships;
- Saltreach — breakwater/coastal service routes, pressure/tide dependencies;
- Emberstep — freight roads, thermal switch housings, industrial route hardware;
- High Aerie — descent lines, wind-exposed elevated infrastructure, Vane/Crosswind relationships;
- Mireglass — wetland/reed routes where historical/reflective continuity may be unreliable;
- Cairnfall — observatory/night routes and reference-frame windows;
- Meridian/late network — authored synthesis routes only where story authority supports them.

The base Skiff remains learnable everywhere. Regional differences come from route state, geometry, tool relationships, and world context—not arbitrary new steering controls per zone.

# 5. Drift Knots reuse the world network

## Default rule

A Drift Knot should use an ordinary/story/courier route corridor or a believable extension of one whenever possible.

This creates benefits:

- player already understands the broad geography;
- hunt skill comes from reading changed behavior rather than learning a disconnected racetrack;
- route authoring receives reuse across travel and optional content;
- route changes feel like world problems rather than minigame portals;
- performance/camera/readability work benefits multiple systems.

## Special segment exception

A bespoke hunt segment is allowed when its fiction/system test genuinely requires it.

It must still have an authored infrastructure reason to exist.

Examples could include:

- old maintenance bypass;
- observation loop;
- abandoned freight connector;
- emergency service strip.

`We needed somewhere to put Knot #8` is not an infrastructure reason.

---

# 6. No hidden route-unlock completion category

Discovering every Skiff route segment is **not** a new 100% category.

Do not create completion requirements for:

- riding every segment;
- discovering every junction;
- parking at every node;
- waking every dormant strip;
- mapping 100% of network distance;
- completing arbitrary route percentages.

Completion remains governed by existing categories:

- Drift Knots;
- Skill Trials;
- Tool Mastery Upgrade (Switchshoe);
- Repair Contracts;
- Setup/Payoff;
- Community Projects;
- other locked categories where a route participates.

The route network is infrastructure supporting content, not another checklist layered on top.

---

# 7. Ordinary utility beyond hunts

Even with 12/12 Drift Knots complete, the Skiff remains useful through:

## Cross-region travel

Fast, expressive return travel between known regions remains the baseline value.

## Tessa / courier infrastructure

The network supports Tessa's independent courier relationships and Unringing route logic where authored.

The player may participate in selected courier/service interactions, but Stillring does not need an infinite delivery-job economy to justify the Skiff.

## Changed-world navigation

Routes communicate:

- what infrastructure survived;
- what communities repaired;
- what central systems failed;
- how local connections evolve during Unringing.

## Optional content access

The Skiff can make revisits to Faults, refuges, contracts, records, community work, and other known-world content less tedious without automatically delivering the player directly to every secret.

## Story use

Selected story approaches may use the network where already authored, including later Cairnspire/Unringing relationships.

---

# 8. Courier / service content boundary

Stillring may author a small number of meaningful courier/service activities that use Skiff traversal.

They should exist because the world has something worth moving, coordinating, or repairing—not because the game needs repeatable vehicle missions.

## Allowed

- one-time authored courier beats;
- local route recovery/service tasks;
- relationship beats with Tessa/route workers;
- community-project logistics;
- world-pulse route updates;
- contextual non-completion travel interactions.

## Not required

- endless generated deliveries;
- courier XP levels;
- random parcel rewards;
- daily route challenges;
- fuel/profit economy;
- repeating the same route for progression currency;
- vehicle reputation grind.

If repeatable courier flavor exists, it is non-completion-bearing and cannot hold unique required progression hostage.

---

# 9. Route density / content-bloat rule

Route coverage is judged by **utility and world fit**, not kilometers of reinforcement strip.

## A route is justified when it materially supports one or more of:

- meaningful travel-time transformation;
- established courier/maintenance history;
- story route function;
- regional/world-state connectivity;
- mastery/trial use;
- Drift Knot use;
- optional-service/repair use;
- a later route synthesis purpose.

One strong purpose can be enough when the route is naturally justified.

## Multi-use is preferred, not a quota

A segment serving several systems is production-efficient and world-coherent.

However, do not force unrelated content onto every road merely to hit a reuse score.

## Redundancy audit

Content review should flag route segments that:

- duplicate another route with no meaningful choice/function;
- exist only to lengthen travel;
- add a nearly identical Drift Knot corridor;
- require substantial bespoke geometry for one minor reward;
- create a parallel road that makes the old road irrelevant for no story reason.

Human review decides whether the redundancy is intentional.

---

# 10. Hub / social-space protection

The Skiff should usually approach rather than penetrate dense authored social spaces at high speed.

## Arrival rhythm

Preferred pattern:

1. trunk travel at expressive speed;
2. hub approach slows/reshapes route naturally;
3. safe arrival/park;
4. player enters settlement/social space on foot.

This preserves:

- NPC readability;
- environmental storytelling;
- ordinary-life texture;
- conversation staging;
- camera scale;
- foot-level secrets and interactions.

A settlement may contain slow Skiff service infrastructure if fiction/design requires it, but high-speed drive-through is not the default.

---

# 11. Discovery model

## Network visibility

Compatible reinforcement infrastructure has physical world language even before it is fully usable.

The player may learn routes through:

- prior First Circuit exposure;
- Tessa's tutorial/route knowledge;
- natural physical exploration;
- Roadhand Pulse;
- local signs/workers;
- story/community changes;
- broad map/journal topology after discovery.

## No full-network dump

Acquiring the Skiff does not automatically reveal every route, legacy transfer, maintenance lane, or Hunt path on the world map.

Known main trunks may be explained because Tessa/route workers reasonably know them.

Optional/dormant/changed-world lanes can remain discoveries.

## Discovery is not completion

A discovered route is knowledge, not a completion award unless some existing content category explicitly owns a resolved fact on it.

---

# 12. Drift Knot discovery / reacquisition

`docs/100` remains authoritative.

## Discovery

Once family and individual prerequisites are met, a Knot may be surfaced through:

- broad courier report;
- regional route-sector clue;
- changed route behavior;
- Cantor/Roadhand signal while using the relevant corridor;
- existing journal/map completion-assist tiers.

Do not permanently attach an exact GPS marker to a moving Knot.

## Reacquisition

Losing a Knot:

- enters a clear lost-signal state;
- does not destroy the Hunt;
- does not require restarting a long cross-region approach;
- returns/continues through a deterministic authored route phase;
- allows reacquisition from a reasonable sector/state.

A player who abandons a Hunt can return later without consuming a ticket or losing completion eligibility.

---

# 13. Route-tool interaction contracts

The existence of a tool in Neris's inventory does not automatically mean every full-speed Skiff state supports using it.

Each route interaction that involves another tool must declare an explicit **Route Tool Interaction Contract**.

## Interaction classes

An authored interaction may be one of:

### Observe / Diagnose Compatible

The tool reads/diagnoses route state without taking transform authority in a way that conflicts with Skiff travel.

### In-Motion Compatible

A specifically authored interaction can occur while coupled/moving and defines:

- valid speed/window;
- input ownership;
- route effect;
- player/Skiff continuity;
- failure behavior.

Roadhand Pulse is the baseline example.

Other tools may use this class only where their own authority permits it.

### Slow-Zone Compatible

The route transitions into an authored reduced-speed interaction envelope so the tool can be used safely without fully parking.

### Park / Service Required

The Skiff must stop at a compatible semantic node before the tool interaction begins.

## No generic mounted-tool sandbox

Do not automatically enable full ordinary Anchor/Glasslung/Temper/Mirror/seam toolkits at maximum Skiff speed.

That would create:

- animation/input ownership conflicts;
- camera conflicts;
- combat-like balance scope;
- large failure/recovery complexity;
- accidental traversal exploits.

Each Drift Knot/tool synthesis specifies the minimum route interaction actually needed.

---

# 14. Cross-tool synthesis quality rule

Later Knots should change **what the player must notice/decide**, not merely add more simultaneous button presses.

Examples already locked:

- Anchor changes structural timing;
- Glasslung changes pressure-gate state;
- Temper changes switch-housing state;
- Switchshoe changes route topology choice;
- Vane/Crosswind changes wind/reference handling;
- Palinode changes which apparent route history is trusted;
- Mirror changes committed branch state;
- Fine Fork changes signal discrimination;
- seam mode changes late route continuity.

Reject a late Hunt that is only:

> same chase, but press two extra tool buttons.

The synthesis must alter route reasoning.

---

# 15. Anti-racing-repetition authority

The Line Skiff is not a racing-game subsystem hidden inside Stillring.

Across Trial 05 + 12 Drift Knots:

- route choice should vary;
- tool relationship should vary;
- tracking problem should vary;
- world state should vary;
- success condition should vary where appropriate;
- speed pressure should not be the only escalating axis.

## No repeated race ladder

Do not add:

- Skiff Cup 1–10;
- increasingly strict par times as main optional progression;
- AI rival championships;
- repeated same-route time attacks for completion;
- vehicle stat grind to meet later speed gates.

Optional personal-best play may exist without completion/reward authority.

---

# 16. Network progression without arbitrary locks

The network may become more capable over time because:

- story/world state changes;
- communities repair infrastructure;
- Roadhand can wake compatible dormant strips;
- new tools solve specific route problems;
- Switchshoe opens compatible legacy transfers;
- Hush/seam relationships become understood.

It does not need a generic `Skiff Network Level 1–5` progression bar.

Every new route possibility should have an authored reason.

---

# 17. Route Listener relationship

After all 12 Drift Knots resolve, the bounded Route Listener service remains the final Hunt reward under `docs/100`.

Its function:

- while entering/using an eligible road sector, it may indicate whether unresolved **route-class Fault/courier service work** remains broadly in that sector;
- it does not reveal exact Knot coordinates because all Knots are already required before it unlocks;
- it does not become universal collectible radar;
- it does not locate unrelated vaults/secrets/puzzle answers;
- it is a completion-assist/readability service built from route mastery.

Route Listener therefore extends the usefulness of the network after 12/12 rather than making the network obsolete.

---

# 18. Performance / world-production boundary

High-speed route coverage multiplies production requirements.

Every sustained-speed corridor must be reviewed for:

- world streaming readiness;
- collision continuity;
- camera sightlines;
- junction readability;
- HLOD/pop behavior;
- audio transition stability;
- NPC/encounter streaming where relevant;
- route-state transitions;
- changed-world representation;
- safe stop/recovery.

This is why the network is authored and selective rather than everywhere.

A route is not justified simply because it would shorten a line on the map if supporting it at Skiff speed produces poor visual/streaming quality or destroys nearby authored content.

The Line Skiff / Route Network workbench must add a portfolio/network-coverage view.

## Graph coverage

Expose:

- all semantic route segments/nodes;
- region;
- route-purpose family;
- world/layer variants;
- current availability;
- connected regional hubs/approaches;
- park/service nodes;
- expected speed envelope;
- content consumers.

## Purpose/consumer tags

Show whether each route is used by:

- ordinary transport;
- story;
- courier/service;
- Trial 05;
- Drift Knot(s);
- community project;
- repair/setup payoff;
- tool synthesis;
- late route/Unringing work.

These tags are design inspection, not completion facts.

## Hunt portfolio

For each Knot expose:

- `hunt.*` ID;
- route segment/variant set;
- eligibility;
- core route reasoning test;
- required tool relationship;
- discovery/reacquisition sector;
- changed-world fallback route;
- Hush support class;
- similarity warnings relative to other Knots.

## Audits

At minimum:

- major-region approach coverage;
- first-visit/before-memory bypass risk;
- hub high-speed intrusion risk;
- route with no safe last-mile access;
- orphan/one-off expensive route segment;
- route used only by one minor Hunt with no infrastructure rationale;
- duplicate route/function similarity;
- Hunt route unavailable under changed-world state;
- too many consecutive Knots whose primary test is speed/timing;
- tool interaction with no explicit Route Tool Interaction Contract;
- advanced route requiring Switchshoe before upgrade can be acquired;
- segment exceeding streaming/camera performance budget in evidence capture.

# 20. Deterministic Decision #5 fixtures

Future implementation/content evidence must prove at minimum:

1. natural late-game network meaningfully approaches every major region where story/world state permits;
2. network does not automatically enter/erase major social hubs at high speed;
3. at least one long familiar connector is materially faster/more expressive by Skiff than on foot;
4. route network remains useful with all Drift Knots resolved;
5. route discovery does not create a new completion category;
6. acquiring Skiff does not reveal every optional/legacy lane automatically;
7. each of 12 Knots has a valid semantic route/variant set;
8. each Knot retains deterministic reacquisition after losing signal;
9. changed-world route changes preserve each Knot's availability;
10. no Knot is auto-completed because an old route disappeared;
11. Graymile Bellwake functions as base Skiff/Cantor hunt rather than generic race;
12. Outer Loop Stutter requires the supported Switchshoe transfer relationship;
13. late Knots alter reasoning through Palinode/Mirror/Fine Fork/seam rather than only increasing speed;
14. route-tool interactions reject unsupported mounted use cleanly;
15. an In-Motion tool contract preserves Skiff/route continuity;
16. a Park-required interaction cannot be triggered at full speed as an exploit;
17. selected courier/service route content uses the same authoritative network as ordinary travel;
18. no repeatable courier grind is required for completion/progression;
19. Route Listener unlocks only at 12/12 and remains bounded to route-class completion assistance;
20. System IDE identifies routes that have become expensive one-off content islands.

---

# 21. Rejection conditions

Reject/revise implementation/content if:

- the Skiff is primarily useful only during Drift Knot hunts;
- all twelve Knots use bespoke isolated racetracks;
- the network covers essentially every ordinary path and makes on-foot traversal obsolete;
- a major region becomes a high-speed drive-through that loses its social/environmental identity;
- route discovery becomes another completion percentage;
- route network progression becomes a generic XP/level bar with no authored world reason;
- courier/service content becomes infinite required delivery grind;
- later Knots are merely the same chase at higher speed;
- cross-tool synthesis means only more button presses rather than changed reasoning;
- every tool becomes generically usable at top Skiff speed;
- an expensive route exists solely to hold one minor reward with no credible infrastructure reason;
- Skiff ownership automatically reveals all hidden maintenance lanes;
- lost Knots require long dead-road replay;
- Route Listener becomes universal radar;
- high-speed network scope outruns streaming/camera/world-production capability and degrades the remembered world it was supposed to transform.

# Research basis

Fresh design references used for this decision include:

- Hideo Kojima / PlayStation discussion of players valuing traversal infrastructure such as highways enough to engage with it as meaningful play, supporting the broader principle that infrastructure can be an enduring gameplay object rather than only transport downtime;
- GDC traversal/open-world material emphasizing that high-speed movement requires world layouts and systems designed around that speed rather than treating traversal as a bolt-on;
- existing Stillring progression, Drift Knot, completion, Roadhand, Switchshoe, world-state, Hush, and regional content authority.

These references inform design problems only. Stillring does not copy Death Stranding's construction/social systems, another game's route layout, progression economy, or side-content structure.

## Final locked statement

> **The Line Skiff network is shared world infrastructure, not a collection of Hunt racetracks. Its selective trunk/approach/spur/legacy/hazard network must remain useful for ordinary cross-region travel, story/courier/community connectivity, changed-world navigation, mastery, and optional revisits even after all Drift Knots are complete. The twelve existing Knots reuse believable network corridors where possible and escalate through different route/tool reasoning rather than speed alone. Route discovery is not a new completion checklist, courier play is not a required grind, and other tools interact with the Skiff only through explicit route-tool contracts. By late-game the network should meaningfully approach every major region without penetrating every social hub or replacing the last mile of authored on-foot exploration.**
