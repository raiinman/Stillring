# 1. Purpose

Board 04 exists to prove that Stillring has **one coherent lighting philosophy with eight regional expressions**, rather than eight environment teams inventing unrelated grading and exposure habits.

It is not a cinematic-lighting mood board.

It is not a renderer benchmark.

It is not permission to redesign the regional scripts in `docs/161`.

The board must make the following production truths visually obvious:

- ordinary daylight is readable before it is dramatic;
- dawn, dusk and night retain regional identity rather than applying global presets;
- inhabited light means work, food, shelter, maintenance and community;
- darkness creates hierarchy without hiding valid traversal, characters or hazards;
- weather changes composition and material response rather than merely adding particles;
- atmosphere separates depth and reveals/hides landmarks deliberately;
- precise resonance can use restrained cool emphasis without becoming spellcasting language;
- Hush wrongness comes from contradictory relationships, not a universal blue/purple grade;
- Stillness can communicate through reduced motion, interrupted activity and missing human light;
- post-Stillness and Unringing retain the same region while changing how light and activity are organized;
- the board remains valid even if optional post-process/VFX richness is removed.

The governing test is:

> **If the grade, particles and bloom are stripped back, the light/value structure should still tell the player where they are, what kind of state the place is in, and where attention belongs.**

# 4. Comparison discipline

## 4.1 Same-place comparisons first

When comparing time, weather, Hush or Stillness, use the **same or recognizably matched location/camera relationship** wherever practical.

A beautiful sunset in one location compared against a dark rainy alley somewhere else proves nothing about the state transition.

For a comparison strip:

- retain the landmark;
- retain broad route geometry;
- retain major material families;
- keep framing close enough that value/light changes can be judged honestly.

## 4.2 No fake numerical authority

Board captions may use relational language such as:

- brighter foreground than background;
- warmer inhabited pool against cooler exterior;
- lower-contrast distant fog layer;
- harder directional daylight;
- softer diffuse overcast;
- sparse night markers;
- stronger local shadow separation.

Do not print invented production values for:

- EV/exposure;
- lux;
- lumen/candela;
- color temperature;
- fog density;
- volumetric step count;
- shadow distance;
- bounce/GI strength;
- bloom threshold;
- light radius;
- reflection quality.

Those numbers require later renderer/target-hardware evidence.

## 4.3 Review without grade dependence

At least one review pass for every key family must include:

- grayscale/value reduction;
- saturation-reduced version;
- atmosphere/VFX reduced version where applicable.

Pass condition:

> The region, focal hierarchy and critical route/character relationship remain understandable without depending on hue or decorative effects.

## 4.4 Human-light memory test

For every ordinary-world region, Board 04 must show at least one **pre-crisis inhabited light composition** before showing catastrophe or Hush treatment.

The player must first learn what warmth, work and ordinary occupancy look like there.

A region that only receives dramatic ruined/Hush keys fails the board.

# 5. Sheet A — Time-of-Day Regional Master Band

Sheet A establishes the regional time language.

For each of the eight ordinary-world regions, show a controlled four-state band:

1. dawn / early-light transition;
2. ordinary daytime;
3. dusk / evening transition;
4. ordinary inhabited night.

Dawn and dusk are execution studies inside the regional relationships in `docs/161`; they do not create new canonical clock events.

Each region must preserve its shape language from Board 02/`docs/165` while light changes.

## 5.1 Brindle / First Circuit

Required reads:

- warm, comprehensible agricultural daylight;
- clean shadow shapes on slopes/workshop fronts;
- dusk capable of warm-gold versus deepening cool-depth contrast;
- intimate night made from windows, inn/workshop spill, relay lanterns and sparse road markers;
- no theme-park path outlining;
- Vale Workshop retains visible work hierarchy rather than becoming a single orange rectangle.

Failure modes:

- sepia nostalgia filter;
- doomed-village gloom before catastrophe;
- uniformly amber night;
- generic blue moonlight swallowing timber/stone separation.

## 5.2 Cairnspire

Required reads:

- daylight separates stacked terraces and service layers through top-light and atmospheric depth;
- dusk reveals vertical civic mass without turning the city into glowing fantasy towers;
- night reads as a coordinated network of route boards, lift landings, counters, food/service windows and maintenance corridors;
- warm human/service islands remain distinct from cooler civic stone/blue-gray masses;
- monumental fronts and practical service spaces coexist.

Failure modes:

- marble-palace glamour lighting;
- modern metropolis blanket illumination;
- every brass edge glowing;
- night visibility solved by flattening the entire city into one mid-value exposure.

## 5.3 Rootmere

Required reads:

- daylight filtered through layered canopy rather than global green fog;
- controlled warm shafts where canopy opens;
- inhabited clearings remain warmer/readable against wet vegetation;
- dusk increases water/canopy depth without losing root silhouettes;
- night uses sparse fungal/warm lantern, cooking and ferry-marker light;
- not every plant emits light.

Failure modes:

- mystical bioluminescent forest;
- uniform green grade;
- shafts used everywhere until they stop directing attention;
- water reflections busier than routes/people.

## 5.4 Saltreach

Required reads:

- daylight can move between diffuse fog and hard broken coastal light while retaining coast/marker identity;
- bright spray/water values yield to route and figure readability;
- dusk preserves signal verticals and weather protection;
- night is sparse: fog signals, harbor condition lamps, tavern/workshop light and dark useful intervals;
- reflected signal points do not double into false interaction cues.

Failure modes:

- pirate-port lantern spectacle;
- modern harbor glow;
- every wet surface mirror-bright;
- fog used to erase foreground navigation.

## 5.5 Emberstep

Required reads:

- exterior day uses hard highland sun, dark ground/light sky and strong shade structure;
- inhabited interiors contrast controlled warm work sources with cooler rest/shade zones;
- dusk reveals geothermal/foundry identity without making every surface orange;
- night geography is readable through real active heat sources, kiln doors, vents, worker lamps and cooling yards;
- inactive masses remain dark.

Failure modes:

- lava-level orange grade;
- all hot material white/yellow;
- heat glow as universal fill light;
- permanent smoky darkness.

## 5.6 High Aerie

Required reads:

- large sky participation and atmospheric depth;
- daylight gives readable cliff silhouettes and warm interior thresholds;
- dawn/dusk can emphasize exposure and distance without permanent storm drama;
- night contains sparse cliff settlements, lift/route markers and small warm civic interiors against large dark sky;
- moving cloud shadow may support ordinary motion when appropriate.

Failure modes:

- mystical monastery backlight in every frame;
- permanent thunderstorm;
- sky so bright characters/routes become silhouettes by accident;
- every rope/marker rim-lit for decoration.

## 5.7 Mireglass

Required reads:

- soft directional daylight creates broad horizontal/reflection bands while routes remain honest;
- dawn/dusk may strengthen horizon/reflection relationships without losing real geometry;
- night uses ferry lamps, domestic lantern reflections and sparse raised-path markers;
- reflections may enrich composition but cannot duplicate gameplay cues ambiguously.

Failure modes:

- dreamlike glow replacing route clarity;
- every water surface acting like a mirror;
- cyan/purple swamp night;
- random reflection distortion treated as atmosphere.

## 5.8 Cairnfall

Required reads:

- clear daylight with long instrument shadows, broad plateau visibility and strong sky participation;
- dusk demonstrates instrument silhouettes without magical glow;
- night is a first-class identity state: dark sky, restrained work lamps, readable instruments by silhouette/rim/value, credible celestial visibility;
- public observation culture should make sense visually.

Failure modes:

- magical star-projector aesthetic;
- glowing constellation machinery;
- observatory reduced to black silhouettes with neon cyan trim;
- identical day composition merely exposure-lowered for night.

# 6. Sheet B — Human Light / Interior Workspaces

This sheet proves Stillring's ordinary inhabited lighting language.

Use one representative working/social interior or sheltered workspace per region.

Recommended authority-backed subjects:

- **Brindle:** Vale Workshop or Dori's Road Inn;
- **Cairnspire:** public/service counter, Fourth Landing Commons or route-kitchen space;
- **Rootmere:** flood hall, ferry shelter or Reed-Screw work area;
- **Saltreach:** shipwright/dry-dock shelter, Split Mast work/service space or pressurewright room;
- **Emberstep:** foundry work/rest transition, clinic work area or cooling-yard shelter;
- **High Aerie:** Wind Kitchen, archive work area or lift/rigging service interior;
- **Mireglass:** reed-paper workroom, ferry shelter or domestic/interpretation space;
- **Cairnfall:** instrument workshop, Night Survey Hut or public observation work area.

Each panel must show three layers where the space supports them:

1. **task light** — where hands/tools/records/work are read;
2. **human occupancy light** — food, conversation, shelter, waiting or maintenance;
3. **deeper storage/service value** — present but subordinate.

Required principles:

- warm does not mean uniformly orange;
- practical sources have understandable placement;
- faces/hands/tools remain readable without beauty-lighting every character;
- exterior/interior transition preserves spatial comprehension;
- work materials remain distinguishable;
- ordinary light must be attractive enough that losing it later has emotional consequence.

Do not use volumetric beams as the default proof that an interior is "cinematic."

# 7. Sheet C — Weather / Atmosphere Families

Atmosphere is compositional and causal.

Each region requires at least one ordinary weather/atmosphere comparison and one reduced-atmosphere check.

## Brindle

Show:

- rain deepening soil/timber and increasing flood/drainage relevance;
- dry conditions softening vegetation saturation and increasing dust/road read;
- human light remains warm/usable in rain.

## Cairnspire

Show:

- rain revealing runoff/service gutters and darker old stone;
- haze separating vertical districts while preserving landmark recognition;
- no city-wide mirror-gloss treatment.

## Rootmere

Show:

- ordinary rain with leaf weight, runoff and water-channel activity;
- darker bark and distinct resin/waxed response;
- low/channel fog rather than one uniform forest volume.

## Saltreach

Show:

- fog hiding/revealing known vertical markers while preserving foreground routes;
- storm depth compression with near-field spray kept subordinate to gameplay silhouettes;
- wet protected/unprotected materials separating correctly.

## Emberstep

Show:

- ash/dust accumulating and softening distance without coating everything equally;
- controlled heat shimmer only where useful;
- one rare-wetness material comparison may be included to demonstrate how ash/glass/ceramic separate under changed conditions.

## High Aerie

Show:

- wind through coherent environmental response: cloth, rope, vegetation, cloud, loose environmental material;
- storm compression without route loss;
- not every object moving at equal priority.

## Mireglass

Show:

- mist creating layered horizontal depth;
- rain breaking reflection symmetry and sometimes clarifying real surfaces;
- local ripple/wind behavior revealing actual water planes.

## Cairnfall

Show:

- dust revealing wind direction and softening distant plateau edges;
- cloud cover changing observation light/social use without erasing instrument silhouettes.

For all regions:

- VFX-A atmospheric effects are removable richness, not gameplay ownership;
- a low-density/reduced-effects frame must retain the environmental read;
- particles may not become the sole signal of wind, danger, route or Hush state.

# 8. Sheet D — Precise Resonance / Hush / Stillness / Unringing

This sheet prevents the project's abnormal states from collapsing into generic fantasy lighting.

## 8.1 Precise resonance

Show at least two bounded examples of resonance/precision presentation tied to real machinery or tool relations.

Requirements:

- restrained pale cool/cyan emphasis is allowed where supported by authority;
- source relationship remains mechanical/diagnostic;
- light/material response begins at an understandable object or connection;
- shape/rhythm/mechanical state carries meaning with color;
- no free-floating spell aura;
- no sacred beam from the sky merely because resonance is important;
- no bloom dependence.

The purpose is to establish **precision**, not magic.

## 8.2 Hush comparison set

Use at least four regions that demonstrate different Hush lighting relationships. The full execution package should eventually cover all regions through their regional key sets.

Minimum examples should include:

- **Brindle:** warm light with implausible occupancy/use relation; familiar wear relationship wrong;
- **Cairnspire:** locally precise lights/clocks/landings whose coordination contradicts adjacent systems;
- **High Aerie:** expected movement/light relationship interrupted; stillness can do more work than added effects;
- **Mireglass or Cairnfall:** reflected/shadow/measurement relationship contradicts the visible condition.

Hush rules:

- regional palette survives;
- landmark recognition survives;
- gameplay readability survives;
- wrongness may come from source direction, occupancy, reflection, timing or material relation;
- post-process is support only;
- no global purple/blue grade;
- no chromatic-aberration/VHS/glitch identity;
- no constant particles added just to prove the Hush is active.

## 8.3 Stillness comparison set

Stillness must be compared to a learned ordinary state.

Show at minimum:

- ordinary active state;
- Stillness consequence;
- changed-world active pocket.

The comparison should use absence/interruption where appropriate:

- fewer continuous human-light networks;
- stalled machine light;
- missing wind/motion relationship;
- isolated active pockets;
- surface/motion disagreement;
- practical emergency lighting rather than universal catastrophe color.

Stillness is not "everything darker."

## 8.4 Unringing / epilogue relationship

Show at least two examples where recovery becomes more distributed rather than simply brighter.

Possible written-authority expressions include:

- multiple small signals in Brindle;
- warm local-service islands and paper-route activity in Cairnspire;
- local observation/condition signals in Rootmere/Saltreach;
- locally managed heat/provenance in Emberstep;
- independent wind/route condition markers in High Aerie;
- labeled memory/route markers in Mireglass;
- multiple preserved calibration readings in Cairnfall.

The key visual change is **organization and plurality**, not victory-gold color grading.

# 10. Source-reference baskets

External references are selected to answer a lighting problem, not to define Stillring's style.

Every collected source must use the extraction protocol in `docs/155`:

- source/artist/work identifier;
- production question;
- extracted principle;
- Stillring translation;
- forbidden carryover.

## 10.1 Natural-light basket

Useful source categories:

- hill/orchard dawn and overcast;
- vertical stone city top-light and reflected service-alley light;
- canopy shafts and wet woodland ambient light;
- coastal fog/broken sun;
- highland hard light/deep shade;
- mountain atmospheric depth/cloud shadow;
- wetland horizon/reflection light;
- dry plateau daylight and astronomical night.

Extract:

- value structure;
- shadow shape;
- depth separation;
- material response;
- source direction.

Do not copy a specific composition or color grade.

## 10.2 Working-interior basket

Useful real-world subjects:

- workshops;
- repair sheds;
- kitchens;
- ferry/harbor workspaces;
- foundries with controlled safe viewing;
- archives;
- observatories/instrument rooms;
- covered markets/service counters.

Extract:

- task-light hierarchy;
- source placement logic;
- face/hand/tool readability;
- warm/cool transitions;
- storage depth.

Do not import modern fixtures or historical ornament one-to-one unless independently justified by Stillring's world.

## 10.3 Weather/atmosphere basket

Useful photographic/film/painting study categories:

- fog depth layering;
- rain material response;
- coastal spray;
- ash/dust distance softening;
- wind-driven cloud shadow;
- wetland mist/reflection disruption;
- high-altitude haze;
- night visibility with sparse practical lights.

Extract physical/compositional behavior, not a recognizable shot.

## 10.4 Stylized-lighting basket

Illustration/game/film references may be used to study:

- selective value compression;
- exaggerated foreground/background separation;
- restrained saturation hierarchy;
- silhouette support;
- warm human-light memory;
- readable night without photographic flattening.

A commercial game's LUT, night color, bloom treatment or Hush-like state is never a direct target.

## 10.5 Abnormal-state basket

Prefer real optical/physical relationship studies before fictional "glitch world" references:

- reflection/source disagreement;
- still air versus moving cloud/water;
- mismatched wear;
- repeated shadow forms;
- preserved old paint/material states;
- absent occupancy in active infrastructure.

The goal is believable wrongness built from relationships.
