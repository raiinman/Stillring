## Decision question

Should the nine Cantor Patterns remain a completion-bearing category, and what exactly does it mean to "collect" an operational procedure without turning it into a passive lore pickup or a renamed song list?

## Fresh research conclusion

**Keep all 9 Cantor Patterns as completion-bearing learned procedures.**

The current catalog already earns this count: every pattern has a distinct operational relationship and at least several authored external uses. They are not nine keys for nine locks and they are not melodies the player memorizes.

The critical completion distinction is simple: **learning/acquiring the canonical operational procedure counts. Repeated use, mastery scores, or opening the catalog page do not.**

## Locked philosophy

> **A Pattern counts when Neris learns a reusable relationship, not when the player clears a menu badge.**

## Shipping target — 9

The existing nine-pattern catalog is **CONFIRMED**:

1. P01 Common Measure — optional / strongly surfaced;
2. P02 Hollow Measure — optional;
3. P03 Breath Ledger — optional;
4. P04 Temper Cycle — optional;
5. P05 Roadhand Pulse — story-required for Line Skiff use;
6. P06 Crosswind Measure — story-required within High Aerie after acquisition;
7. P07 Palinode Pattern — story-required;
8. P08 Witness Interval — optional;
9. P09 Local Accord — optional / strongly relevant to community projects.

Thus the current completion split is **3 story-required + 6 optional**. Story-required Pattern obligations count automatically when their learning transaction occurs; completionists must seek the six optional procedures.

P01 remains deliberately optional rather than being retroactively declared mandatory simply because it is useful. If later story implementation truly requires it, that is explicit progression change control rather than completion inference.

## Learning / acquisition semantics

A Pattern is learned when the player completes its authored teaching/discovery/calibration event and the Cantor Key gains the stable operational procedure.

Learning requires more than touching a pickup:
- a teacher, record, machine, or evidence source establishes the relationship;
- Neris/Key performs or validates a short authored calibration/understanding step where appropriate;
- the procedure becomes available in the Cantor selector/catalog;
- one durable `pattern.<slug>` learned fact settles.

The completion obligation is satisfied at that durable learning transaction.

Not required for 100%:
- using the Pattern N times;
- finding every compatible target;
- reading its catalog description;
- achieving a performance score;
- manually replaying a memorized note sequence.

## Pattern versus critical-path verb

Major verbs such as Anchor Line, Glasslung Reed, Temper Gauntlet, Line Skiff, Vane Cloak, Palinode/Mirror/seam capabilities remain their own progression/tool/world authority.

A Cantor Pattern is a **procedure applied through Cantor** to interpret or coordinate compatible relationships. It may work with a major verb but does not secretly replace that verb.

Examples:
- Breath Ledger records/represents pressure history; Glasslung physically manipulates pressure.
- Crosswind Measure diagnoses air/structure disagreement; Vane changes Neris's relationship to airflow.
- Temper Cycle tracks/coordinates nonuniform heating; Temper Gauntlet performs physical heat transfer.

Completion must not count one story scene twice as both "Pattern" and "Tool Mastery Upgrade" unless two genuinely distinct durable capabilities are awarded.

## Pattern versus Tool Mastery Upgrade

Patterns alter **operational knowledge/procedure** available through Cantor.

Tool Mastery Upgrades alter a tool's **physical capability/handling/possibility**.

If an authored reward can be described equally well as either one, its category is unresolved and must be fixed before implementation.

No upgrade may be duplicated as a Pattern merely to increase completion totals.

## Utility requirement

Every shipping Pattern retains the catalog's existing rule: **at least three meaningful authored external uses** outside its acquisition scene.

At least one use should occur outside the Pattern's home region/quest where practical, demonstrating that it is a reusable part of Neris's professional vocabulary rather than a local lock code.

Optional Patterns may make optional content more legible, efficient, or combinatorially rich, but ordinary critical-path completion cannot assume all six optional Patterns.

## Input / clean-room boundary

Patterns remain operational procedures selected from known options and followed through contextual tuning.

Forbidden expression:
- memorized musical-note strings;
- copied song-button sequences;
- magic tune as arbitrary lock key;
- requirement that players reproduce audio pitch from memory;
- copyrighted melody/rhythm resemblance used as identity.

The pattern UI presents project-owned semantic names, relationships, compatible-target feedback, and accessible visual/text representations.

## Accessibility

Pattern use may include audio/haptic feedback, but critical state cannot rely solely on it.

Required support:
- visual/text equivalent for resonant relationship and success/failure;
- non-color-only status communication;
- remappable/contextual controls;
- timing windows adjustable where accessibility policy permits without changing semantic result;
- catalog descriptions readable/scalable;
- no required pitch discrimination or melody reproduction;
- rejection reason clear when a Pattern is incompatible with the target/state.

## Missability / recovery

Completion-bearing optional Patterns must remain obtainable through the completion sweep.

If the original teacher/source becomes inaccessible:
- an archive/refuge/changed-world source may preserve the same procedure;
- the semantic Pattern ID remains unchanged;
- recovery cannot award a duplicate learned state/reward.

A story phase transition may change **how** P02/P03/etc. are learned, not silently make 100% impossible.

## Persistence ownership

Learned Pattern state belongs to **Inventory & Tool Progression / Player Progression** under the locked save taxonomy. Completion Ledger derives the nine Pattern obligations from those settled learned facts.

Rules:
- stable `pattern.<slug>` ID;
- one learned fact per procedure;
- selector order/UI slot is representation, not identity;
- learned state survives save/load/migration;
- downstream world/quest effects remain owned by their systems;
- mandatory Pattern learning counts automatically without separate completion mutation;
- no repeated-use counters influence 100%.

## Pattern / Completion System IDE

The workbench must expose:
- Pattern semantic ID;
- mandatory/optional classification;
- source/teacher/gate;
- learned state;
- Completion Ledger state;
- compatible-system relationship tags;
- authored-use references/count;
- accessibility feedback channels;
- acquisition/recovery transaction;
- collision with Tool Mastery IDs or duplicated-capability validation.

Required fixtures:
1. unlearned optional Pattern;
2. normal teacher-based acquisition;
3. changed-world recovery acquisition;
4. mandatory P05/P06/P07 automatically counted;
5. learned Pattern never opened in catalog still counts;
6. repeated use does not change completion;
7. incompatible target gives semantic rejection reason;
8. audio disabled / visual equivalent still usable;
9. save/load learned state;
10. 8/9 completion audit identifies missing Pattern.

## Red-team / rejection conditions

Reject a Pattern if:
- it exists only for one acquisition-scene lock;
- it is really a physical Tool Mastery Upgrade;
- it copies musical-input expression from another franchise;
- optional acquisition becomes silently mandatory for critical-path progression;
- completion requires repetitive use/practice count;
- learning depends on permanently missable source with no recovery;
- critical feedback is pitch/audio-only;
- it is added merely to preserve the number nine.
