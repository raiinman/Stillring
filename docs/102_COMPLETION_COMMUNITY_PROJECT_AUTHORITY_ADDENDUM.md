## Decision question

Do the six existing Community Disconnection Projects all deserve completion-bearing status, and how do we make them feel like six distinct local futures rather than a checklist of regional towers to deactivate after the story already told the player centralization was bad?

## Fresh research conclusion

**Keep all 6 existing Community Disconnection Projects.**

They survive individual review because each region solves a different practical problem created by leaving Meridian-style central correction:
- Rootmere distributes living reference authority;
- Saltreach coordinates warnings without one clock;
- Emberstep shares safety information without forcing identical kiln cycles;
- High Aerie distributes records without selecting one master copy;
- Mireglass makes selected natural seams civic memory spaces while closing genuinely unsafe ones;
- Cairnfall turns correction infrastructure into honest disagreement reporting.

Each project is one completion-bearing regional resolution. Its prerequisite contracts, records, Patterns, tools, and other completion-bearing facts keep their own category identities and do **not** gain extra completion weight merely because this project checks them.

The decisive rule is:

> **A community project counts when a region can keep functioning differently—not when Neris flips the regional off switch.**

## Locked philosophy

> **Disconnect the command. Preserve the capability. Let the place choose what replaces it.**

## Individual project validation

### D01 — Many Roots, Many Bells
**Regional problem:** one state-linked bell-oak reference concentrates authority and fragility.  
**Resolution:** distribute reference across several living resonators maintained by keeper families while allowing agreed drift.  
**Why it survives:** it is a load/tolerance design problem tied to Rootmere's living infrastructure, not a generic shutdown.

### D02 — Fog Without Meridian
**Regional problem:** coastal warning depends on a single capital timing signal.  
**Resolution:** establish overlapping manual fog-tower and pressure-bell warning windows, then physically validate a ship route.  
**Why it survives:** coordination and redundancy replace central timing without pretending independent systems never disagree.

### D03 — Maker's Clock
**Regional problem:** foundry safety coordination has been conflated with identical standardized kiln cycles.  
**Resolution:** preserve independent local heat cycles while sharing practical shift/heat warnings and repurposing selected state hardware.  
**Why it survives:** it requires thermal/material understanding and creates a distinct industrial future.

### D04 — Open Copies
**Regional problem:** one master archive makes contradiction look like corruption.  
**Resolution:** distribute copy sets through actual courier routes and annotate conflicts rather than choosing a single winner.  
**Why it survives:** physical distribution and contradiction literacy matter; this is not merely "upload archive copies."

### D05 — Public Seams
**Regional problem:** natural Hush seams are either hidden or treated as defects to erase.  
**Resolution:** stabilize safe civic memory/mourning seams, establish use rules with residents, and close one genuinely unsafe seam.  
**Why it survives:** the project distinguishes preservation from negligence. It is not an ideological "never close anything" meter.

### D06 — Report the Drift
**Regional problem:** Cairnfall's observatory reports disagreement only in order to correct it.  
**Resolution:** disconnect correction actuators while preserving observation instruments and publish raw ranges to other regions.  
**Why it survives:** it transforms an existing measurement system's social purpose rather than destroying useful infrastructure.

## Critical-path versus 100% boundary

The canonical story may assume that after Cairnfall, communities have begun taking local initiative. That world movement is **not conditional on the player completing all six projects**.

Locked boundary:
- critical-path story progression does not require six project completions;
- ordinary changed-world NPC behavior may show communities starting provisional local work independently;
- 100% requires Neris to participate in and fully resolve all six authored projects;
- completion enriches preparedness, services, specificity, and credits-state detail without creating a separate "good ending";
- an incomplete project does not freeze its region in pre-Cairnfall stasis waiting for the player.

This preserves the project's existing rule that the world changes while Neris is elsewhere.

## Completion event

Each community project exposes one stable completion item:

`community.<region>.<slug>`

It becomes satisfied only when the project's authored **operational acceptance state** settles: the replacement/local system has been built/configured and proven through its required field test or equivalent final action.

Completion is not derived from:
- unlocking the project;
- satisfying its prerequisites;
- talking to the initiating NPC;
- delivering the first component;
- disconnecting one central device;
- watching the completion scene;
- receiving the convenience reward.

The local replacement must actually function.

## Prerequisites and double-counting

Projects may depend on already-existing category facts such as Repair Contracts, Cantor Patterns, records, story verbs, or regional story state.

Those prerequisites are **gates, not additional project percentage pieces**.

Example:
- Rootmere contracts C07–C09 can retain their own Repair Contract completion identity;
- their use as prerequisites for D01 does not cause them to count again inside the Community Project category;
- D01 contributes exactly one community-project completion item when its own operational resolution settles.

A project journal may show prerequisite progress for clarity without implying extra global weight.

## Optional convenience requirements

Existing optional relationships remain optional:
- D01 may become easier with an Anchor mastery upgrade but cannot require it;
- D02 Local Accord may improve convenience/result quality but is not required for initial valid project success;
- D06 Local Accord is recommended but not mandatory.

No project may silently turn an optional upgrade/Setup-Payoff outcome into mandatory power unless a later explicit authority revision changes that requirement.

Different convenience variants may alter service quality, dialogue, staging, or credits specificity; they do not create multiple completion tiers for the same project.

## No hidden morality meter

Community projects are authored civic/mechanical resolutions, not six morality votes feeding a secret score.

Rules:
- no hidden benevolence/obedience/rebellion meter;
- no aggregate "correct politics" score selecting the ending;
- project success is based on whether the authored local system safely functions under its stated principles;
- where residents discuss tradeoffs, dialogue can preserve disagreement without converting every conversation into a branching karma transaction;
- one canonical ending remains authoritative.

## Anti-tower-shutdown / anti-project-bloat authority

A project must contain meaningful **replacement work**, not only removal of Meridian/state hardware.

Every project must include at least:
1. understanding the regional operational problem;
2. configuring/building/distributing a local replacement relationship;
3. a playable validation step proving the replacement works;
4. persistent changed-world consequence.

Forbidden filler:
- six copies of "climb tower and press deactivate";
- N generic material collection from repeatable nodes;
- parcel chains with no playable problem;
- arbitrary construction currency;
- waiting real-world build timers;
- repeating existing contracts solely to fill a project bar;
- clearing map icons whose contents have no relationship to the civic change.

## Failure / abandonment / recovery

Projects are multi-step world activities and must be recoverable.

Rules:
- settled prerequisite facts never roll back because a later project step fails;
- local mechanical validation failure resets to a nearby safe project state;
- project-specific parts/data cannot be permanently sold, destroyed, or lost;
- abandoning and returning restores the current meaningful work state or a documented changed-world equivalent;
- partial construction/configuration persists where semantically appropriate;
- no project becomes permanently unavailable because the main story advanced;
- completion/reward settlement is idempotent.

## Changed-world persistence

Community projects are explicitly about world change, so their result must persist visibly beyond the completion popup.

Required persistent consequences include an appropriate mix of:
- changed infrastructure state;
- NPC work/routines/dialogue;
- regional service change;
- route/world affordance;
- project-specific journal/world-state facts;
- credits-state detail.

A resolved project remains resolved through save/load, fast travel, region streaming, and later story phases.

If later world events materially alter the site, the new state must be derived from the resolved project rather than reverting to a generic default.

## Rewards

The existing contextual rewards remain valid:
- Rootmere local service shortcut;
- Saltreach harbor travel service;
- Emberstep advanced forge service;
- High Aerie lore-assist service;
- Mireglass seam travel convenience;
- Cairnfall late completion-assist upgrade;
- credits-state expansion/details.

Rules:
- service/reward settles once;
- rewards reinforce what the community built rather than dropping unrelated rare loot;
- no randomized reward table;
- replay/revalidation cannot duplicate progression;
- credits changes remain specificity/consequence, not a separate ending grade.

D06's late completion-assist concept remains bounded to **unresolved category counts by subregion** until the later dedicated completion-assist/UI decision defines exact presentation. This decision does not expand it into exact treasure radar.

## Accessibility / objective clarity

Multi-step civic projects can impose memory/attention load even when none of their individual controls are difficult. Accessibility therefore includes **objective clarity**, not only button remapping.

Current Microsoft accessibility guidance specifically recommends that players can review current tasks/objectives and ongoing progress, especially in complex worlds with prerequisite chains. Stillring adopts that problem-solving principle without turning the journal into an answer key.

Required support:
- current project objective and completed major steps reviewable at any time;
- prerequisite facts shown clearly when the player has already discovered the relevant relationship;
- objective text distinguishes "find information," "configure system," and "validate result" rather than vague progress bars;
- all mechanical inputs follow existing remapping/hold-toggle/accessibility authority;
- state distinctions are not color-only or audio-only;
- timed validation may use approved timing accommodations;
- multi-step tasks can be resumed after long absence with a concise factual recap;
- accessibility changes never reduce project completion/reward authority.

Guidance may clarify what Neris currently knows. It must not reveal hidden puzzle solutions, undiscovered testimony contents, or privileged world metadata.

## Persistence ownership

Quest/Community/World State owns project progress and operational resolution.

Persist:
- stable project semantic ID;
- unlocked/available state;
- resolved prerequisite references;
- project step/state necessary for recovery;
- authored local replacement configuration where player/state variation is meaningful;
- operational validation result;
- resolved fact;
- service/reward idempotency;
- credits/world-pulse consequence facts.

Completion Ledger derives each `community.*` item from the settled operational resolution.

Do not derive project completion from UI checklist percentage, NPC dialogue playback, or central-device destruction alone.

## Community / World / Completion System IDE

The shared workbench must expose:
- semantic project ID / region;
- availability and prerequisite facts with owners;
- current project phase;
- meaningful replacement-system state;
- local configuration/tolerance values where relevant;
- validation predicates and exact rejection/failure reasons;
- optional convenience influences separated from required facts;
- world-state variant;
- service/reward settlement;
- credits/world-pulse output;
- Completion Ledger derivation;
- duplicate prerequisite weighting validator.

Required fixtures:
1. project unavailable before Cairnfall/required regional gates;
2. prerequisites partially met with clear status;
3. project begun, save/load, resume exact meaningful phase;
4. local validation failure and nearby retry;
5. completion with optional convenience upgrade absent;
6. completion with convenience relationship present → same completion authority;
7. accessibility-modified mechanical validation → same completion/reward;
8. project resolved, leave/reload/return → persistent changed region;
9. changed-world variant after later story pulse;
10. prerequisite completion item counted once globally, not again inside project;
11. project completion reward/service settles once;
12. 5/6 audit identifies exact unresolved region;
13. main story reaches ending with zero/some community projects and remains canonically valid;
14. all six projects enrich final world/credits without selecting a different ending.

## Red-team / rejection conditions

Revise a Community Project if:
- it is primarily a tower/off-switch interaction;
- it duplicates another region's gameplay with renamed props;
- it becomes generic material or currency grind;
- its prerequisites are counted twice toward global completion;
- an optional mastery/Setup-Payoff outcome becomes an unadvertised hard requirement;
- main story completion requires all six;
- incomplete projects freeze communities as if they cannot act without Neris;
- it feeds a hidden morality/ending score;
- a story phase can permanently brick it;
- save/load loses partial meaningful state or duplicates the service reward;
- objective complexity relies on player memory with no reviewable recap;
- accessibility settings disable completion/reward;
- it survives only to preserve the number six.
