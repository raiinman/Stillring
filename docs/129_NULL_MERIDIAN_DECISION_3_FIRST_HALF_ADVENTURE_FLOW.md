# 129 — Null Meridian Decision #3 — First-Half Adventure Flow

**Status:** APPROVED / LOCKED OWNER AUTHORITY  
**Updated:** 2026-09-05  
**Issue:** #11 — Define Null Meridian synthesis trial matrix  
**Decision:** #3 — first-half dungeon flow, S1/S2 simplification, worker-layer reveal, S3 Quiet Court access, combat restraint, failure/recovery and IDE fixtures

## Decision question

How should the first half of Null Meridian turn the six-sequence architecture and the one-governing-dungeon idea into an actual memorable adventure rather than a row of system exams?

## Locked answer

The first half follows the experiential progression:

> **monument → machine → workplace → buried history**

The player first experiences Null Meridian as monumental and nearly sacred, then discovers the physical machine beneath that image, then the ordinary human labor spaces that kept it operating, then the institutional history that was corrected out of public view.

Sequence identities remain synthesis-based, but retail experience must not feel like `Puzzle 1 → Puzzle 2 → Puzzle 3`.

The governing first-half progression is:

1. **Arrival / monumental shell** — brief observation and spatial ownership before challenge;
2. **S1 — The Hanging Walk** — forced synchronized movement is physically destructive;
3. **S2 — The Equal House** — forced standardized operating state breaks unlike machines;
4. **maintenance lift / worker layer** — solving S1+S2 restores ordinary service infrastructure rather than removing abstract magical barriers;
5. **worker-life breathing interval** — meal room, tools, shift boards, repairs, banal human residue;
6. **S3 — The Door That Was There** — official history and worker history disagree about whether a service door existed;
7. **Quiet Court archive** — the player physically enters buried institutional history through the locally restored historical doorway.

S1 and S2 remain order-flexible within the first service layer. Both must be settled before the maintenance descent becomes available.

Combat is deliberately restrained through this first half. Null Meridian begins by asking the player to understand the place. Hostile/adversarial pressure escalates only after the Quiet Court threshold.

---

# 1. Governing player memory

The first half must be remembered as one place with one idea becoming progressively more human and disturbing.

Target player memories:

- **S1:** “Those huge walkways were tearing themselves apart because Meridian would not let them move independently.”
- **S2:** “The machines worked when I stopped making all of them run the same way.”
- **S3:** “The official plans said there was no door, but workers used one for years—and I walked through its remembered state.”

Reject first-half implementation if normal players instead describe it primarily as:

- the Anchor/Vane room;
- the Glasslung/Temper room;
- the Palinode/Mirror room;
- three colored tool locks;
- a sequence of abstract synchronization meters.

Decision #2's one-sentence mental-model test remains mandatory.

---

# 2. Arrival — let the place exist before testing the player

Null Meridian should not attack the player with a mechanic immediately on entry.

The arrival establishes:

- monumental civic-sacred architecture;
- repeating visual rhythm;
- enormous central scale;
- apparently perfect simultaneous movement;
- distant indications that the structure exists partly in Hush;
- no immediate tutorial prompt or scoreboard framing.

The player receives a short bounded observation/traversal interval using ordinary movement and camera ownership.

The first legible defect is small relative to the architecture:

> a support or service joint strains every time several large assemblies move at exactly the same instant.

This is the first physical clue that the apparent perfection is itself harmful.

No character needs to state the theme.

The environment introduces it.

# 3. S1 — The Hanging Walk

**Semantic sequence ID:** `nullmeridian.sequence.hanging_walk`  
**Primary established verbs:** Anchor Line + Vane Cloak  
**Supporting guaranteed procedure:** Crosswind Measure may clarify but may not be required to understand the basic problem  
**Retail label:** may be absent; “The Hanging Walk” is production shorthand unless later narrative naming explicitly adopts it

## One-sentence player-facing problem

> **These walkways are being forced to move together, and they are tearing themselves apart. Let them move separately.**

## Physical setup

Three visibly different worker/service walks or suspended bays are coupled to one central movement rhythm despite having different:

- mass/load;
- wind exposure;
- damage/wear;
- support geometry.

The central relation drags all three through one synchronized motion.

Consequences are visible without Cantor:

- one walk snaps/jerks at its supports;
- another bows or sheds debris;
- one repeatedly loses a useful wind pocket;
- cable/load marks show overstrain.

## Player action

The player uses an established Anchor-compatible service/load point to release or redirect one forced relationship.

This does **not** introduce a new Anchor operating mode.

The changed structural relationship causes that suspended walk to move according to its own lawful load/wind response.

That changed motion exposes or restores an already-understood authored airflow route.

Vane is then used to cross.

Core causal chain:

```text
forced shared motion
→ Anchor changes a real load relationship
→ walkway gains lawful independent movement
→ useful airflow/traversal condition becomes available
→ Vane crossing
```

Anchor and Vane therefore matter to one physical problem rather than appearing as sequential key sockets.

## Crosswind Measure role

Crosswind Measure may:

- confirm that structure motion and airflow are no longer destructively coupled;
- help a returning player understand a less obvious second-stage variation;
- provide accessible redundant state feedback.

It may not be the only way to discover the first-stage solution.

## Complexity ceiling

Do **not** require finale-only concepts such as:

- calculating phase lag;
- matching an invisible ideal offset;
- reading a new structural-synchronization meter;
- learning that a particular numerical lag produces Vane lift.

The immediate rule remains ordinary:

> this structure should not be forced to move exactly with the others.

## Optional mastery recognition

Optional upgrades may enrich the route only.

Examples:

- `upgrade.anchor.split_cleat` may hold two reviewed static braces for a faster/high worker route;
- `upgrade.anchor.brake_reel` may allow cleaner recovery/catch behavior on an alternate route;
- `upgrade.vane.crossstay` may support a tighter optional lateral traversal;
- `upgrade.vane.storm_stitching` may open a dirty/intermittent-gust side balcony or optional record.

No optional upgrade may be necessary for the critical crossing.

## Failure and recovery

Wrong or incomplete structural handling must visibly worsen or fail to improve the relationship.

If the player attempts an unsafe/unusable airflow crossing:

- use an authored lower worker-safety catwalk, catch volume, or equivalent ordinary service recovery where spatially credible;
- avoid lethal punishment for a normal first misunderstanding unless the player deliberately enters a clearly lethal hazard;
- return access to the active stage through a short worker lift/stair/route;
- preserve already-settled earlier stages.

No distant checkpoint replay.

No generic “incorrect” message.

# 4. S2 — The Equal House

**Semantic sequence ID:** `nullmeridian.sequence.equal_house`  
**Primary established verbs:** Glasslung Reed + Temper Gauntlet  
**Optional supporting Patterns:** Breath Ledger / Temper Cycle if learned  
**Retail label:** production shorthand unless later adopted diegetically

## One-sentence player-facing problem

> **Meridian is forcing three different machines to run the same way. Put each one back into the condition it actually needs.**

## Physical setup

The service area contains three clearly different industrial functions. Current production direction:

1. pressure reservoir / pressure service unit;
2. cooling or service chamber;
3. heated maintenance bed / thermal service component.

They are not three copies of one puzzle prop.

A central standardizing regulator has driven unlike systems toward one shared operating condition.

The failure should be readable before tool use:

- one system is visibly overpressurized;
- one cannot shed heat;
- one cannot remain warm enough to perform its intended work;
- worker paint/inspection marks show different normal bands or handling notes.

## Player action

The player uses already-established tool relationships:

- Glasslung changes pressure/flow where its existing authority permits;
- Temper changes thermal/material state where its existing authority permits.

The puzzle does not introduce a new hidden pressure/thermal topology model.

The important learning is not a new equation. It is recognizing that unlike machines require unlike safe states.

## Successful state

The final room is visibly heterogeneous.

Illustrative outcome only:

- one machine remains hot;
- one is cool;
- one retains useful pressure;
- another is relieved.

The exact final values remain implementation/tuning work, but the semantic invariant is locked:

> **success is safe local function, not numerical sameness.**

Never require all gauges to match.

Never resolve the puzzle through a master “all green” synchronization panel.

## Worker legibility

Technicians maintained this place for years.

Therefore critical operating information should exist through believable human artifacts where appropriate:

- painted working marks;
- worn valve positions;
- thermal discoloration;
- pressure flags/gauges;
- repair notes;
- inspection scratches;
- physical vent response.

Cantor may deepen diagnosis, but ordinary physical affordance must carry the basic puzzle model.

## Optional mastery recognition

Optional capabilities may shorten, enrich, or open secondary handling:

- `pattern.breath_ledger` may make pressure history more legible;
- `pattern.temper_cycle` may make intentionally uneven heat behavior easier to interpret;
- `upgrade.glasslung.metered_valve` may permit finer optional handling;
- `upgrade.glasslung.twin_chamber` may shorten a transfer path;
- `upgrade.temper.bimetal_palm` may preserve two reviewed thermal regions for an alternate solution;
- `upgrade.temper.quench_collar` may open a fragile optional worker casing/record route without damaging it.

The Story Minimum fixture must complete the critical route with none of these.

## Failure and recovery

Worker safety systems make common mistakes informative rather than punitive.

Possible reviewed behavior:

- overpressure opens a mechanical relief path into a visible safe trench/vent;
- excessive heat triggers an interlock or visibly drops a component out of working alignment;
- a bad local state stops the specific machine rather than resetting the entire room.

The player should see **what happened** and correct it.

No finite campaign resource is consumed by experimentation.

No whole-dungeon reset.

# 5. S1 + S2 dependency settlement

S1 and S2 are sibling problems in the first service layer and may be completed in either order.

Their settlement facts remain independent.

Conceptual facts:

- `nullmeridian.sequence.hanging_walk.settled`
- `nullmeridian.sequence.equal_house.settled`

Exact persistence owner/schema follows the final Issue #11 state-contract decision and existing Save/World-State authority; these strings are semantic design identities, not permission to invent a second hidden save model.

When both are settled, an ordinary maintenance system becomes usable.

Preferred expression:

> **a worker maintenance lift / service route starts functioning because its supporting movement and service systems are now locally operable.**

Forbidden expression:

- two magical seals disappear;
- a boss door lights two icons;
- a UI says `2/2 TRIALS COMPLETE`;
- six regional symbols illuminate.

The consequence should feel like repaired infrastructure.

# 6. The descent — sacred shell becomes workplace

The restored maintenance route takes Neris behind the monumental public-facing architecture.

This is a deliberate pacing transition.

The environment should reveal ordinary labor architecture already established in finale/regional authority:

- worker bypasses;
- maintenance lifts;
- pressure service rooms;
- emergency release valves;
- meal stations;
- shift boards;
- inspection marks;
- confiscated or nonstandard fittings/tools;
- repeated repairs accumulated over many years.

## Required breathing interval

Do not immediately convert the worker layer into another puzzle.

Provide a bounded low-pressure exploration interval.

Candidate environmental details already aligned with canon:

- mugs left at a meal table;
- a half-finished card game;
- a note or scratched complaint that somebody never cleans/returns a valve wrench;
- mismatched repair methods on one service door;
- ordinary handwriting on shift/inspection boards;
- worn informal shortcuts that ceremonial plans omit.

The point is not comic relief for its own sake.

The player should understand without a speech:

> **this supposedly godlike system was built and kept alive by workers.**

Human-scale architecture becomes more important as the player approaches institutional history.

# 7. S3 — The Door That Was There

**Semantic sequence ID:** `nullmeridian.sequence.door_that_was_there`  
**Primary established verbs:** Palinode + Mirror Nail  
**Primary story transition:** worker layer → Quiet Court archive

## One-sentence player-facing problem

> **The official plans say there was never a door here. Worker records say there was. Find what happened and use the old doorway.**

## Setup

Neris reaches an apparently solid archive/service wall.

Nearby physical/record evidence supports a contradiction:

### Official history

The section is recorded as continuously sealed or as never having contained a service opening.

### Worker history

Shift/maintenance evidence shows an actual service door was used for a meaningful period.

Neither side is presented as a cartoon “fake timeline” versus “true timeline.”

Palinode exposes incompatible historical states and the evidence relationship between them.

## Player action

Mirror Nail does not rewrite global history.

At an explicitly compatible local state node, Neris temporarily commits/holds the historical doorway expression long enough to use the physical opening.

Core causal chain:

```text
ordinary worker evidence creates suspicion
→ Palinode exposes two incompatible histories
→ doorway state becomes a legitimate local historical possibility
→ Mirror Nail temporarily commits the useful doorway locally
→ Neris physically walks through
→ local temporary state may later release under existing Mirror authority
```

The player does not select “which timeline is true.”

The player uses a historically supported local state while preserving knowledge that the later sealed-wall history also existed.

## Why the door matters

The doorway is not a decorative side secret.

It is the physical transition into the Quiet Court archive.

The institution's buried history is therefore reached through a service access that institutional history itself corrected away.

This is the mechanical/story payoff of the first-half progression.

## Forbidden forms

Reject S3 if it becomes:

- a journal trivia quiz;
- “choose the true timeline”;
- a glowing Mirror portal unrelated to physical evidence;
- a generic Hush wall toggle;
- a quest marker pointing directly to `SECRET ARCHIVE` before the contradiction is understood;
- mandatory `Witness Etch` or `Duplex Head` dependence.

Optional Mirror/record capabilities may expose side evidence or alternate archive niches only.

# 8. Quiet Court archive reveal cadence

The archive critical path establishes essential history without becoming an exposition dump or villain manifesto.

The reveal progression should move approximately:

1. genuine Meridian accidents/harm;
2. grieving-family pressure for honest investigation;
3. worker/engineer objections and practical reform attempts;
4. early decentralization/non-master proposals;
5. Ilyra and Maelor working on real technical problems, including correction/disagreement;
6. evidence that preservation/continuity logic increasingly dominates;
7. language gradually shifting from human names and circumstances toward abstractions such as continuity/state loss/acceptable variance;
8. coercive institutional behavior becoming legible as an evolution, not as “they were evil all along.”

Exact document/record placements remain narrative/content work, but the ethical arc is locked by existing finale authority.

## Presentation discipline

Prefer:

- ordinary forms;
- marginal notes;
- conflicting revisions;
- worker objections;
- redacted/corrected plans;
- physical evidence;
- sparse responsive residue only where already supported.

Avoid:

- Maelor hologram explaining each discovery;
- a single diary containing the whole conspiracy;
- long mandatory lore dumps blocking traversal;
- making all early Quiet Court reformers knowingly malicious.

---

# 9. Combat restraint and escalation boundary

The first half is primarily about comprehension and place ownership.

Therefore:

- arrival is not an enemy gauntlet;
- S1 uses environmental/physical danger as its primary pressure;
- S2 uses machine-state consequence as its primary pressure;
- S3 uses historical/Hush tension as its primary pressure;
- incidental hostile presence may exist only where it does not destroy the intended pacing/readability;
- no requirement exists to kill a roomful of enemies before operating the first-half machines unless later content gives a specific fiction/mechanical reason.

After the player crosses into / substantially uncovers Quiet Court, adversarial pressure may escalate.

That transition is intentional:

> **first half: understand what Null Meridian is**
>
> **second half: survive someone actively using that system against you**

This prepares S4/S5 and Maelor without making combat background noise from the entrance onward.

# 10. Save / retry / reload

Existing Save/World-State, Tool, Hush and Mirror authority remain controlling.

Decision #3 adds these sequence-specific requirements:

- S1 and S2 settled state survives ordinary save/load once semantically committed;
- solving one sibling sequence does not force replay when the other fails;
- maintenance-lift availability is derived from its authoritative prerequisites, not a separately trusted presentation boolean;
- worker-layer breathing/exploration state cannot invalidate prior sequence settlement;
- S3 temporary Mirror state follows Mirror persistence/reconstruction rules and may not become an arbitrary permanent duplicate world;
- if saving is permitted while the doorway is held, reload must converge to a valid existing Mirror/local-state contract, not a half-wall collision state;
- archive access fact/sequence settlement must not depend on a cutscene finishing;
- death/retry never sends the player back through already-settled S1/S2 merely to inflate finale difficulty;
- invalid half-puzzle representations are reconstructed from semantic/source state.

Exact save fixture implementation remains future UE5.8 evidence work.

---

# 11. Accessibility / readability

The first half must preserve Decision #2's simplicity under accessibility settings.

Required:

- structural overstrain in S1 is not audio-only, color-only, or haptic-only;
- usable/unusable airflow is readable through multimodal world cues;
- S2 machine state is not communicated solely by colored gauges;
- worker operating marks have scalable/readable UI support where inspected text is necessary;
- Palinode disagreement in S3 has non-audio readable representation;
- Mirror-compatible state and commitment are not dependent on color discrimination;
- timing accommodation does not invalidate semantic success;
- optional diagnostic assistance may become stronger under reviewed accessibility settings without becoming an objective/solution radar;
- camera remains under locked camera authority and does not force cinematic angles during precision traversal.

Accessibility never invalidates story completion.

# 14. Supersession / clarification of Decision #1 sketches

Decision #1 remains authority for the six-sequence dependency architecture.

Decision #2 remains authority for the one governing dungeon idea and simplicity discipline.

This Decision #3 **replaces the earlier broad S1/S2 sketches where they imply a systems-heavy final exam**.

Specifically:

- S1 is not a hidden structural-phase calculation puzzle; it is a simple problem of harmful forced synchronized movement whose correction exposes a familiar Vane route;
- S2 is not a newly learned coupled pressure/thermal topology model; it is a simple problem of unlike machines being forced toward one standardized operating condition;
- S3 remains Palinode + Mirror synthesis but is now concretely tied to a worker doorway erased/corrected from official history and serves as the physical access into Quiet Court;
- first-half combat is intentionally restrained until after the archive threshold.

Later decisions may refine exact blockout dimensions, content placement, encounter composition and tuning, but may not reintroduce a hidden finale-only systems model without explicit owner review.

## Final locked statement

> **Null Meridian's first half is an adventure progression—monument, machine, workplace, buried history—not three abstract mastery rooms. The Hanging Walk shows forced synchronized movement physically failing; the Equal House shows unlike machines failing under one standardized operating state; settling both restores ordinary worker-maintenance access. The descent exposes the human labor architecture beneath Meridian's sacred shell. The Door That Was There uses Palinode and Mirror to let the player physically traverse a historically supported worker doorway that official history corrected away, leading directly into the Quiet Court archive. Combat remains restrained until after that reveal so the second half can escalate from understanding the place to surviving its deliberate use against Neris.**
