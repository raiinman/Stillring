# 1. Purpose

Board 02 exists to prove visually that **Stillring's regions are different places before color, texture, weather, VFX, signage or UI are allowed to rescue them**.

It is not a general environment mood board.

It is not a competition to redesign the regions.

`docs/154`, `docs/160`, `docs/161` and `docs/162` already define the regional identities and their Waking/Hush/state relationships. Board 02 converts those written rules into one controlled visual comparison package.

The board must answer production questions such as:

- can Brindle, Cairnspire, Rootmere, Saltreach, Emberstep, High Aerie, Mireglass and Cairnfall be identified in grayscale from massing alone?
- does each region have a dominant directional rhythm rather than merely a different texture set?
- does the skyline tell the same story as the settlement's labor, transport, weather and civic machinery?
- is the major landmark visible as a navigational organizer rather than background decoration?
- can the player infer the broad route language from terrain and architecture without a GPS overlay?
- does ordinary life visibly fit the geography?
- does the Hush preserve the place strongly enough that wrong relationships remain meaningful?
- do Stillness and Unringing alter organization/state rather than replace the region with a generic ruined version?
- do Hush Cairnspire, Still-Cairn and Null Meridian remain recognizably descended from the world already learned?

The governing visual test is:

> **If all color and fine surface detail disappear, the player should still know where they are.**

# 5. Global presentation grammar

Every ordinary-region panel must use the same comparison grammar so apparent differentiation is not created by presentation tricks.

## 5.1 Required panel stack

For each region show:

1. **Spatial sentence** — one short line naming the region's dominant spatial idea;
2. **solid silhouette / skyline strip** — no texture, interior linework or color;
3. **three-value massing frame** — foreground / identity midground / atmospheric background;
4. **directional-line diagram** — dominant vertical, horizontal, diagonal, arc/circle or layered-band behavior;
5. **negative-space diagram** — gaps, water, sky, drops, channels, courtyards or horizon bands that define the region;
6. **landmark + settlement relation** — landmark must organize the place rather than float behind it;
7. **route-language thumbnail** — broad route rhythm only, not final gameplay route authorization;
8. **civic/labor rhythm thumbnail** — repeated machinery/architecture forms tied to ordinary work;
9. **Waking/Hush correspondence pair** — same recognizable place before wrongness is introduced;
10. **day / night / weather note** — written callout derived from `docs/161`, not a full lighting key.

## 5.2 Camera consistency

Cross-region comparison frames should use comparable field-of-view and framing logic wherever practical.

Do not make one region look more monumental merely by using a heroic ultra-wide camera while another is shown from eye level.

Where the geography genuinely requires a different viewing position, label why.

## 5.3 Scale figures

Neutral human silhouettes may be included for scale.

They are reference figures only and may not redesign Neris, C3 characters, population costumes or final runtime scale.

If a named character is used, the figure must follow `docs/158`/`docs/160` and be clearly subordinate to the environment question.

## 5.4 Grayscale first

The primary Board 02 proof is grayscale/value-based.

Small restrained regional color tags from `docs/161` may be shown in labels, but the board fails if color is required to tell regions apart.

# 6. Global recognition reduction tests

Every region must be shown through the following reductions.

## 6.1 Solid silhouette test

Reduce environment + landmark to one solid dark mass against a light field.

Question:

> Does the skyline/massing still belong to only one Stillring region?

## 6.2 Three-value test

Use only three values.

Question:

> Does the settlement/landmark/route hierarchy survive without texture?

## 6.3 Thumbnail test

Reduce the frame until only major forms remain.

Question:

> Can an informed reviewer identify the region without labels?

## 6.4 Landmark removal test

Temporarily remove the hero landmark.

Question:

> Does the region still possess a regional shape language, or was one hero asset doing all the work?

A region whose identity disappears when its dungeon/landmark is removed needs stronger ordinary-world massing.

## 6.5 Color-removal test

Desaturate any concept frame.

Question:

> Do architecture, terrain, route rhythm and negative space remain distinct?

## 6.6 Hush recognition test

Show the Hush version before its label.

Question:

> Does the reviewer recognize the Waking place first and the wrong relationship second?

Unless the content is intentionally H5, “generic strange Hush place” is failure.

# 7. Source-acquisition protocol for Board 02

All source collection follows `docs/155`.

A source is useful only when the team records:

- **Reference** — artist/work/photo/location/source identifier;
- **Question** — the production problem being studied;
- **Extracted principle** — generic transferable visual logic;
- **Stillring translation** — how the principle is expressed using Stillring's own geography, labor, architecture and gameplay needs;
- **Forbidden carryover** — recognizable expression that may not be copied.

No reference image becomes design authority by inclusion alone.

## 7.1 Existing abstract reference anchors

The following already-approved extracted principles from `docs/155` may guide source selection:

- Erik Taberman study — simplified large environment masses, distant readability, route-led composition, restrained background noise;
- sandara study — small inhabited settlement nested within enormous natural forms; fantasy through scale rather than ornament;
- Jeremy Fenske study — one dominant geographic feature organizing settlement memory and daily life;
- Tyler Edlin study — architecture/ruins implying traversal through bridges, arches, gaps, elevation and water while monumental forms remain readable;
- grounded real-world environment research — construction logic, weather response, drainage, service access, storage and evidence of occupation.

These are **principle references, not style targets**.

## 7.2 Protected-game reference rule

Commercial games may be studied for abstract questions such as:

- landmark navigation;
- region differentiation;
- shape hierarchy;
- route composition;
- density control.

Do not use a commercial-game location as a direct target or copy:

- skyline;
- landmark silhouette;
- settlement layout;
- dungeon facade;
- bridge arrangement;
- iconography;
- architecture motifs;
- palette;
- named prop shapes.

“Make this look like [game location]” is not an acceptable production instruction.

# 8. Brindle / First Circuit — shape source brief

## 8.1 Locked spatial sentence

**A sloped hill settlement where terraced agriculture, flood channels, workshop-homes and a bell-road service network interlock at human scale.**

## 8.2 Shape fingerprint

Dominant line behavior:

- stepped horizontals from agricultural terraces;
- gentle road diagonals climbing/descending the shelf;
- narrow verticals from chimes, relay hardware, orchard trees and modest structures;
- channel cuts interrupting otherwise readable human-scale bands.

Massing:

- low-to-medium building clusters;
- workshop and domestic masses intentionally overlap;
- no monumental skyline wall;
- repaired add-ons and service structures create useful asymmetry.

Negative space:

- field rows;
- road gaps;
- flood channels;
- orchard openings;
- relay square / social clearings.

Landmark relationship:

- Vale Workshop / Relay Square / bell-road infrastructure should feel embedded in ordinary work rather than detached as quest architecture.

Route language:

- road and service path first;
- terraces and water controls explain why paths bend;
- crossings are practical bridges/viaduct/service structures rather than fantasy parkour decoration.

## 8.3 Required source baskets

Collect reference principles from:

- terraced hill agriculture;
- orchard settlements;
- mill races and flood channels;
- workshop-homes and farm outbuildings;
- old road-service inns;
- practical timber/stone viaducts;
- rural relay/post/service squares;
- hand-maintained irrigation and warning systems.

Do not copy a picturesque European village composition and rename it Brindle.

## 8.4 Hush correspondence proof

Use one familiar Brindle frame where the massing remains intact.

Wrongness should come from authored relationship changes such as:

- altered/missing repair evidence;
- too-perfect work arrangement;
- occupancy/light mismatch;
- repeated/reset local states.

Do not create a haunted-village silhouette or generic destroyed opening area.

## 8.5 Time/weather note

From `docs/161`:

- ordinary day is warm, open and readable;
- night is sparse intimate human light, not theme-park path lighting;
- rain makes drainage/flood systems visually relevant;
- weather changes values/material response without changing the regional shape language.

# 9. Cairnspire — shape source brief

## 9.1 Locked spatial sentence

**A vertical civic-service city of stacked terraces, freight bridges, pressure lifts, signal boards and public machinery whose grandeur comes from coordinated infrastructure rather than monarchy.**

## 9.2 Shape fingerprint

Dominant line behavior:

- strong stacked horizontals at terrace levels;
- decisive vertical lift shafts/towers/service risers;
- bridging diagonals and cross-city freight spans;
- repeated civic module rhythms interrupted by older masonry/service layers.

Massing:

- tall layered city wall/terrace relationships;
- monumental fronts backed by narrow practical service depth;
- dense midground with controlled long vertical sightlines.

Negative space:

- vertical drops between terraces;
- bridge gaps;
- lift voids;
- civic squares/counter halls;
- atmospheric separation among districts.

Landmark relationship:

- Meridian tower/Waybell and major civic structures organize sightlines but must not convert the city into palace imagery.

Route language:

- stairs, lifts, freight bridges, service galleries and alleys form visible circulation layers;
- public-facing route and worker/service route should both be readable as real city systems.

## 9.3 Required source baskets

Collect principles from:

- steep terraced cities;
- civic infrastructure layered onto older masonry;
- freight-first bridges;
- historical/industrial lifts and funicular service architecture;
- public works offices;
- archives and municipal service corridors;
- worker districts attached to monumental civic institutions;
- visible maintenance access and utility systems.

Reject royal-capital/palace reference as the dominant source language.

## 9.4 Hush correspondence proof

Preserve the vertical civic skyline.

Wrongness should emerge from:

- precise local systems disagreeing with neighboring systems;
- repeated counters/barriers;
- missing service circulation;
- selected terraces aligning too perfectly or breaking expected spatial relation.

Do not build a second dark-fantasy city.

## 9.5 Time/weather note

From `docs/161`:

- day requires vertical depth separation and readable stacked districts;
- night reads as a distributed civic network rather than raw light density;
- rain exposes gutters/runoff/service logic;
- haze separates terraces without erasing landmarks.

# 10. Rootmere — shape source brief

## 10.1 Locked spatial sentence

**A wet floodplain settlement nested among colossal root arcs and vertical trunks, with raised lived platforms, ferries and engineered living spans threaded through the canopy.**

## 10.2 Shape fingerprint

Dominant line behavior:

- huge root arcs;
- tall trunk verticals;
- raised platform horizontals;
- low water-channel horizontals;
- flexible bridge curves connecting otherwise separated mounds.

Massing:

- environment forms larger than buildings;
- architecture nests into/around living structures rather than competing with them;
- inhabited warm pockets sit inside a darker enclosing canopy mass.

Negative space:

- water channels;
- spaces under root arches;
- canopy openings;
- ferry gaps;
- raised platform separation from floodplain.

Landmark relationship:

- bell-oak / Root Cathedral must organize the region as living civic infrastructure, not sacred fantasy-tree spectacle detached from work.

Route language:

- raised paths, root bridges, ferries and mound transitions;
- water and living load explain where routes can exist.

## 10.3 Required source baskets

Collect principles from:

- buttress-root forests;
- floodplain/wetland raised settlements;
- trained/grafted living wood;
- ferry landings;
- root/wood bridge interfaces;
- flood halls and elevated service platforms;
- orchard/wetland irrigation;
- plant-fiber/resin structural repair.

Avoid tree-city fantasy architecture copied from another artwork.

## 10.4 Hush correspondence proof

Recognition must remain root/water/platform first.

Allowed wrongness includes:

- incompatible growth states;
- selective motion suspension;
- repair appearing before damage;
- water continuing while flexible systems stop.

## 10.5 Time/weather note

From `docs/161`:

- day uses layered canopy depth and inhabited clearings;
- night uses sparse ferry/cooking/fungal-light pockets, never an emissive forest;
- rain is ordinary and should emphasize runoff, leaf weight and channel behavior;
- low fog belongs to channels/low ground rather than one even volume.

# 16. Late spaces — Hush Cairnspire / Still-Cairn / Null Meridian

These spaces require a separate sheet because their visual power depends on **recognition plus transformed relationship**, not the invention of a ninth unrelated region.

## 16.1 Hush Cairnspire

Locked base identity:

- Cairnspire stone/blue-gray/brass ancestry;
- civic terraces;
- familiar service architecture;
- recognizable route/infrastructure fragments.

Shape transformation may use, where authority supports it:

- familiar civic modules in impossible correspondence;
- repeated service geometry;
- unrealized/rejected plan spaces that remain recognizably Cairnspire;
- missing occupancy around locally precise systems;
- altered parallax/relationship rather than random impossible geometry.

Board requirement:

Show Waking Cairnspire silhouette beside Hush Cairnspire and identify **which large forms remain the recognition anchors**.

## 16.2 Still-Cairn

Locked visual idea:

**preservation becoming imprisonment**.

Shape language should use familiar, repaired or emotionally recognizable spaces held too complete or too stable.

Avoid:

- fortress-of-evil silhouette;
- black crystal castle;
- generic frozen-time ruins;
- unrelated monumental fantasy architecture.

Board requirement:

Show at least one familiar-space comparison where the disturbing read comes from over-preservation / absence of expected change rather than a new kit.

## 16.3 Null Meridian

Locked visual idea:

**forced singular order versus living plurality**.

Shape/material ancestry must synthesize known Orra systems:

- civic stone;
- precision metal;
- brass/bronze resonance hardware;
- service access;
- maintenance geometry;
- old repairs;
- regional interventions where finale authority brings them together.

Shape direction:

- severe order;
- legible service/mechanical structure;
- controlled repetition;
- recognizable Meridian engineering exposed rather than hidden by abstract spectacle.

Avoid:

- black void + neon magic;
- cosmic cathedral;
- random Escher geometry;
- finale-only architecture unrelated to the game's civic/material history.

Board requirement:

Show a **recognition ancestry diagram** tracing at least four familiar ordinary-world form families into Null Meridian's late composition without implying literal asset reuse where canon does not require it.

# 17. Waking / Hush shape-comparison rules

Board 02 must not accidentally become a Hush redesign exercise.

For each representative pair:

1. preserve regional recognition first;
