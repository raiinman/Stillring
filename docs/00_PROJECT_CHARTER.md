# 00 — Project Charter

## Mission

Build an original 3D fantasy action-adventure that recaptures the **clarity, mystery, physicality, and authored progression** of the late-1990s console adventure era without copying protected characters, stories, maps, assets, code, music, UI, or branding.

## Scope target

- Single-player.
- PC first.
- Controller-first controls.
- 20–30 hour first playthrough target.
- 8–10 major dungeon-scale spaces including the finale.
- One interconnected overworld with regional hubs rather than a modern open-world checklist.
- No live service.
- No procedural story.
- No loot rarity treadmill.
- No crafting tree unless a later prototype proves it strengthens the core game.
- No multiplayer during base-game production.

## Player promise

The player should repeatedly feel:

- “I wonder what is over there.”
- “I understand why I lost that fight.”
- “I just learned a new way to read this world.”
- “That dungeon changed the place I came from.”
- “I remember that character.”
- “The ending paid off things introduced near the beginning.”

## Creative pillars

### 1. Deliberate adventure

Travel has shape and memory. Roads, gates, rivers, towers, weather, and landmarks create orientation. Fast travel is earned and limited enough that the world still matters.

### 2. Tools are verbs

Major rewards are not +8% damage. They add actions: pull, anchor, tune, redirect, reveal, launch, silence, resonate.

### 3. Dungeons are authored machines

A dungeon has a central idea, teaches it safely, complicates it, combines it with old knowledge, then tests mastery in the boss.

### 4. Combat is readable

Few buttons, strong animation commitment, clean tells, good audio, target lock, meaningful defense. Enemies are puzzles under pressure.

### 5. Story lives in the world

Narrative is delivered through people, consequences, architecture, rituals, damaged infrastructure, and changed spaces—not only cutscenes.

## Hard constraints

- Original IP only.
- No ripped or traced commercial assets.
- No ROM/decompilation dependency.
- No “10% different” reasoning.
- Stable engine versions only for production.
- Vertical slice before full production.
- Each production milestone must have explicit exit criteria.

## Working title policy

**Project Stillring** is an internal codename. A commercial title requires a proper trademark/name-clearance pass before public release.
