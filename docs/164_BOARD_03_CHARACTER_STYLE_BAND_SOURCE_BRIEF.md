# 1. Purpose

Board 03 exists to **prove visually that the locked Stillring character designs work together**.

It is not a mood board and it is not a new character-design round.

`docs/158_CHARACTER_PRODUCTION_MANIFEST.md` already locks the principal cast's creative identity. Therefore Board 03 must answer production questions such as:

- do the eight principal human silhouettes separate clearly at gameplay distance?
- does Neris read as a seventeen-year-old female apprentice bellwright rather than a generic fantasy heroine?
- do face proportions remain grounded and stylized without becoming actor-scan photoreal or anime-coded?
- do hair masses remain readable without strand-dependent identity?
- do hands and tool relationships survive gameplay framing?
- do costume materials and repairs communicate occupation before archetype?
- does age/build diversity remain visible when color and face detail are removed?
- do character palettes remain distinct without becoming MMO class colors?
- can Waking/Hush/state variants preserve identity without a universal effect pass?
- does the Listener remain legible as many incomplete possibilities rather than a humanoid god/monster?

The final board is an **execution-verification artifact subordinate to written authority**.

If a board execution conflicts with `docs/158`, the board is wrong.

Purpose:

- prove age/life-stage diversity;
- prove face-shape separation;
- prove stylization consistency;

Key separation anchors:

- Tessa — long oval/diamond, high cheekbones, broad straight nose, wide expressive mouth;

- Sena — broad square, strong straight nose, heavy brow, full mouth;
- Rusk — square broad jaw, level brows, restrained middle-aged lines.

## 6.2 Stylization band

- **TOO REALISTIC** — actor-scan/pore-first/photographic noise; reject;

The examples may use a generic noncanonical study head or Neris only if clearly labeled that the first/third are noncanonical failure examples.

## 6.5 Material hierarchy panel

Show representative swatches and garment examples for:

- medium woven work cloth;
- heavy weathered courier cloth;
- field-coat woven material;

For each C1/C2 principal, show base and one meaningful changed state side by side.

Required pairings:

- Tessa — courier → relief-network/route-responsibility state;
- Ilyra — Hush survival → returned/Waking repair state;

- Sena — kiln captain → distributed-production state;
- Rusk — administrator → post-Stillness/local-access state.

Changes must be practical and traceable, not “Act II costume upgrades.”

## 7.2 Hush recognition test

Show Neris and Ilyra in Waking and Hush-related presentation.

Requirements:

- identity remains readable;
- no universal blue/purple recolor;
- no ghost transparency;
- no corruption veins;
- no particle aura;
- Hush differences arise from relationship/material/motion logic consistent with `docs/162`.

For Neris, the burnt-amber repair thread remains an unusually stable recognition cue.

For Ilyra, repeated near-identical repairs and inconsistent wear may communicate wrong time without making her spectral.

## 7.3 Named versus population differentiation

Show a production comparison with:

- one C0/C1 principal;
- one C2 major recurring character;
- one C3 named regional character treatment;
- several C4 population archetypes.

The purpose is to establish **authorship density**, not social rank.

Expected gradient:

- C0/C1 — unique head, body proportion, costume construction, signature equipment, strong facial burden;
- C2 — unique named identity with possible technical reuse;
- C3 — named head/occupation silhouette/region costume/state continuity;
- C4 — bounded modular population system.

Do not make background population visually inferior through absurdly low detail. Difference is production uniqueness and performance burden, not whether they look like humans.

## 7.4 Population variety floor diagram

Illustrate the minimum visible-variety target from `docs/158` without claiming final counts:

- 5 adult body silhouettes;
- 2 youth silhouette families where supported;
- 2 elder silhouette families;
- 10 recognizable head bases before hair/age variation;
- 8 hair/headwear families;
- 4 occupation outerwear families;
- 3 footwear families;
- 3 climate/protection layers where required.

This panel is a **system diagram**, not a promise that every combination will ship.

## 7.5 Listener manifestation sequence

Show at least four stages of one canonical Listener presentation:

1. distant unresolved volume;
2. fragment overlap becoming briefly person-like;
3. one human-scale communicative gesture assembling;
4. gesture dissolving back into incomplete possibilities.

The volume should occupy roughly 2.3–2.6 m reference height but must not resolve into a giant humanoid.

Show:

- 12–20 overlapping incomplete human-scale fragments;
- partial shoulders, hands, profiles, backs of heads and gestures;
- slices of familiar environment surface passing through the volume;
- central negative-space corridor;
- no stable face;
- no eyes;
- no mouth;
- no coherent torso/limbs;
- partial non-converging shadows;
- optional reflection variant using a related but different fragment set.

Prohibited visual solutions must be shown in a small **DO NOT** strip:

- giant humanoid god;
- masked deity;
- cosmic person;
- shadow demon;
- tentacle creature;
- angel;
- giant eye;
- face in sky;
- random particle blob;
- generic glitch monster.

Do not use another commercial character/entity image as the “do not” example. Use simple diagrammatic failure silhouettes.

# 11. Reference/provenance method for Board 03

Every external or generated reference used during board execution must follow `docs/155`.

For each reference, record:

- source/artist/work identifier;
- production question;
- extracted abstract principle;
- Stillring translation;
- forbidden carryover.

Safe reference questions include:

- how does heavy woven cloth fold around a working elbow?
- how does a courier satchel actually distribute load?
- what footwear construction supports ladders and wet stone?
- how do heat-safe foundry garments protect without becoming armor?
- how do coarse-wave bob shapes read in motion?
- how do middle-aged and older faces preserve expressive readability under simplified surface treatment?
- how do repaired garments reveal use through construction rather than dirt?

Unsafe instructions include:

- “make Neris look like [commercial heroine]”;
- “use [actor]'s face”;
- “give Tessa this exact game costume”;
- “make Maelor like this villain”;
- tracing/copying a specific illustration's face, clothing layout, silhouette, prop arrangement or material-expression choices.

`docs/05_IP_GUARDRAILS.md` remains binding.
