## Mandatory-chain fairness rule

Story-mandatory chains teach world persistence and causal memory. They must not hide a major irreversible binary future inside what presents as routine critical-path compliance.

Mandatory chains may still have meaningful downstream consequences, but large elective dilemmas belong primarily in the story-optional portfolio unless later owner authority explicitly changes a particular chain.

## Canonical twelve questions

The portfolio is protected by the distinct question each chain asks:

1. **Crooked Fire Bell** — When is an imperfection part of how something works?
2. **Graymile Bypass** — What happens when the unofficial route is safer than the certified one?
3. **Common Measure** — Can coordination exist without a master reference?
4. **Green Span** — Should a living structure be preserved by immobilizing it or supported while remaining alive?
5. **Wren's Margin** — Can unofficial marginal knowledge outlive the archive that ignored it?
6. **Manual Fog Code** — When geography changes, whose calibration is actually correct?
7. **Levi's Pressure Release** — Who should control infrastructure when local needs and central efficiency disagree?
8. **Maker Marks** — Is provenance sentimental, or can knowing who made something be technically important?
9. **Quench Bypass** — Is redundant emergency infrastructure waste, or resilience?
10. **Distributed Copy** — Is disputed knowledge safer when held by one authority or distributed among ordinary people who can challenge it?
11. **Personal Provenance** — Can personal ownership/provenance preserve meaning that anonymous state cataloguing destroys?
12. **Uncorrected Lens** — Can a known error be more truthful than corrected data?

These questions are design-review tools, not player-facing quest slogans.

## Portfolio progression

The portfolio intentionally matures with the story.

Early post-Grand-Ring payoffs are weighted toward **physical and operational recognizability** so the player quickly learns that the changed world remembers prior work.

Later payoffs increasingly emphasize **social, historical, and epistemic consequence**:

- who is allowed to know;
- which record survives;
- who may act without authority;
- how provenance changes interpretation;
- whether a known error is more truthful than correction.

The progression therefore moves from questions like **"what happened to the machine?"** toward **"who remembered what happened, and what counts as true?"**

This is intentional portfolio escalation, not a requirement that every individual chain become more abstract over time.

## Setup-presentation variation

The twelve chains must not all present as explicit binary choice prompts.

The current portfolio deliberately includes:

- **explicit repair dilemmas:** S01, S02, S04, S07, S09;
- **practice adopted through play:** S03, S06;
- **preserve/document/provenance actions:** S05, S08, S11, S12;
- **trust/distribution decision:** S10.

The game should not constantly announce which actions are "important choices." Later recognition is part of the system's intended pleasure, subject to Decision #3's legibility requirement.

## Reconvergence portfolio policy

Different chains may diverge for different durations.

Some consequences may last for a single rescue/problem; others may shape a region, an NPC relationship, or credits-state material.

Reconvergence is permitted to control production scope, but shared later state may not falsify how that state was reached.

A reconverged save must retain the historically relevant setup variant, payoff resolution, involved NPC/knowledge facts, testimony, completion settlement, and other source-owned facts required by downstream content.

## No portfolio-wide consequence-size quota

The portfolio must contain variation in scale:

- large physical consequences;
- medium regional/operational consequences;
- intimate human consequences;
- epistemic/knowledge consequences;
- cross-system consequences.

No individual chain is required to satisfy every class.

A quiet, load-bearing epistemic payoff may be stronger than another collapsed bridge or new shortcut.

## Portfolio Similarity Audit

The shared Quest / Objective / World-State System IDE must include a portfolio authoring/audit view that compares chains on at least:

- setup action/form;
- conceptual question;
- commit boundary class;
- primary payoff manifestation;
- authoritative source systems involved;
- player activity during payoff;
- approximate narrative/world distance between setup and payoff;
- emotional/production scale;
- story-mandatory versus story-optional status;
- reconvergence behavior.

Human review is required when two chains substantially share:

**the same setup action + the same payoff type + substantially the same thematic question.**

Similarity is not automatically invalid; deliberate thematic rhyme is allowed. Unexamined repetition is not.

## Canonical vertical-slice chain

`setup.brindle.crooked_fire_bell` (S01) is the flagship Issue #9 vertical-slice Setup/Payoff chain.

It is selected because it can prove, in a naturally noticeable sequence:

1. a place/player relationship exists before catastrophe;
2. tactile setup interaction;
3. valid alternate configurations;
4. explicit semantic commit;
5. meaningful separation through Grand Ring;
6. changed-world return;
7. visibly different payoff state;
8. survivor/social recognition;
9. both supported variants remain completion-valid;
10. save/load/retry determinism;
11. causal explanation through the System IDE.

This selection satisfies the design intent of Issue #9's requirement for a vertical-slice setup/payoff testers can notice naturally once implementation exists.

## Red-team rejection conditions

Reject portfolio/content implementation if:

- all twelve chains reduce to "preserve weird local thing, see weird thing work later";
- a story-mandatory chain hides a major elective binary future behind routine critical-path compliance without explicit authority;
- S07 remains merely another moving-pocket/refuge payoff rather than the revised local-versus-central operational-control consequence;
- S10 is implemented as only "a backup copy survived" rather than distributed agency/knowledge;
- two chains substantially duplicate setup action, payoff type, and thematic question without deliberate review;
- every chain is forced to the same consequence scale;
- every setup is presented as an explicit binary dialogue decision;
- reconvergence erases historical truth;
- the portfolio loses major-region coverage;
- the System IDE cannot compare chains for thematic/experiential redundancy.
